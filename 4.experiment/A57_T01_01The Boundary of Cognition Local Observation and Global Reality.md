### Section 1 Thought-Experiment: Friction-Free Sphere in Gravitational Field

This is an idealized thought-experiment.

The system resides within a constant uniform gravitational field under ultra-high vacuum conditions. A rigid fixed channel with artificially configurable geometry holds a homogeneous solid metallic sphere.

Ideal constraints: Zero friction, no energy dissipation, no heat exchange, and no additional external perturbation.

Initial operation: Lift the sphere to a given height on one side of the channel and release it from rest.

When the channel adopts a simple smooth circular-arc profile, the observable phenomenon is straightforward: the sphere reciprocates perpetually with fixed amplitude and fixed period.

Nevertheless, the channel geometry is not restricted to simple circular arcs. It may take elliptical shapes, segmented slope assemblies, or highly complex configurations featuring undulations, depressions, bifurcations, inflection points of varying elevation. In theory, the geometric complexity of the channel can rise without bound.

Given unchanged gravitational field and unchanged sphere, merely modifying channel geometry fundamentally alters the subsequent trajectory of the sphere.

1. Simple channel: Trajectories are highly regular. From a short segment of observation, one may reliably reconstruct past motion and predict future paths.

2. Highly complex channel: The sphere still obeys identical physical rules, yet its trajectory becomes intricate. Observers can only capture positional and velocity information over a limited interval. Based solely upon such local measurements, it proves difficult to reconstruct the full geometry of the channel, let alone predict complete future states across all positions along the path.

Core Speculation: The complete set of possible motions for the sphere is governed by the full objective reality constituted by channel geometry, gravitational field and sphere mass. Human observers only ever obtain partial temporal snapshots of the sphere¡¯s runtime behaviour.

What we perceive constitutes manifest behaviour within a limited domain, not full access to all objective possibilities inherent to the system.

### Section 2 Analogy Extending from Physical Experiment to Real-World Cognition

Project this thought-experiment onto the real universe:

We observe the Solar System and witness Earth orbiting the Sun stably. This resembles the regular reciprocation of a sphere inside a simple circular-arc channel. What we record is merely an extremely short fragment within timescales of cosmic evolution. The entirety of human civilisation amounts to but an instant on cosmic timescales.

Observation of stable orbital operation does not imply that this mode represents the complete truth of the universe. Within larger-scale objective reality, higher-order gravitational configurations and perturbation potentials persist. Limited temporal and spatial observational scope of humanity cannot exhaust the full evolutionary possibility space of the system.

This constitutes the generalized "blind-men-and-elephant" dilemma: complete objective reality exists, yet human observational windows are finite in time and space. We frequently encounter partial fragments and tend to equate locally observed phenomena with the full objective reality.

### Section 3 Three Core Propositions of Cognition

**Proposition 1 [Local-Fragment Global-Nesting Proposition]**
Any objective system accessible to human observation and modelling generally represents a partial fragment embedded within a larger global system. Systems can be encapsulated inside higher-order, larger-scale super-systems.

For instance, the Sun-Earth orbital system is nested inside the Milky Way and galaxy clusters. We only observe its behaviour across a finite epoch. We cannot rule out perturbations originating from the super-system which may modify or even break existing orbital relations. All human knowledge of nature captures only partial glimpses of "the elephant".

**Proposition 2 [Configurational Ontological Primacy Proposition]**
Configuration denotes structure. For both physical objective configurations and logical conceptual configurations, structure is more fundamental than material carriers or manifest dynamic processes.

Material carriers may decay, manifest processes may terminate. Yet configurational structure may lie dormant. When coupling conditions re-emerge, phenomena corresponding to that structure can manifest again. Material entities represent transient manifestations of underlying structure. Observable processes correspond to partial state traversals among the full possibility set defined by structure.

**Proposition 3 [Mental Configurational-Simulation Proposition]**
Objective systems exhibit diverse and distinct configurations. The human mind can construct ordered conceptual configurations internally as mental models to map and depict external objective systems. Mentally simulated configurations are not identical to objective reality itself. It is via such simulation that humans comprehend, deduce and forecast the external world.
