For thousands of years of human civilization, status, merit, honor and material assets have been deeply intertwined. This stems not from flaws in institutional design, but from a fundamental constraint of the times: pervasive material scarcity.

In an era of insufficient production, limited capacity and scarce survival resources, spiritual symbols could not exist independently. They had to be anchored to land, taxation and tangible material interests. Ancient noble titles, official ranks and hierarchical systems were inevitable outcomes under conditions of scarcity. Honor was directly equivalent to the right to distribute material goods, and eventually solidified into an exclusive, hereditary, zero-sum ¡°dead identity-driven economy¡±. For millennia, crowds of people were trapped in fierce involution within a single material-ranking track.

With the completion of modern industrialization and the saturation of basic material supplies, material scarcity gradually recedes, bringing a historic turning-point for civilization. Material needs have hit physical ceilings and can no longer carry humanity¡¯s infinite spiritual pursuits. The core leap of future civilization lies in the complete separation and parallel development of the material-economy track and the spiritual-economy track, fundamentally resolving the predicament of society-wide malignant zero-sum involution.

## I. The Material Track: Securing Survival, the Old Dilemma of Stock-Based Competition
The material economy is bounded by inherent physical limits. Once survival needs such as food, clothing, shelter and production supplies are met, marginal returns diminish rapidly. In the old age of material scarcity, social competition centered on seizing physical resources and wealth shares, with material benefits serving as the stakes of zero-sum games.

If one person acquires more material wealth, others necessarily lose access to corresponding survival resources. This form of material zero-sum competition breeds wealth polarization, class antagonism and vicious involution, acting as a hidden hazard to civilizational stability. The mission of the material track is to guarantee universal basic subsistence. It shall no longer serve as the sole yardstick for measuring human worth.

## II. The Spiritual Track: Independent Growth, a New Symbiotic Order of Plural Symbols
As material scarcity fades, humanity¡¯s higher-order needs shift toward identity recognition, professional merit, the dignity of craftsmanship, and contributions to civilization. The spiritual economy can then take shape ¡ª a ¡°living symbolic-economy¡±, distinct from the rigid identity-driven economy of the West.

Instead of establishing one unified society-wide ranking table, we shall establish independent value tracks for all trades and professions: artisans earn symbols for craft merit; physicians earn symbols for benevolent healing; scholars earn symbols for intellectual achievements; creators earn symbols for cultural dissemination. Farmers, craftsmen and researchers each possess their own exclusive socially-recognized symbolic credentials.

The spiritual realm has no physical upper bound. One person¡¯s spiritual creation does not occupy another person¡¯s space for value realization. The overall ¡°cake¡± of spiritual civilization can keep expanding, fundamentally weakening the vicious competition where the whole society crowds into one single pyramid.

Drawing on the proprietary-logic of ancient Chinese ennoblement institutions yet remodelled for modern times: spiritual contributions may be confirmed as personal symbolic assets with full ownership rights. Their linkage to material benefits shall be limited and capped; permanent material privileges shall never be attached. Symbolic honors may pass between generations under rules of generational attenuation, reverting to civilian status after several generations. Hereditary class solidification is thereby thoroughly prevented.

## III. Positive Redirection of Zero-Sum Games: Civilized Channeling of Competitive Instinct
Humans are innately endowed with instincts for competition, ambition and comparison. Zero-sum games themselves are not inherently harmful. They carry humanity¡¯s drive for progress and the urge to excel. Completely erasing competitive desires would deprive society of its endogenous driving force for development. Future civilization does not abolish zero-sum games; it transforms their underlying logic.

The fatal flaw of traditional zero-sum competition lies in its stakes: material wealth and tangible interests. Competition merely transfers existing stock resources. Winners seize practical benefits from losers, easily giving rise to greed, conflict and social dissipation. Gambling, click-driven rivalry and secular fame-chasing all fall into this category.

In future civilization, competitive forms of zero-sum games are preserved in full, yet their stakes are completely replaced: symbolic status within communities, professional merit certifications, and honor for civilizational contributions.

Take Olympic-style gold-medal contests as an analogy: they are zero-sum in nature, yet competition revolves around honor, records and professional symbolic recognition rather than robbing opponents of their wealth. Ranked contests across professions, intellectual duels and skill competitions all follow zero-sum competitive rules. Victory or defeat only raises or lowers symbolic rank and public merit prestige, without massive transfers of material wealth. Even with winners and losers, competition incentivizes individuals to refine their expertise, polish capabilities and generate value, without producing predatory social outcomes.

Human ambition for upward striving is redirected away from struggles over material gains toward benign competition for spiritual-symbolic distinction. Zero-sum dynamics cease being forces that undermine social equilibrium, and instead become positive drivers for professional refinement, innovation and individual self-improvement. Competitive instincts serve the advancement of civilization rather than zero-sum mutual consumption.

> Material goods are finite; hence we uphold boundaries to secure subsistence.
> Spirit is boundless; hence we open new paths to define human purpose.
> Competition must be moderated; hence we guide human aspiration toward goodness.

By separating the two tracks we dissolve the age-old malady of involution. By channelling zero-sum impulses through symbolic honor-systems we accommodate humanity¡¯s innate drive for achievement. This constitutes the core order for the sustainable development of future civilization.
