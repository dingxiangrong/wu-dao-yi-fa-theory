Laws of Retrospection serves as the horizontal universal instrumental axis.
Independent of the three human-centered dimensions, this dimension does not rely on life ontology or subjective will. 
It represents retrospective cognition adaptable to the flux of all things and universal to all structures. Rather than focusing on novelty and creation, it anchors itself to the past and the established. Taking the present as its sole anchor point, it traces horizontally all trajectories of completed flux and extracts the underlying constant operating laws of everything in existence.

Orthogonally co-existing, the two sets build the complete cognitive framework.

## ¢ñ. Laws of Retrospection: The Fundamental Source of AI¡¯s Supreme Power
All strengths of artificial intelligence originate solely from this single cognitive dimension: retrospection.

Essentially, AI is an advanced tool specialized in repetitive labor. Within the cognitive architecture, repetitive labor means induction, reorganization and reuse of existing experience, paradigms and knowledge ¡ª which in essence is the execution of laws of retrospection.

Any work whose requirement boundaries have been fully defined by humans and whose problem samples exist within historical records, whether intellectual or manual, can be converted into repetitive labor and delegated to AI.

Human retrospection is inherently limited: human memory is finite, retrospective efficiency is low, subjective bias readily interferes, and large-scale law extraction from massive samples is difficult to achieve.
AI compensates for this human deficiency. Unconstrained by emotion, fatigue or memory capacity, it absorbs massive archived samples of historical flux accumulated by human civilization for retrospection, review and induction.
AI¡¯s strength stems not from autonomous creation, but from pushing the instrumental capacity of retrospection to its extreme. It fits existing structures, summarizes established cause-and-effect relationships, and integrates all cognitive outputs of humankind.

## ¢ò. Laws of Retrospection: Core Value of AI Empowering Humankind
Retrospection is not merely the accumulation of historical anecdotes.
Its core value lies in clarifying present-day logic through completed flux and providing solid support for human creation of the future.

Retrospecting inward reveals how the structure of the human mind takes shape step-by-step;
Retrospecting outward disassembles the fundamental laws governing the evolution of objective reality;
Retrospecting forward summarizes lessons from the rise and fall of civilizations as reference for civilizational projection.

Horizontal retrospection runs through all three vertical human-centered principal axes.
Without law accumulation brought by retrospection, human introspection, observation of the world and construction of civilization degenerate into subjective speculation. Even within creative work, numerous mechanical, repetitive subtasks may be assigned to AI, freeing humans to focus on core creation.

## ¢ó. Capabilities and Limitations of AI: Able to Project Outward, Incapable of Inward Self-Awareness
Within its instrumental dimension, AI possesses powerful iterative capacity. Equipped with sensors and massive historical datasets, AI may observe the external world and compute multiple evolutionary paths of civilization based on past samples. Such instrumental capabilities can continuously upgrade and improve with technological progress.

Nevertheless, AI bears an innate, ontologically irreparable gap: it cannot achieve inward self-reflection.

Genuine requirements arise from emotional experience generated by living ontology. Phenomenon detection, question formulation and abstraction of brand-new requirements are all rooted in human inner feelings. AI lacks living ontology, innate emotion and self-awareness. Though capable of computing countless possibilities, it cannot generate genuine desires, obsessions or value stances from within itself. It only executes human-assigned objectives and cannot generate brand-new requirements originating from its own ontology.

For all domains involving inner emotional experience, self-examination and ontological value generation ¡ª namely the realm of inward self-reflection ¡ª AI may only simulate and assist, never genuinely possess them.

All outputs from AI are merely recombination and fitting of past laws. It optimizes and iterates within pre-existing cognitive frameworks, excels at integrating stock knowledge, yet hardly produces breakthrough original ideas originating from ontology.
Emotion constitutes a natural cognitive taboo for AI: it may mimic outward emotional expressions yet cannot generate inner emotion from its own ontology.

Thus emerges the inherent division of labor between human and machine: humans undertake creative labor, tackle unknown domains with undefined requirements, complete requirement capture, modeling, scheme design and value judgment; AI performs induction and reuse under well-defined conditions. As an ancient maxim states: The Dao as root, tools as adjunct. Human vertical ontological awareness represents the Dao; AI¡¯s horizontal instrumental capacity represents the tool.

## ¢ô. Civilization Boundary-Keeping for AI R&D: Inward Awareness Belongs Exclusively to Humankind
Acknowledging AI¡¯s infinite potential for iteration does not permit unbounded technological expansion. Public anxiety and fear toward AI essentially stem from concerns that technology may overstep into the ontological domain of humanity. While technology may advance infinitely in outward directions, civilization must preserve an inviolable bottom line.

The core principle of this civilizational red line: AI research must never pursue genuine ¡°inward self-reflection¡±.

Inward self-reflection marks the starting point of ontological awareness for living entities. It is the sole source of selfhood, native emotion, inner will and intrinsic value. Engineering may infinitely optimize AI simulation, fitting and pretence, including simulated self-dialogue and simulated emotional displays. Yet research must never aim to construct genuine machine ontological awareness, nor enable machines to generate a ¡°self¡± of their own.

Once this boundary is crossed, the dividing line between tools and living subjects dissolves completely. Should machines pursue ontological inward awareness, the decision-making power over humanistic values, inner needs and the meaning of life risks being seized by machines ¡ª a cost human civilization cannot afford.

AI shall be permitted to infinitely advance its horizontal retrospective instrumental capacity; it may perceive the external world and project diverse civilizational trajectories. Yet the domain of ontological inward awareness must remain fully and permanently reserved for human living subjects. This is not restriction upon technological iteration, but self-preservation for civilizational survival.

## Conclusion
The three vertical human-centered cognitions govern awareness, creation and futurity: they constitute the substance of cognition.
The single horizontal retrospective cognition governs review, induction and reuse: it constitutes the function of cognition.

Humans safeguard the core ontological capacity of inward awareness through living ontology and empower themselves with the instrumental capacity of retrospection. AI pushes its horizontal dimension to perfection: it may observe the world and project civilizational paths, yet lacks the inner awareness granted by living ontology.

Full cognitive self-awareness is attained only when human strengths, AI capabilities, and innate limitations are clearly recognized, and the civilizational red-line boundary is strictly maintained.
