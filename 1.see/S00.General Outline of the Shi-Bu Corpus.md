## Preface
The Shi-Bu (See-S) Series is a cognitive-speculative system grounded in human-subject ontology.

All genuine insights into human cognition, the observation of all things in the world, and civilizational reflection originate from "Seeing".

Shi-Bu establishes the Four-Perspective Framework as its complete four-dimensional foundational architecture: Looking Inward at the Self, Looking Outward at the World, Looking Forward at Civilization, and Looking Backward at Laws. Together they form a closed 3+1 four-dimensional cognitive loop, acting as the cognitive gateway, observational foundation, and source of insight for the whole system.

*Looking Inward at the Self*, *Looking Outward at the World*, and *Looking Forward at Civilization* are the three vertical human-subject principal axes. Rooted in life-form ontology, self-awareness and autonomous volition, they stand for initiative cognition exclusive to living beings, centering on awareness, creation and future-orientation.

*Looking Backward at Laws* is the single horizontal cognitive principal axis, an innate mental capacity of human beings. Its core lies in retrospection and review of what has already occurred, extracting constant underlying mechanisms from the evolution of past events. Artificial intelligence can serve as a real-world observational sample; relevant analyses and civilizational boundary-setting axioms are elaborated in full in Chapter S99.

The three vertical human-subject principal axes plus the one horizontal cognitive principal axis form a 3+1 orthogonal structure, which constitutes the fundamental ground for all inferences within Shi-Bu.

All papers of the Shi-Bu corpus are expanded discussions under the Four-Perspective Framework: dissecting mental boundaries, training cognitive capabilities, deducing physical-world logic, reviewing rise-and-fall of civilizations, and conceiving future orders.

This general outline provides no detailed argumentation. It only defines ontological concepts, distinguishes boundaries, clarifies the division of roles among the four perspectives, and documents the chapter layout of the S-series. It serves as the meta-anchor for all S-series papers.

## Chapter 2 Interrelation of the Four Perspectives (Orthogonal Mechanism)
1. Looking Backward at Laws is one of humanity¡¯s four cognitive dimensions, tasked with review and induction, yet it is not the source of cognition itself. It cannot independently generate brand-new original demands or values; it serves the other three vertical principal axes.
Looking Inward at the Self relies on retrospection to trace how human mentality forms;
Looking Outward at the World summarizes objective mechanisms from historical observational samples;
Looking Forward at Civilization deduces civilizational futures from records of rise-and-fall.

Without mechanisms distilled by Looking Backward, introspection, world observation and civilizational construction are prone to subjective fantasy. Nevertheless, relying solely on Looking Backward can only process existing paradigms and cannot bring forth breakthrough creation originating from life-form ontology.

2. The three vertical human-subject principal axes form the ontology and origin of the whole system. Looking Inward at the Self is the starting point of all cognition. Only after humans understand their own perception, desires and cognitive limits can they examine the outer world and further deduce the future of human civilization.
Logical chain: Looking Inward ¡ú Looking Outward ¡ú Looking Forward; Looking Backward at Laws runs through the full process as the horizontal cognitive dimension.

3. Each of the four perspectives has inherent boundaries and shall not be abused beyond scope.
Looking Inward at the Self: Subjective feelings cannot replace objective facts.
Looking Outward at the World: Physical explanations shall not eliminate human spirit, emotion and value; reductionism of pure science shall be rejected.
Looking Forward at Civilization: Deduction of futures is not prediction of inevitable outcomes; it is mechanistic speculation instead of empirical proof.
Looking Backward at Laws: Mechanisms induced from past samples cannot be unconditionally applied to entirely new scenarios.

**General Axiom of Shi-Bu**: All human observation, perception and induction are partial, approximate and finite. No cognition can exhaust absolute truth. The purpose of the Four-Perspective Framework is not to secure final answers, but to continuously iterate and refine human cognitive models.
