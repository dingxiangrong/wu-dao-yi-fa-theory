## Abstract
This document is derived from observations and summaries of objective human-cognition phenomena. It contains no theoretical hypotheses, causal speculations or model constructions. It faithfully records observable, verifiable and repeatable cognitive phenomena and features, serving as the factual foundation for subsequent theoretical construction.

## 1. Inherent Core Traits of the Cognitive System
1. The human cognitive system is inherently goal-oriented. Such orientation originates first from instinctive needs shared by humans and animals, acting as the underlying driving force of cognition.

2. Human cognitive goals fall into two major tiers: firstly, instinct-driven goals common to all animals; secondly, uniquely-human higher-order goals generated by society, culture and ideological systems, centered on value judgements including judgements of right-wrong, meaning, and rationality of methods. Acquired concepts, knowledge and rules all serve and integrate into this higher-order goal system.

3. An individual's set of goals is finite as a whole, yet individuals differ greatly in quantity, type and value orientation of goals. This constitutes a core source of huge gaps between people.

4. Existing goal structures can expand outward and undergo partial refinement-iteration. For instance, the internal goal of threat may be defined as: the state of an entity may cause harm to oneself. Entities are highly diverse, their states and structural forms keep changing dynamically. Therefore during cognition, humans keep expanding, refining and iterating the threat-related goal-structure to cover more complex scenarios and forms.

5. Cognition supports partial-matching; full-whole matching is not required, analogous to partial-recognition mechanisms of QR-codes.

6. Humans persistently ingest receive massive external information.

7. Cognition possesses an autonomous filtering mechanism, automatically discarding irrelevant information and retaining only valid information related to one's own goals.

8. Information effectively matched to goals directly triggers corresponding goal-oriented responses.

9. Effectively-matched information progressively reinforce and refines pre-existing goal-structures.

10. Newly-acquired knowledge initially exists as weak origin-concepts with no practical value for the individual. Only after practical personal verification and alignment with internal cognitive goals will the corresponding cognitive structure consolidate and form valid cognition.

11. Human cognition features both fuzziness and precision: precision guarantees stable and reliable judgements; fuzziness creates room for cognitive expansion, trial-and-error and adjustment.

12. Human cognition contains innate curiosity, a special internal need independent of survival, safety and subsistence. Curiosity drives humans to actively explore the unknown, try new approaches, and engage with information with no direct survival value. It pushes cognitive structures to expand, reorganize and iterate, and is the fundamental driving-force behind human innovation, creation and discovery.

[Note] Cognitive restructuring and structural expansion driven by curiosity correspond to phenomena traditionally labelled as intuition, insight, inspiration and creation.

## II. Core Classification and Formal Definitions of Information
Based on one single core criterion  -  whether the receiving subject is capable of decoding intrinsic semantics  -  information is divided into three categories with clear boundaries, judgement logic and scenario-affiliation, forming a self-consistent definitional system.

### (1) Definition of High-Order Information
High-order information denotes information whose intrinsic meaning can be fully decoded and accurately understood by the receiving subject, carrying explicit human semantics, logical connotations and intentional orientation.

Information qualifies as valid high-order information for a subject only when its content, logic, emotion and purpose are truly comprehended. Even if information carries complete semantics for others, it does not count as high-order information for a subject unable to decode it.

### (2) Definition of Low-Order Information
Low-order information denotes information where the subject only perceives outer appearances and raw sensory signals, without decoding intrinsic semantics, logic or real-world meaning.

It presents only directly-perceivable outer features such as sound waveforms, visual imagery, gestures, tones and physical appearances, with no directly-interpretable ideological content. Even if the source carries human semantics, it remains pure low-order information for a subject incapable of interpreting its content.

### (3) Instrument-Observed Information (Transitional-State Information)
Instrument-observed datasets, sensor signals and device outputs do not strictly belong to high-order or low-order information; they occupy an intermediate transitional state. Their classification shifts depending on pre-processing depth:

1. Raw bare signals, unprocessed waveforms, underlying sensor readings: zero pre-processing and no semantic orientation, lean toward low-order information.
2. Instrument-encapsulated outputs with embedded analytical conclusions, human-readable texts and judgements: carry interpretable semantics, lean toward high-order information.
3. Conventional experimental datasets, charts and standardized numerical values: require human interpretation, translation and logical assignment for comprehension; typical intermediate-transition state.

For system consistency and processing simplicity, this convention applies: all instrument-observed information is provisionally categorized as low-order information while retaining its transitional-state nature.

### (4) General Boundary-Judgement Canon
- Comprehend inner meaning and fully decode semantics  -> High-order information
- Perceive only appearances and sensory signals, unable to interpret intrinsic meaning  -> Low-order information
- Instrument outputs requiring secondary human interpretation, non-bare raw signals  -> Intermediate-transition state; provisionally categorized as low-order information within this system.

### (5) Practical Scenario Illustration
Take two French-speaking people engaged in lively conversation:

1. For the two participants: they understand semantic content and emotion from each other; they receive high-order information.
2. For an onlooker who does not understand French: semantic content cannot be decoded; only sounds, facial expressions and gestures are perceived; all incoming information is low-order information.

Corollary: Whether the dialogue consists of casual chat, banter, argument or emotional confrontation, as long as the onlooker cannot interpret language and intent and merely observes outer behaviour, the information remains low-order information for that onlooker.

## III. Two Core Operational Processes of Human Cognition
All human cognitive activities can be clearly split into two independent, complementary modules: learning process and reasoning process. They possess distinct functional boundaries and operating logics and jointly constitute a complete cognitive closed-loop; both are objectively observable phenomena.

- Learning process: focuses on information acquisition, storage, internalization and cognitive-structure construction. It belongs to the "input-deposition" phase and primarily processes high-order information.
- Reasoning process: focuses on information retrieval, judgement, response and decision-making output. It belongs to the "invocation-application" phase and handles both low-order and high-order information.

### (1) Learning Process (Processing Pipeline Exclusive to High-Order Information)
The learning-process operates on structured knowledge and content comprehensible to the subject. It is essentially a systematic cognitive-construction procedure. Four modes progress sequentially from passive-to-active and shallow-to-deep, with clear boundaries and coherent logic.

#### Type-1: Rote Learning (Passive-Shallow Storage)
1. Core behaviour: store texts, materials and symbols via repetition and mechanical memorization, without logical comprehension, cognitive deduction or meaning reconstruction.
2. Essential nature: only physical storage of memorized materials. It does not directly build cognitive logic or upgrade cognitive structures. Functionally comparable to consulting books or retrieving knowledge-base entries.
3. Implicit side-effect: this mode does not directly improve content-comprehension capacity. Nevertheless long-term repetition shapes linguistic intuition, sentence rhythm, textual structure and patterns of verbal organisation, indirectly influencing expressive forms and modes of thinking. It constitutes implicit training for underlying output frameworks.
4. Analogy: training-and-generation mechanisms of modern large-language models correspond to human rote-learning. They fit expressive patterns and reproduce sentence structures from massive corpora, without native autonomous semantic-understanding or cognitive-construction capacity.

#### Type-2: Imposed-Passive Learning (Passive Structured Reception)
1. Core behaviour: obtain systematic concepts, pre-established conclusions and standardized frameworks via external instruction and one-way transmission. It constitutes the main way to build foundational cognitive bases.
2. Operating logic: pre-formed cognitive structures are received without active deduction or verification. Initial reinforcement is weak; stability of cognitive frameworks positively correlates with repetition frequency. Repeated reinforcement drives progressive consolidation? of cognitive frameworks.
3. Core limitation: passively-formed cognitive structures exhibit weak extensibility and adaptability. They can hardly migrate autonomously, diverge-innovate or flexibly adapt, and can only be invoked within pre-set frameworks without self-driven upgrades.
4. Deep-seated impact: imposed-learning imposes strong constraints on individual goal-setting, behavioural choices and value-judgement systems. Long-term exposure to one-way external instruction and pre-defined standards narrows goal-space, solidifies value orientations and weakens autonomous value-judgement capabilities.

#### Type-3: Active Learning (Active Cognitive Upgrade, Dual-Path Closed-Loop)
Active learning represents the highest-order cognitive-processing mode. Its core lies in autonomously invoking information, closing logical loops and iteratively reconstructing cognitive structures. It is the sole path toward essential improvement of cognitive capability, containing two complementary sub-paths:

1. Logical-Deductive Active Learning: draw on stored information together with external materials for autonomous analysis, reasoning, argumentation and reflection. This includes thought-experiments: constructing hypothetical mental scenarios for deduction without real-world physical operations, continuously optimizing cognitive structures and raising cognitive levels.
2. Practical-Experiential Active Learning: obtain first-hand feedback through hands-on real-world practice, using real-world outcomes to verify, revise and calibrate cognitive frameworks and compensate limitations of logical deduction.

The two paths are highly complementary: logical deduction (including thought-experiments) extends knowledge and builds frameworks; practical-experience performs real-world validation and revision. Together they accomplish autonomous cognitive construction.

#### Type-4: Strong-Stimulus Experiential Acquisition (Once-Bitten-Twice-Shy Pattern)
A special passive-learning mode. Strong single or repeated real-world negative stimuli passively solidify cognition and behavioural rules without deliberate study or external instruction. Extreme feedback imprints patterns firmly into the cognitive system. When similar scenarios reappear, avoidance-behaviour triggers directly.

### (2) Reasoning Process (Unified Processing Pipeline for Low-Order and High-Order Information)
The reasoning-process is the information-application-and-response stage of cognition. Built upon established cognitive structures, it filters, matches and judges incoming low-/high-order information and outputs decisions. It is divided into low-order-information processing and high-order-information processing.

#### Type-1: Low-Order-Information Processing (Massive-Sensory-Signal Filtering & Structuring Mechanism)
Processes raw sensory signals of sight, hearing, touch etc., plus instrument-derived transitional-state information provisionally classified here. It runs automatically in the background without conscious volition.

1. Information feature: humans receive massive volumes of low-order information. The brain cannot process all data; over 99.9-% of incoming information is filtered out at early-stage processing. Only a small subset of high-value items proceed to further processing.
2. Core mechanism: cyclic structural computation of "division-and-integration". Information enters as holistic input and forms temporary structures instantly. Goal-irrelevant structures are discarded. Relevant information enters repeated "split-merge" cycles until stable invocable structures emerge.
3. Priority rules:
    - Instinct-level objective functions run permanently with highest priority; associated information gets forced capture and priority processing.
    - Higher-order objective functions can be loaded, unloaded or suppressed. Information related to long-term-focused domains gets rapid identification and processing.

#### Type-2: High-Order-Information Processing (Semantic-Information Inference & Response)
Targets comprehensible high-order information carrying complete semantics. Core operations include meaning-decoding, value-assessment, risk-evaluation and behavioural decision-making. The whole procedure builds on pre-existing cognitive-structures and goal-systems and is perceivable and intervenable by conscious awareness.

Typical scenario: receiving semantic messages such as "earthquake occurs", "stock market crashes", "war is imminent". The subject rapidly interprets meaning, judges against personal cognition and triggers avoidance-or-safety-seeking responses.

## IV. Four Fundamental States of Human Cognition
During operation the human cognitive system exhibits four well-defined, observable and distinguishable fundamental states with distinct functional roles.

### (1) Real-Time Reasoning State
State of instant cognitive-structure invocation for judgement, response and ad-hoc adaptation when facing ongoing scenarios, sudden information or practical problems.

Core features: online processing, instant decision-making and goal-matching; real-time cognitive-response mechanism.

### (2) Mental-Clearing State
A preparatory state clearing channels for advanced-cognition operation.

Consciously clear acquired knowledge points, fixed heuristics and distracting thoughts, allowing innate "observation-function" and "goal-matching function" to operate unobstructed.

Common in relaxed conditions: rest, walking, scenery-watching, day-dreaming and other low-deliberation activities. Within the mental-clearing state, unexpected discoveries, inspirations and insights often emerge. It constitutes a critical state for intuition and creative breakthroughs.

Mental clearing is not a low-level activity; it is an advanced mental preparatory state for higher-order cognition, intuition and insight.

### (3) Active-Thinking State
Deep original thinking governed by higher-order cognitive-functions. Core workflow:

Observation  -> match internal goals  -> generate internal decisions  -> form abstract representations.

This process grasps essences, produces qualitative judgements, yields insights and fosters comprehension. It does not rely on fixed steps or formulaic heuristics. It represents uniquely-human high-order cognitive capacity difficult for AI to replicate.

### (4) Low-Level Cognitive State
Standardized deductive procedures following pre-defined rules, steps, formulae and logical-chains, including:

- Mathematical reasoning
- Logical calculus
- Problem-solving heuristics
- Step-by-step computation

Traits: trainable, standardizable, replicable and offloadable. Executable by computers and AI. Belongs to basic execution-layer functions within the cognitive system.
