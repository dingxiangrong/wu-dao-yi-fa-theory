## Abstract
Traditional enterprise profit analysis relies on the ex-post accounting system of ¡°Revenue-Cost¡±, which can only review historical operating outcomes. Lacking causal transmission and predictive capability, it cannot achieve forward market situational awareness. To address evaluation challenges where cross-border corporate profits are doubly disturbed by exchange rates and overseas commodity prices, this paper constructs the Y504 Microeconomic Enterprise Profitability Forecasting Model under the modeling philosophy of ¡°discard outcomes, retain causes¡±, built upon the dual underlying logics of **value derivation** and **price pass-through**.

The model establishes a ¡°Three-Slow-Two-Fast¡± variable analytical framework. Industry-specific premium, firm-specific premium, exchange-rate sensitivity coefficient, and USD-denominated underlying-commodity-price sensitivity coefficient are defined as endogenous slow variables that depict long-run business genetics and risk endowments of enterprises. The USD-denominated price of the underlying commodity and the USD-CNY exchange rate are defined as exogenous fast variables representing short-term external market shocks. Based on these two categories of variables, core profit formulas incorporating an exchange-rate disturbance function and a commodity-price disturbance function are constructed. This avoids logical flaws of traditional linear models such as profit distortion caused by zero-exposure conditions, and enables precise separation between an enterprise¡¯s steady-state value base and external volatility disturbances.

Following short- and long-cycle analytical logic, scale decomposition is realized. In the short run, endogenous slow variables are treated as constants, and total-differential formulas are deployed to deduce marginal profit changes triggered by exogenous-variable fluctuations. In the long run, exogenous fast variables take their steady-state mean values, with focus shifted to the iterative evolution of core endogenous corporate capabilities. Meanwhile, a standardized enterprise-profiling system is established via the two-dimensional registration rule of **static curve classification plus dynamic trend classification**, defining criteria for parameter-grade judgment to standardize characterization of industry dividends, individual competitiveness, exchange-rate exposure, and commodity-price sensitivity.

This model fully clarifies differentiated transmission mechanisms through which exchange rates and USD-denominated underlying-commodity prices affect cross-border firms¡¯ profits, and specifies model applicability boundaries and directions for further research. Breaking the limitation of ex-post review compared with conventional financial-analysis tools, this model centers on causal chains. It can be widely applied in situational perception of cross-border-enterprise profitability, risk decomposition, scenario simulation, and operational-decision optimization, providing a rigorous and reusable theoretical-analysis framework for micro-level corporate profit forecasting.

## Chapter 1. Causality-Driven Situational Awareness
Traditional profit accounting adopts the formula ¡°Revenue - Cost = Profit¡±. Essentially an ex-post financial-statistical tool, it aggregates realized revenue and cost data after all transactions are completed to compute profit-and-loss results and review historical performance, yet it is unsuitable for market forecasting.

The core of market forecasting lies in situational awareness. Situational awareness emphasizes capturing faint early-stage signals at the front-end of a system. Minor disturbances propagate and amplify along causal chains and eventually evolve into substantial outcomes. By the time final outcomes such as revenue decline or net-loss emerge, risks have already materialized and decision-making room shrinks substantially.

Situational awareness is grounded in cognitive causal networks. No business fluctuation or industry shift arises out of nowhere. Recognition of faint signals and anticipation of transmission paths depend on the completeness of the agent¡¯s causal network. Conventional profit accounting only reviews realized outcomes; without causal-transmission links, it possesses no predictive situational-awareness capacity.

## Chapter 2. The Essence of Business Operation: Value Derivation + Price Pass-Through
Enterprise operation contains two core underlying mechanisms: value derivation and price pass-through.

Value derivation describes the process whereby an enterprise transforms upstream inputs including materials, technologies and services and generates incremental value. Inputs themselves carry only externally-given exchange value. After corporate production, organization, technological iteration and brand empowerment, outputs acquire value increments exceeding original inputs. Value derivation defines the potential boundary for enterprises to capture excess returns and constitutes the intrinsic foundation of core operational capabilities. Without value derivation, price pass-through cannot occur.

Price pass-through represents an enterprise¡¯s capacity to transmit external price and exchange-rate shocks to downstream customers. Combined value-derivation capability and price-pass-through capability jointly form the value premium $\Pi$. The value premium is further decomposed: $\boldsymbol{\Pi=\Pi_0+\Pi_1}$. Here, $\Pi_0$ denotes the industry-specific premium determined by inherent industry conditions such as industry structure and entry barriers; $\Pi_1$ denotes the firm-specific premium stemming from unique corporate strengths including technology, branding, cost control and product differentiation.

$\Pi$ is an inherent firm attribute, classified as a slow variable that does not shift rapidly within short time horizons and forms a core component of enterprise profiling. Beyond the value premium, firms possess two further sensitivity attributes toward external shocks: sensitivity to exchange-rate fluctuations and sensitivity to fluctuations in the **USD-denominated price of the underlying commodity**, both also endogenous slow variables.

## Chapter 3. Model Design: Discard Outcomes, Retain Causes
Traditional financial analysis and market research habitually analyze using two sets of ex-post variables: revenue and cost. These variables are mutually entangled with severe endogeneity, capable only of explaining realized results rather than forecasting trends. Adopting the modeling philosophy of ¡°discard outcomes, retain causes¡±, this paper completely eliminates ex-post outcome variables such as revenue and retains pre-determinant core variables driving corporate profitability.

The model comprises five time-dependent functions divided into slow variables and fast variables:
- **Slow Variables (Endogenous Enterprise Profile)**: $\Pi(t)$, $\gamma(t)$, $\beta(t)$
- **Fast Variables (Exogenous Market Variables)**: $P_{\text{usd}}(t)$, $E(t)$

> **Unified Nomenclature Specification**:
>
> $P_{\text{usd}}(t)$: **USD-denominated price of the underlying commodity** (not simply ¡°USD price¡±; USD is the pricing currency; the commodity itself is the fluctuating object).
>
> $\beta(t)$: **Sensitivity coefficient for the USD-denominated price of the underlying commodity**, the elasticity of enterprise profit in response to changes in the USD-denominated price of the underlying commodity.

This chapter defines each variable before summarizing variables and scale-switching rules.

### 3.1 Slow Variable 1: Value Premium $\boldsymbol{\Pi(t)}$
$$\Pi(t)=\Pi_0(t)+\Pi_1(t)$$
- $\Pi_0(t)$: Industry-specific premium.
- $\Pi_1(t)$: Firm-specific premium.

$\Pi(t)$ represents the steady-state baseline profit level of an enterprise after removing disturbances from the USD-denominated underlying-commodity price and exchange rates. It synthesizes value-derivation and price-pass-through capabilities. As a slow variable, corporate operational genetics and industry structures rarely change rapidly in the short run and may be approximated as constants. In the long run, it evolves gradually alongside industry transformation and capability upgrades.

### 3.2 Slow Variable 2: Exchange-Rate Sensitivity Coefficient $\boldsymbol{\gamma(t)}$
$\gamma(t)$ is the exchange-rate sensitivity coefficient measuring profit exposure to exchange-rate movements, originating from currency structures of cross-border businesses, defined as:
$$\gamma(t)=c_{\text{buy}}(t)-c_{\text{sell}}(t)$$
- $c_{\text{buy}}(t)$: Proportion of total costs denominated in USD on the procurement side.
- $c_{\text{sell}}(t)$: Proportion of total revenue denominated in USD on the sales side.

$\gamma(t)$ carries signed values:
- $\gamma>0$: USD procurement exposure exceeds USD sales exposure.
- $\gamma<0$: USD sales exposure exceeds USD procurement exposure.
- $\gamma=0$: USD exposures on procurement and sales sides roughly offset each other.

$\gamma(t)$ is a slow variable. In the short run it is locked by supply chains, settlement currencies and customer structures and approximates a constant; over the long term it can shift through business restructuring and settlement adjustments.

### 3.3 Slow Variable 3: Sensitivity Coefficient for USD-Denominated Underlying-Commodity Price $\boldsymbol{\beta(t)}$
$\beta(t)$ denotes the **sensitivity coefficient for the USD-denominated price of the underlying commodity**, characterizing profit elasticity with respect to fluctuations in $P_{\text{usd}}(t)$.
- $\beta>0$: Rising USD-denominated underlying-commodity price generates positive upward pressure on enterprise profit.
- $\beta<0$: Rising USD-denominated underlying-commodity price suppresses enterprise profit.
- $\beta=0$: Enterprise profit is unaffected by fluctuations in the USD-denominated underlying-commodity price.

$\beta(t)$ is also a slow variable jointly determined by product mix, bargaining power and industry competition patterns. It remains relatively stable in the short run and evolves over longer horizons alongside shifts in competitive positioning.

### 3.4 Fast Variable 1: USD-Denominated Price of the Underlying Commodity $\boldsymbol{P_{\text{usd}}(t)}$
$P_{\text{usd}}(t)$ stands for the USD-denominated price of the relevant underlying commodity for an enterprise. It may represent overseas quotation for imported raw materials or international selling prices for exported goods. Determined by exogenous factors including global supply-demand balances, capacity cycles, overseas macroeconomic conditions and geopolitical conflicts, it lies largely outside corporate control.

> **Research Note**: The underlying pricing anchor for $P_{\text{usd}}(t)$ remains a proposition for further research. Further clarification is required regarding whether this nominal USD price anchors global physical supply-demand, USD-liquidity conditions or commodity benchmarks, alongside distinction between nominal USD prices and inflation-adjusted real USD prices. Empirical work is reserved for follow-up studies.

$P_{\text{usd}}(t)$ is a fast variable exhibiting high-frequency short-run fluctuations and constitutes a major source of exogenous profit disturbance.

### 3.5 Fast Variable 2: USD-CNY Exchange Rate $\boldsymbol{E(t)}$
$E(t)$ denotes the USD-CNY exchange rate, the quantity of Chinese Renminbi convertible per one US Dollar.

Sign convention:
- $E\uparrow$: Renminbi depreciates; US Dollar appreciates.
- $E\downarrow$: Renminbi appreciates; US Dollar depreciates.

Driven by interest-rate differentials, capital flows, macro expectations and balance-of-payments dynamics, $E(t)$ is an exogenous variable independent of the USD-denominated underlying-commodity price. All analyses in this paper adopt the Renminbi home-currency perspective without mixing USD-denominated viewpoints to avoid directional misjudgment.

> **Critical Perspective Reminder**:
>
> $E(t)$ represents the **USD-CNY exchange rate** (Renminbi per one US Dollar).
>
> $E\uparrow$ = Renminbi depreciation = US Dollar appreciation; $E\downarrow$ = Renminbi appreciation = US Dollar depreciation.
>
> This model calculates corporate profit entirely from the **Renminbi (home-currency) perspective**. Do not adopt USD-denominated viewpoints, or directional misinterpretations will occur.

### 3.6 Variable Summary and Scale-Switching Rules
|Variable|Symbol|Property|Short-Run Analysis|Long-Run Analysis|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|Value Premium|$\Pi(t)=\Pi_0+\Pi_1$|Slow Variable|Approximated as constant|Treated as variable|
|Exchange-Rate Sensitivity Coefficient|$\gamma(t)$|Slow Variable|Approximated as constant|Treated as variable|
|USD-Denominated Underlying-Commodity-Price Sensitivity Coefficient|$\beta(t)$|Slow Variable|Approximated as constant|Treated as variable|
|USD-Denominated Price of Underlying Commodity|$P_{\text{usd}}(t)$|Fast Variable|Treated as variable|May take long-run mean and approximate constant|
|USD-CNY Exchange Rate|$E(t)$|Fast Variable|Treated as variable|May take long-run mean and approximate constant|

Derived scale-analysis rules for the model:
- **Short-Run Analysis**: Three slow variables are approximated as constants. Focus lies on profit disturbances generated by the two exogenous fast variables.
- **Long-Run Analysis**: Fast variables take steady-state mean values and approximate constants. Research shifts toward evolution of the three endogenous slow variables $\Pi,\gamma,\beta$.

This classification is not an absolute attribute division but a reasonable approximation conditional upon the analytical time window.

## Chapter 4. Model Formulation
### 4.1 Profit Curve: Trend Model
Corporate profit is unsuitable for formulation using simple linear addition-subtraction or direct multiplication, which would produce logical defects such as profit becoming zero under conditions $\boldsymbol{\gamma=0}$ or $\boldsymbol{\beta=0}$.

Two disturbance functions are constructed:
- $f_E\big(E(t),\gamma(t),t\big)$: **Exchange-rate disturbance function**. Inputs: exchange rate $E$, exchange-rate sensitivity coefficient $\gamma$, time $t$. Constraint: when $\gamma=0$, $f_E=1$, and the exchange-rate disturbance channel closes.
- $f_P\big(P_{\text{usd}}(t),\beta(t),t\big)$: **USD-denominated underlying-commodity-price disturbance function**. Inputs: USD-denominated underlying-commodity price $P_{\text{usd}}$, USD-denominated underlying-commodity-price sensitivity coefficient $\beta$, time $t$. Constraint: when $\beta=0$, $f_P=1$, and the commodity-price disturbance channel closes.

**Core Profit Expression**:
$$
\boldsymbol{\text{Profit}(t)=\Pi(t)\cdot f_E\big(E(t),\gamma(t),t\big)\cdot f_P\big(P_{\text{usd}}(t),\beta(t),t\big)}
$$

**Economic Interpretation**: Enterprise profit equals baseline value premium multiplied sequentially by the exchange-rate disturbance function and the USD-denominated underlying-commodity-price disturbance function.
- When $\gamma=0,\beta=0$, then $f_E=1,\ f_P=1$, so $\text{Profit}(t)=\Pi(t)$. The enterprise is insulated from exchange-rate and commodity-price disturbances; profit is fully determined by its own value premium.
- Magnitude and sign of $\gamma$ govern transmission direction and intensity of exchange-rate shocks. Magnitude and sign of $\beta$ govern transmission direction and intensity of commodity-price shocks. Explicit functional forms for the two disturbance functions await further derivation and empirical calibration.

### 4.2 Expectation Model: Rate-of-Change Form
Under short-run analytical scenarios, $\Pi,\gamma,\beta$ may be approximated as constants. Total differentiation of the profit function yields the expression for marginal profit change:
$$
\boldsymbol{\frac{d\text{Profit}(t)}{dt}
\approx
\Pi \cdot f_P \cdot \frac{\partial f_E}{\partial E}\cdot \dot E(t)
+\Pi \cdot f_E \cdot \frac{\partial f_P}{\partial P_{\text{usd}}}\cdot \dot P_{\text{usd}}(t)}
$$

Where:
- $\dot P_{\text{usd}}(t)$: Time rate-of-change for the USD-denominated underlying-commodity price.
- $\dot E(t)$: Time rate-of-change for the exchange rate.

This formula represents the expectation form of the model. Given externally-observed movements in USD-denominated commodity prices and exchange rates together with endogenous firm parameters, marginal direction and magnitude of profit shifts may be inferred.

> **Remark**: Since explicit analytical forms for $f_E$ and $f_P$ are not yet specified, differentials remain in partial-derivative abstract form. Explicit expectation formulas can be substituted once disturbance-function forms are determined.

## Chapter 5. Economic Interpretation of Signs
### 5.1 $\gamma>0$: Procurement-Side Exposure Dominant (Import-Dependent Enterprises)
**Typical Examples**: Paper-making, airlines, refining segments of Sinopec. Enterprises purchase large volumes of USD-denominated raw materials while selling predominantly domestically.

**Interpretation**: Procurement-side USD exposure exceeds sales-side USD exposure. The exchange-rate disturbance function $f_E$ behaves as follows:
- $E\uparrow$, Renminbi depreciates: terms containing $P_{\text{usd}}\cdot E$ expand and profits are compressed.
- $E\downarrow$, Renminbi appreciates: terms containing $P_{\text{usd}}\cdot E$ shrink, home-currency costs of raw-material conversion decline and profits rise.

### 5.2 $\gamma<0$: Sales-Side Exposure Dominant (Export-Oriented Enterprises)
**Typical Examples**: Foreign-trade OEM manufacturers, crude-oil export business segments. Enterprises sell goods denominated in USD with low USD-denominated procurement costs.

**Interpretation**: Sales-side USD exposure exceeds procurement-side USD exposure. The exchange-rate disturbance function $f_E$ behaves as follows:
- $E\uparrow$, Renminbi depreciates: USD-denominated revenues convert into higher home-currency earnings, boosting profits.
- $E\downarrow$, Renminbi appreciates: USD-denominated revenues convert into lower home-currency earnings, weighing on profits.

### 5.3 $\gamma=0$: Natural Exchange-Rate Hedging State
**Interpretation**: Exchange rates no longer generate profit disturbances via currency exposure. Higher-order indirect effects from inventory cycles, contract mismatches and price-pass-through lags may persist; full insulation from all exchange-rate influences is not achieved.

> **Note**:
>
> $\gamma=0$ denotes only cancellation of first-order effects and does not mean zero risk. Inventory and contract-term mismatches may still generate higher-order disturbances.

> **Special Emphasis**:
>
> $P_{\text{usd}}(t)$ and $E(t)$ are two independent variables. Even amid Renminbi appreciation, concurrent sharp rises in the USD-denominated underlying-commodity price may completely erase appreciation-driven benefits. Conversely, dual favorable conditions emerge only when raw-material prices fall together with Renminbi appreciation. Reliance solely upon exchange-rate movements or solely upon commodity-price movements yields one-sided judgments.

### 5.4 Parameter $\boldsymbol{\beta}$: Differentiated Corporate Responses to USD-Denominated Underlying-Commodity Prices
Even for two firms with identical $\gamma$ (currency exposure), differing values of the USD-denominated underlying-commodity-price sensitivity coefficient $\beta$ produce divergent profit responses facing identical swings in the USD-denominated underlying-commodity price.
- Firms with $\beta>0$ receive profit tailwinds when the USD-denominated underlying-commodity price rises.
- Firms with $\beta<0$ experience profit compression when the USD-denominated underlying-commodity price rises.
- When $\beta=0$, fluctuations in the USD-denominated underlying-commodity price exert no direct impact on corporate profits.

### 5.5 Independence of $\boldsymbol{P_{\text{usd}}(t)}$ and $\boldsymbol{E(t)}$
The model maintains the setup that $P_{\text{usd}}(t)$ and $E(t)$ are mutually independent exogenous fast variables. Neither exchange rates nor commodity prices should be analyzed in isolation. For instance:
- Renminbi appreciation does not automatically benefit firms; concurrent sharp rises in USD-denominated commodity prices may fully offset such benefits.
- Renminbi depreciation does not automatically benefit exporters; concurrent sharp declines in USD-denominated commodity prices may erase depreciation-driven gains.

Analysis must examine combined shifts of both $P_{\text{usd}}(t)$ and $E(t)$.

## Chapter 6. Model Interpretation and Application
### 6.1 Valuation-Decomposition Logic
An enterprise¡¯s value base originates from its value premium $\Pi(t)$; risk discounts stem from profit volatility caused by exchange-rate and USD-denominated underlying-commodity-price disturbances.

$$
\text{Valuation}= \text{DCF}\big(\Pi(t)\big)- \text{RiskAdjust}\big(\,f_E(\cdot),\,f_P(\cdot)\,\big)
$$

- $\text{DCF}(\Pi)$: Deterministic value base corresponding to the value premium.
- $\text{RiskAdjust}(\cdot)$: Risk-adjustment discount generated jointly by the two categories of external disturbances. Its magnitude is jointly determined by $\gamma$, $\beta$ and joint-volatility characteristics of $P_{\text{usd}}$ and $E$.

> *Remark*: Explicit forms for disturbance functions remain unspecified, hence risk-adjustment terms retain abstract functional expressions.

### 6.2 Enterprise Profiling
Enterprise profiling serves as a human-oriented decision-classification tool and does not construct abstract multi-dimensional geometric spaces.

Each endogenous slow variable adopts a unified **two-dimensional registration format: [Curve Classification (current static level) | Trend Classification (derivative, dynamic evolutionary direction)]**.
Curve Classification: High / Medium / Low.
Trend Classification: Rising / Steady / Falling.

Each parameter is registered independently before manual comprehensive evaluation. Pre-packaged fixed asset labels are avoided to prevent real-world distortion caused by forced categorization.

|Parameter|Curve Classification |Trend Classification|
|---|---|---|
| $\boldsymbol{\Pi_0(t)}$|High / Medium / Low|Rising / Steady / Falling|
| $\boldsymbol{\Pi_1(t)}$|High / Medium / Low|Rising / Steady / Falling|
| $\boldsymbol{\gamma(t)}$|High Exposure / Moderate Exposure / Low Exposure|Rising / Steady / Falling|
|$\boldsymbol{\beta(t)}$|High Sensitivity / Moderate Sensitivity / Low Sensitivity|Rising / Steady / Falling|

#### Field and Classification Definitions
##### 1. $\boldsymbol{\Pi_0(t)}$ Industry-Specific Premium (Industry-Level Slow Variable)
> Meaning: Profit dividends generated by industry structure and entry barriers, independent of individual-firm behavior.
- **Curve Classification (Current State)**
    - High: Strong industry endowments and barriers; sufficient aggregate industry profit dividends.
    - Medium: Moderate industry endowments and profit dividends.
    - Low: Weak industry barriers; scarce aggregate industry profit dividends.
- **Trend Classification (Dynamic Evolutionary Direction)**
    - Rising: Industry structure improves and barriers strengthen; industry dividends expand.
    - Steady: No material short-run shifts in industry structure; dividends remain roughly unchanged.
    - Falling: Industry competition intensifies and barriers erode; industry dividends contract.

##### 2. $\boldsymbol{\Pi_1(t)}$ Firm-Specific Premium (Firm-Level Slow Variable)
> Meaning: Competitive capacity derived from internal corporate technology, branding, cost control and product differentiation.
- **Curve Classification (Current State)**
    - High: Strong core corporate competitiveness; substantial individual-firm profit safety buffer.
    - Medium: Moderate individual-firm competitive strength.
    - Low: Weak individual-firm competitiveness; insufficient internal safety buffer.
- **Trend Classification (Dynamic Evolutionary Direction)**
    - Rising: Sustained improvement in corporate technology, branding and bargaining power; enhanced individual competitiveness.
    - Steady: Individual-firm competitive position remains largely unchanged in the short run.
    - Falling: Deteriorating competitive standing; declining individual-firm competitiveness.

##### 3. $\boldsymbol{\gamma(t)}$ Exchange-Rate Sensitivity Coefficient
> Meaning: Characterizes cross-border currency exposure of enterprises; $\gamma>0$ denotes procurement-side exposure dominance; $\gamma<0$ denotes sales-side exposure dominance; $\gamma¡Ö0$ denotes first-order exposure offset.
- **Curve Classification (Current State)**
    - High Exposure: Large absolute exposure magnitude; exchange-rate swings generate material profit disturbances.
    - Moderate Exposure: Non-trivial currency exposure; exchange-rate movements produce noticeable profit disturbances.
    - Low Exposure: Near-offset exposures; first-order exchange-rate disturbance impacts are minor.
- **Trend Classification (Dynamic Evolutionary Direction, for exposure magnitude $|\gamma|$)**
    - Rising: Absolute exposure magnitude $|\gamma|$ expands; corporate exchange-rate risk rises.
    - Steady: Supply-chain, settlement and customer structures unchanged; exposure magnitude stable.
    - Falling: Absolute exposure magnitude $|\gamma|$ shrinks and converges toward hedged status; exchange-rate risk diminishes.

##### 4. $\boldsymbol{\beta(t)}$ USD-Denominated Underlying-Commodity-Price Sensitivity Coefficient
> Meaning: Characterizes profit responsiveness to swings in the USD-denominated underlying-commodity price; $\beta>0$ means rising commodity prices benefit profits; $\beta<0$ means rising commodity prices suppress profits; $\beta¡Ö0$ means profits are nearly insulated from commodity-price disturbances.
- **Curve Classification (Current State)**
    - High Sensitivity: Strong profit disturbances driven by underlying-commodity-price movements.
    - Moderate Sensitivity: Non-negligible profit disturbances from underlying-commodity-price movements.
    - Low Sensitivity: Weak profit disturbances driven by underlying-commodity-price movements.
- **Trend Classification (Dynamic Evolutionary Direction, for sensitivity magnitude $|\beta|$)**
    - Rising: Absolute sensitivity magnitude $|\beta|$ expands; commodity-price disturbance impacts intensify.
    - Steady: Stable product mix, long-term contracts and bargaining power; sensitivity magnitude unchanged.
    - Falling: Absolute sensitivity magnitude $|\beta|$ shrinks; commodity-price disturbance impacts weaken.

### 6.3 Hedging and Operational Decision-Making
The model distinguishes adjustable attributes among the three slow variables:
- $\Pi(t)$: Value premium, determined by industry endowments and core corporate capabilities, difficult to alter in the short run.
- $\gamma(t)$: Exchange-rate sensitivity coefficient, may converge toward zero via real-business adjustments (procurement, sales, settlement currencies) and financial instruments such as forward foreign-exchange contracts to mitigate exchange-rate impacts.
- $\beta(t)$: USD-denominated underlying-commodity-price sensitivity coefficient, may shift gradually through product upgrading, product differentiation, supply-chain restructuring and long-term price-locking contracts.

> **Risk-Management Implications**:
>
> Enterprises should not merely forecast directional movements of $P_{\text{usd}}(t)$ and $E(t)$. They should also optimize their own slow variables: on one hand elevate the value premium $\Pi$; on the other hand proactively adjust $\gamma$ and $\beta$ to reduce transmission intensity of external-market disturbances onto profits.

### 6.4 Model Applicability Boundaries
1. The model targets micro-level enterprises conducting cross-border procurement or cross-border sales. Fully closed-loop domestic-cycle enterprises lie outside the scope of this model.
2. $f_E$ and $f_P$ represent abstract disturbance functions; explicit analytical forms await calibration in follow-up research. This model prioritizes characterizing transmission logic rather than supplying ready-to-substitute explicit calculation formulas.
3. Higher-order factors including taxes, capacity-utilization rates, inventory cycles, contract lags and price-pass-through delays are not incorporated for the moment. These factors may be added in future extensions embedded within disturbance functions.
4. $\Pi,\gamma,\beta$ are slow variables approximated as constants in the short run, yet evolve alongside shifts in industry and corporate operating structures over longer horizons.
5. $P_{\text{usd}}(t)$ and $E(t)$ are specified as independent exogenous variables for now; potential correlation between them awaits empirical testing.

## Chapter 7. Model Summary
Continuing the modeling philosophy of ¡°discard outcomes, retain causes¡±, this paper proceeds from limitations of ex-post financial accounting and builds upon the dual underlying operational mechanisms of value derivation and price pass-through. It expands the prior dual-slow-dual-fast framework into this **Three-Slow-Two-Fast** micro-level enterprise-profitability model.

- Slow Variables (Endogenous Enterprise Profile): Value premium $\Pi(t)=\Pi_0(t)+\Pi_1(t)$, exchange-rate sensitivity coefficient $\gamma(t)$, USD-denominated underlying-commodity-price sensitivity coefficient $\beta(t)$.
- Fast Variables (External Market): USD-denominated price of the underlying commodity $P_{\text{usd}}(t)$, USD-CNY exchange rate $E(t)$.

Instead of simple addition-subtraction or direct-multiplication formulations, abstract disturbance functions are introduced: the exchange-rate disturbance function $f_E\big(E(t),\gamma(t),t\big)$ and the USD-denominated underlying-commodity-price disturbance function $f_P\big(P_{\text{usd}}(t),\beta(t),t\big)$.

**Core Profit Expression**:
$$
\text{Profit}(t)=\Pi(t)\cdot f_E\big(E(t),\gamma(t),t\big)\cdot f_P\big(P_{\text{usd}}(t),\beta(t),t\big)
$$

When $\gamma=0$, $f_E=1$ and the exchange-rate disturbance channel closes. When $\beta=0$, $f_P=1$ and the commodity-price disturbance channel closes. When both parameters equal zero, enterprise profit equals its baseline value premium. This logical construction eliminates the flaw of profit collapsing to zero under zero-exposure conditions found in older models.

From the short-run perspective, three slow variables approximate constants. The total-differential expectation formula for profit-rate-of-change enables observation of marginal profit shifts triggered by external-market shocks. From the long-run perspective, fast variables take steady-state mean values, and analysis focuses upon evolutionary paths of the three endogenous firm parameters $\Pi,\gamma,\beta$.

This paper completes construction of the logical framework. Explicit functional forms for disturbance functions and research into the pricing anchor for $P_{\text{usd}}(t)$ constitute key follow-up research propositions. This full framework supports enterprise profiling, impact scenario simulation and risk-source decomposition and supplies a new theoretical toolkit for profitability analysis of cross-border micro-enterprises.
