# A40-00. Flux-Construct Ontology: Social Dynamics V2.9

## Abstract

This paper constructs the axiomatic system of Flux-Construct Ontology: Social Dynamics. Taking the symbiotic relationship between Flux and Construct as the underlying ontological substrate, it realizes rigorous mapping onto human social systems based on the ontological rules of potentiality, force, stress and circulation. The system treats social-unit constructs as the basic unit for social analysis, and meta-constructs backed by the prerogative of violence as the top-level evolutionary framework of society. Social order emerges through interface mechanisms, and multi-level power fields form in the fixed generative sequence of the prerogative of violence, rule-making authority, and economic domination authority. All forms of social interaction, confrontation and game play unfold along the boundaries of the geopolitical field. The system defines atomic-constructs as primitive agentic units, and the transport-capacity of composite constructs aggregates from atomic-constructs. The steady-state operation of meta-constructs is sustained by a virtual-real coupling mechanism: physical constructs bear transport-capacity to schedule potentiality, while virtual constructs buffer systemic stress. This system demarcates strict boundaries among transport-capacity, potentiality and force, and clarifies the three-dimensional dislocation among potentiality lock-in, nominal ownership and scheduling authority. It defines the micro decay mechanisms of social substrates via weathering and oxidation of atomic-constructs, and identifies the fundamental contradiction of social systems as the confrontation between the spontaneous disorderly growth tendency of social-unit constructs and the order constraints imposed by meta-constructs. This axiomatic paradigm can self-consistently interpret the full dynamical process of iteration and gaming of social organizations, institutional evolution, power succession, and the rise and collapse of meta-constructs. It provides a unified underlying modeling framework for comparative research on social organizations across civilizations and institutional forms.

**Keywords**: Flux (C); Construct (S); social-unit construct; meta-construct; interface; potentiality; transport-capacity; force; stress; physical construct; virtual construct; weathering; oxidation; social field

## Preface

This system models and interprets social-unit interactions, organizational genesis and extinction, power games, institutional iteration, and the evolutionary rise and fall of meta-constructs. The theory is divided into two layers. The first layer, the Flux-Construct ontology, contains only primitive conceptual definitions and introduces no social-specific axioms. The second layer, the social mapping of Flux-Construct, inherits all primitive ontological concepts, adds definitions and axioms exclusive to the social domain, and closes the logical loop from primitive flux to complex human social phenomena. Derivative manuscripts accompanying this system include A40-01 Evolution, A40-02 Political Field, and A40-03 Economic Field. All primitive axioms and concepts are benchmarked against this A40-00. Derivative manuscripts do not repeat primitive definitions and only carry out scenario deduction and phenomenon extension.

# Part I Flux-Construct Ontology

## Chapter 1 Flux and Construct Ontology

> **Preliminary Remark**: Terms such as force, potentiality and stress in this system borrow the shell of physical terminology, but refer to inter-construct relations and locked flux states. They do not share ontological commitments with physics.

### Flux and Construct

Flux and construct coexist as an indivisible whole.
Flux: The primordial disordered flux of the universe, directionless, ruleless and boundless.
Construct: A framework that locks, constrains and orders flux. All observable entities, organizations and structures are ordered flux ensembles locked and constrained by constructs.

A complete construct contains two components simultaneously:

1. The total locked flux, i.e. the potentiality borne by the construct;
2. The rules and paths constraining the motion of flux.

Constructs are inherently nested. Multi-layer nesting is permitted: large constructs contain sub-constructs, and all real-world systems are multi-layered composite structures.

### Potentiality

A construct is a constraining framework for locking flux. Potentiality is the endowment locked inside a construct that can be transported and perform work. Potentiality depends on constructs for its existence; a construct itself is not potentiality. All potentiality is borne by constructs, which are the sole carrier of potentiality. Potentiality separated from a construct cannot exist and returns to primordial disordered flux.

### Field

Constructs interact with one another under corresponding conditions. A field describes the relational interaction between constructs. Different fields differ in interaction range and constraint rules.

### Force

Force is the representation of mutual tendency in the interaction between constructs within a field. It may manifest as attractive tendency or repulsive tendency, with magnitude marking interaction intensity. Once the interaction dissolves, the tendential bond vanishes and the force vanishes accordingly. Therefore, force is not an entity; it cannot be transmitted, transported or accumulated.
Note on colloquial expression: “Force transmission” used within the system is merely a loose expression. At the strict ontological level, force is not transmitted. The so-called transmission means that prior interactions alter the state of constructs, bringing other constructs sequentially into the same tendential bond and triggering chained interactions. Potentiality is the sole carrier of transport. An interaction does not necessarily accompany potentiality transport; potentiality transport is a subset of inter-construct interaction rather than a necessary condition for interaction.

### Stress

Stress is the mutual confrontation generated among sub-constructs after deformation of sub-constructs inside a construct. It falls into two categories:

1. Exogenous stress: Confrontation induced by compression from external constructs and external constraints.
2. Endogenous stress: Confrontation formed when open sub-constructs continuously absorb potentiality, spontaneously expand in volume, and squeeze one another within the boundary of the same superordinate construct.

> **Remark**: Internal/external is relative to the observation level. From the perspective of two interacting sub-constructs, each is an external construct to the other and the interaction counts as an external force. From the perspective of the superordinate construct containing both, this sustained interaction is internal stress of the superordinate construct. Switching observation levels reverses the internal/external attribute.

### Potentiality Transport

Potentiality transport is the process of potentiality transfer between constructs during their interaction. Inter-construct interaction is a prerequisite for potentiality transport; interaction does not necessarily trigger transport. If an overarching construct contains both interacting parties, the boundary of potentiality transport is constrained by this overarching construct. If no overarching construct exists, transport rules are determined by the participating constructs themselves.

Four outcome states arise from inter-construct interaction. Absorption, transit and stripping keep potentiality in a transport-active state. Dissolution means the locking mechanism of the construct fails and order disintegrates; the scope of dissolution is determined by the hierarchical rank of the construct.

- Absorption: The local construct acquires potentiality from its interaction counterpart, raising its own potentiality while depleting the counterpart’s.
- Transit: Potentiality flows through the interface of the local construct. The locked stock of the local construct remains unchanged and potentiality flows outward to another construct.
- Stripping: Part of the potentiality locked within the local construct is moved out and stripped by an external construct, lowering the potentiality of the local construct.
- Dissolution
  - threshold breach (rare): Continuous inflow of potentiality exceeds the maximum locking capacity of the construct, leading to holistic dissolution.
  - nodal rupture (dominant): A construct is supported by multi-layer coupled sub-constructs. Interactions apply load to construct nodes, whose bearing strength is uneven. Weak nodes fail first. Rupture of minor sub-nodes causes local failure; large constructs have fault tolerance and dissolve progressively. Rupture of core master nodes triggers cascading failure and holistic dissolution of the construct.

# Part II Social Mapping of Flux-Construct

## Chapter 2 Fundamental Social Units

### 2.1 Atomic-construct

Atomic-construct: Within this model, the indivisible agentic independent unit at the social level, i.e. an individual human being. An atomic-construct is the minimal physical construct. All social composite constructs (families, governmental agencies, regimes, states, commercial organizations) are formed by nesting, locking and constraining atomic-constructs.

Unique attributes of atomic-constructs: They bear exclusive potentiality and schedule their own potentiality autonomously, constituting the origin of primitive agency within the system. Physical capacity, knowledge, volition and spiritual growth potential are all potentiality locked within atomic-constructs. Composite constructs couple from atomic-constructs, aggregate transport-capacity via atomic-constructs, and generate organizational volition and agency. All physical constructs possess volition and agency.

Atomic-construct strength: The inherent bearing capacity of an atomic-construct, split into physical capacity and spiritual growth potential. Physical capacity sets the upper bound of short-term potentiality bearing; spiritual growth potential defines long-term potential.

### 2.2 Interface

Interface: The channel of contract, potentiality transfer and collaboration reached between two interacting social agents. Order is an intrinsic attribute emerging after the steady-state operation of an interface. Sustained operation of a stable interface converges into reproducible interactive order.

Interface rigidity: Characterizes the strength of upper-tier compulsory rule backstop for an interface. The core distinction lies in whether a backstop of upper-tier compulsory rules exists.

Interface roughness: Characterizes the variance of resistance as potentiality crosses the interface. Gentle roughness = uniform resistance and low friction; steep roughness = abrupt local resistance and high friction.

Interfaces are classified into flexible interfaces and rigid interfaces by rigidity. The core distinction lies in whether a backstop of upper-tier compulsory rules exists.

#### 2.2.1 Flexible Interface

No unified upper-tier rules or compulsory backstop. The interactive channel forms only through personal trust, verbal agreements and tacit understanding between agents.
Features: No institutional guarantee, blurred boundaries, high stochasticity, heavily dependent on the personal state of agents.
Typical examples: One-off verbal supply agreements between private parties; an employee negotiating a pay rise individually with an employer.

#### 2.2.2 Rigid Interface

Backed by pre-established upper-tier rules at a higher hierarchical level. Agents conclude secondary contracts and form stable interactive channels within the constraint framework of unified upper-tier rules.
The coercive force of upper-tier rules directly determines the rigidity of the interface and the efficiency of potentiality transport.
Core logic: The more unified and coercive upper-tier rules are, the higher the interface rigidity, the lower transaction friction, and the higher potentiality transport efficiency.

Case comparison:

1. Domestic commercial transactions: Within one state-construct, backed by a unified legal system. Contracts are enforceable, the interface is rigid, transaction friction is low and transport efficiency is high.
2. Cross-border international trade: Transactions belong to separate state-constructs with independent legal systems and inconsistent rules. There is naturally no unified top-level rule enforceable across all participants, leading to high transaction friction. Frameworks such as WTO emerge to mitigate this difficulty. Yet the WTO has no coercive authority overriding national laws, and its binding force is far weaker than domestic legislation within a single state. Hence the interface rigidity of international trade remains weaker than domestic trade.

Supplementary remark: Contracts take many forms. Law is merely one category of highly rigid contract. Regulations, customs, verbal agreements and industry conventions are alternative contractual forms that can constitute an interface.

#### 2.2.3 Two Core Evaluation Metrics of Interfaces

The performance of all interfaces is defined by two core metrics, independent of agent scale and volume:

1. Transport efficiency: Jointly determined by interface rigidity and interface roughness. Higher interface rigidity and gentler interface roughness yield higher transport efficiency. Interface roughness describes the smoothness of the interface and the distribution of resistance during potentiality transport: gentler roughness means a smoother interface, lower friction for potentiality flow; steeper roughness means higher flow resistance and lower transport efficiency.
2. Interface lifespan: A bottom buffer cushion can be built to dissipate interactive stress and distribute wear, effectively extending the steady-state duration of the interface.

### 2.3 Social-unit Construct

Social-unit construct: A social unit formed by linkage through interfaces, with a unified identity and capable of independent external interaction.

Core definition: Social-unit constructs include but are not identical to social organizational entities. A social organizational entity is a subset whose identity gains broad group recognition. A construct qualifies as a social-unit construct once a new unified identity distinct from the original atomic-constructs emerges and the unit can act externally under this identity. This identity requires no registration or confirmation by a meta-construct and need not persist permanently. A construct solidifies merely by consensus from internal contracts and may dissolve once the event terminates. Every social-unit construct contains contractual internal interfaces that sustain itself.

Examples: A temporary group of employees jointly submitting demands, ad-hoc squads, spontaneous factions, tribal polities, registered families, corporate legal persons, courts and government agencies. Some identities receive endorsement from meta-construct confirmation; others only achieve consensus within a circle without upper-tier validation.

#### 2.3.1 Physical Construct

Physical construct: A tangible social-unit construct coupled from atomic-constructs. Atomic-constructs are the foundational components of physical constructs. Physical constructs form organizations and transport-capacity relying on atomic-constructs, acting as the implementation carrier of three-tier power and potentiality scheduling. Transport-capacity is the exclusive feature of physical constructs.

Definition of dual internal-external interfaces: Dual interfaces include internal interfaces and external interfaces. Internal interfaces are contractual channels for interaction among components inside the physical construct. External interfaces are contractual channels for the physical construct to engage with​ other constructs. Both categories are constrained by meta-construct rules.

Distinction between physical constructs and natural objects: Man-made artifacts such as farm tools, roads, bridges and city walls are constructs at the natural level. They are potentiality carriers invoked by physical constructs and are not physical constructs themselves.

Core features:

1. Tangibility: Possess physical scale, including size, spatial scope and organizational structure;
2. Bearing substrate: Atomic-constructs must occupy functional positions within it. No physical construct exists without atomic-constructs; without atomic-constructs, only an empty framework remains;
3. Functional bearing: Bears three tiers of power: the prerogative of violence, rule-making authority, and economic domination authority. It covers production and the full scope of potentiality scheduling as the carrier of daily social operation. The prerogative of violence serves as the ultimate backstop and is normally activated only upon interface failure and conflict outbreak;
4. Interaction dynamics: Internal stress arises from internal interfaces; interaction stress is generated through external interfaces in exchanges with other constructs. System instability occurs once stress crosses the threshold.

Examples: Composite constructs such as dynasties, enterprises and states (government institutions); nodal roles within constructs such as monarchs, chief ministers, presidents and general managers. The same individual acts as a nodal role of the construct in public capacity and as an atomic-construct in private capacity.

#### 2.3.2 Virtual Construct

Virtual construct: A constraining structure that does not hold transport-capacity or schedule potentiality directly, yet can alter interaction paths and stress distribution among physical constructs. Its function is to buffer interactive stress between physical constructs, rather than eliminate stress itself.

Subtypes

- Rigid virtual construct (legal and institutional type): Backed ultimately by the prerogative of violence from physical constructs, it demarcates boundaries around production and potentiality distribution order. Legal texts and institutional documents serve as its blueprint. A virtual construct solidifies once statutory procedures take effect, independent of whether individual atomic-constructs are aware of it. Breaches trigger sanctions, and the violent backstop activates when stress rises. It solidifies through promulgation and establishment, enforced implementation as the supporting mechanism. Rigid virtual constructs depend on meta-constructs for existence and dissolve together with the collapse of the meta-construct.
- Flexible virtual construct (cultural and ethical type): Not directly backed by violence, it operates through intrinsic group recognition. Morality, ideology and status norms serve as its guiding blueprint. It relies on edification and repeated sedimentation, gradually solidifying within group self-awareness to form stable spontaneous behavioral norms that constrain interactions of atomic-constructs.

Core value: Guide atomic-constructs to abide by order, redistribute stress among physical constructs, and reduce the risk of systemic collapse caused by stress overload.

Evolution remark: The functional evolution of virtual constructs and transition conditions between rigidity and flexibility are discussed in A40-01 Evolution.

### 2.4 Social Potentiality (hereafter shortened to potentiality)

All games, growth, contention and dissolution within a social system are essentially the distribution, transfer, contestation and reconstruction of potentiality.

Potentiality attaches to three categories of carriers:

1. Atomic-construct (agentic carrier): The only unit capable of autonomously bearing, scheduling and releasing potentiality. Knowledge, physical capacity and spiritual potentiality all attach to atomic-constructs. Atomic-constructs may transact and conclude contracts directly without intermediation from higher composite constructs.
2. Geographical foundational elements: Underlying substrate including land, water systems, minerals and climate, setting the upper bound of systemic potentiality. Secondary potentiality is generated jointly by atomic-constructs and geographical elements.
3. Wealth: Generated by atomic-constructs laboring upon geographical elements, split into two classes:
   - Tools: Reusable assets such as farm implements, weaponry, roads and fortifications;
   - Consumables (nutrient substrates): Fabrics, grain and other goods whose potentiality dissipates upon consumption.

### 2.5 Transport-capacity

Transport-capacity is the ability of an atomic-construct to schedule potentiality. Physical constructs aggregate from atomic-constructs and form collective scheduling capacity through their coupled structure. The final achievable scheduling performance is jointly determined by the scheduling capacity itself and the stock of schedulable potentiality resources.

Core boundary judgements:

1. Transport-capacity ≠ potentiality. Potentiality is the locked, schedulable endowment stock; transport-capacity is the capacity to schedule that endowment.
2. Transport-capacity ≠ force. Social agents may interact; this interaction maps ontologically to a tendential bond between constructs. Transport-capacity is the scheduling attribute inherent to a physical construct. Only with transport-capacity can an agent drive potentiality transport.
3. Threefold dislocation of potentiality lock-in, nominal ownership and scheduling authority. Lock-in is the ontological reality of potentiality detained within a constraining framework. Ownership is a nominal convention generated by social interfaces. Even if a meta-construct holds nominal ownership over the whole domain, constraints from links, rules and sub-construct separation usually mean its transport-capacity is insufficient to schedule all internally locked potentiality.
4. Transport-capacity belongs exclusively to physical constructs. Virtual constructs supply rules, recognition and status norms; they do not hold or schedule potentiality and possess no transport-capacity.

> **Remark**: At the ontological level, only lock-in exists. “Ownership” is a convention generated by contractual interfaces once entering the social mapping layer and cannot be equated directly with lock-in.

### 2.6 Inter-construct Force (hereafter shortened to force)

- Interaction: Phenomenological description at the level of social agents (human beings, social organizations), referring to communication, contact and contracting. Exchanges between persons or social agents are called interactions.
- Inter-construct force: Ontological modelling term describing the directional tendential bond between constructs underlying such interactions. Interaction is the manifestation; force is the underlying relational state.

Force: A directional mutual tendential bond between constructs within the social domain, the social instantiation of the ontological-layer force. Force is jointly determined by the relationship and endowments of both constructs, and may present attractive tendency, repulsive tendency, or both, pulling toward equilibrium. Force is a relational quantity and cannot be accumulated; it dissipates once the interaction terminates. Zero force does not mean the construct lacks endowment or diminished transport-capacity; it indicates either decay of transport-capacity or absence of interaction conditions.

Two categories:

1. External force: Interaction between two independent social constructs.
2. Stress: Confrontational squeezing force between sub-constructs within the boundary of one parent construct. Stress describes the instantaneous interaction state. Contradictions and evolutionary paths of potentiality are treated in A40-01 Evolution.

### 2.7 Construct Formulae

**Formula 1: Scale Function**
Construct Scale = FC-scale (Atomic-construct, External Potentiality)
Function Statement:
FC-scale is the scale function solving the overall volume of a construct. Atomic-constructs are the agentic substrate and external potentiality denotes attached resources. This formula expresses the material and agentic origin of constructs and corresponds to the logic of grassroots formation.
Parameters:

- Atomic-construct: All agentic units within the construct, with inherent potentiality and native transport-capacity
- External Potentiality: External resources absorbed by the construct, including land, minerals, materials, capital and equipment

**Formula 2: Strength Function**
Construct Strength = FC-strength (Configuration, Mean Atomic-construct Strength, Atomic-construct Strength Distribution)
Function Statement:
FC-strength is the strength function solving a construct’s capacity to resist stress and preserve structural integrity against dissolution. Under identical configuration, higher overall atomic-construct strength yields higher construct strength. Widespread decay among atomic-constructs reduces construct strength.
Parameters:

- Configuration: Organizational form, including institutions, frameworks, hierarchies, coupled nodes and structural layout
- Mean Atomic-construct Strength: Mean strength value of all atomic-constructs within the construct
- Atomic-construct Strength Distribution: The distribution of strength values among atomic-constructs of the construct

**Formula 3: Efficiency Function**
Construct Transport-capacity = FC-efficiency (Sum of Native Atomic-construct Transport-capacity, Configuration)
Function Statement:
FC-efficiency is the efficiency function solving the effective output capacity of a construct for scheduling potentiality. Constructs possess no independent native transport-capacity. Aggregate transport-capacity derives from the aggregation of native transport-capacity of internal atomic-constructs, and transport efficiency is determined by configuration.
Parameters:

- Sum of Native Atomic-construct Transport-capacity: Sum of native transport-capacity belonging to all atomic-constructs inside the construct
- Configuration: Organizational coupling framework of the construct

> **Remark**: These are paradigm functions expressing variable dependency relations. No fixed analytical expression is predefined; explicit forms are deduced for specific scenarios in A40-01 Evolution.

## Chapter 3 Meta-constructs and Social Fields

### 3.1 Meta-constructs, Power Sub-constructs and the Three-tier Power Chain

Meta-construct: A composite social-unit construct formed by multiple sub-constructs converging after power games and linked by interfaces. It possesses a stable order framework and can evolve autonomously, serving as the primary analytical starting point for A40-01 Evolution. **A meta-construct is ultimately backed by the prerogative of violence; this is its defining distinction from ordinary composite constructs.**

Violence is an integrated force and the ultimate backstop of rules. Games in traditional societies often unfold directly relying on violence. Modern civilizations seek to restrict and constrain violence, yet violence remains the underlying backstop of order. After a meta-construct solidifies, it sequentially bears three core powers: the prerogative of violence, rule-making authority, and economic domination authority.

Power sub-construct: A sub-construct nested inside a meta-construct, which aggregates violent potentiality within local boundaries and holds independent transport-capacity. Inherent stress conflict exists between the top-tier meta-construct and power sub-constructs. The top-tier meta-construct tends to centralize the prerogative of violence uniformly, while power sub-constructs incline toward outward expansion and autonomous persistence.

Two-dimensional classification of meta-constructs:
Dimension 1: Classified by the degree of aggregation of the prerogative of violence (power intensity)

1. High-sovereignty meta-construct: The prerogative of violence is fully centralized. No independent competing power sub-construct exists within its domain, and the top tier can stably impose universal compulsory order. Example: Unified dynastic empires.
2. Low-sovereignty meta-construct: The top tier holds nominal universal order, yet its transport-capacity is insufficient to monopolize violence. Competing power sub-constructs coexist within the field, sustaining a long-term high-stress transitional game state. Examples: The Zhou royal system in the Spring and Autumn period, regional meta-constructs with armed separatist factions.

Dimension 2: Classified by the carrier of the prerogative of violence

1. Identity-bound sovereignty: The prerogative of violence binds to bloodline and personal identity.
2. Institution-bound sovereignty: The prerogative of violence binds to a fixed organizational carrier.
3. Impersonal sovereignty: The prerogative of violence decouples from persons and fixed organizations and anchors to public rules; rule-centering is the core feature of this form.

### 3.2 Social Field

Social field: An observational framework representing the distribution and interactive patterns of force and stress among meta-constructs and among sub-constructs within a meta-construct. The field itself is empty and is not a construct, analogous to a gravitational field manifesting only through massive bodies. We use meta-constructs as the core carrier for the study of social evolution, applying this field framework to describe cross-meta-construct games and inter-construct relations within meta-construct boundaries.

The fundamental social-field system has two tiers: **geopolitical field and power field**. The power field subdivides into three independent sub-fields, yielding four fundamental interaction fields in total. The three sub-fields of the power field correspond one-to-one with the three-tier power structure: the prerogative of violence, rule-making authority, and economic domination authority.

#### 3.2.1 Geopolitical Field

Attribute: Observational framework representing geographic constraints and locational interaction distribution among and within meta-constructs.
Origin logic: The population and potentiality persistence of meta-constructs and attached sub-constructs rely on geographic carriers. The geopolitical field demarcates interaction boundaries through mountains, rivers and territorial borders.
Evolution features: The geographic substrate is relatively stable. Meta-constructs holding the prerogative of violence may rewrite boundaries, expanding, contracting and reconstructing territory as power rises and falls. Different meta-constructs may engage in territorial contests within this field.
Activation logic: Interactions are bounded by geography and location, limiting the scope of field manifestation.
Phenomena represented: Geopolitical contest, territorial contention, territorial compression, locational balancing.

#### 3.2.2 Power Field

Attribute: Master observational framework for power operation, covering two layers: (1) order and potentiality-scheduling force among sub-constructs inside a meta-construct; (2) confrontation and power games between independent meta-constructs. Games and stress originate from physical constructs bearing potentiality. Commodity circulation and trade are secondary manifestations of potentiality scheduling by physical constructs. Circulation channels themselves carry no potentiality and cannot initiate interactions. Operating logic: Backstop by the prerogative of violence; rule-making authority produces rules; long-term implementation of rules solidifies into virtual-real coupled constructs (physical + virtual constructs); economic domination is implemented relying on this set of constructs.
Origin logic: The need of meta-constructs to sustain order, lock boundaries and stabilize governance gives rise to this field framework. Collisions of power between independent meta-constructs are also represented herein. The field couples three functionally complementary sub-fields.
Evolution features: More complex meta-construct systems yield finer differentiation of force represented within the power field. When multiple meta-constructs coexist, off-field games continuously perturb internal order.
Activation logic: The effective range of force follows jurisdiction and order-recognition boundaries. Cross-meta-construct interactions escape the governance of a single meta-construct and are constrained overall by the geopolitical field.
Phenomena represented: Regime succession, institutional founding, economic governance, cross-regime games, maintenance of social order.

##### Violence Prerogative Sub-field

Attribute: Observational framework representing force in the violent dimension. It describes internal backstopping, sanctioning and boundary locking within a single meta-construct, as well as violent confrontation and warfare games between independent meta-constructs. The prerogative of violence is borne by physical constructs carrying violent potentiality, and all inter-construct force manifests within this sub-field.
Origin logic: Requirements for confirmation of rights, boundary locking and order stabilization of meta-constructs necessitate this sub-field to model violent backstop force. Coexistence of multiple meta-constructs generates cross-meta-construct violent contestation. The legitimacy of all rights, adjudication, resource allocation and geopolitical boundaries is ultimately guaranteed by the prerogative of violence.
Evolution features: Every mature meta-construct contains a violent dimension. In multi-meta-construct systems, confrontation of violent potentiality continuously reshapes the field pattern.
Activation logic: Within one meta-construct, the scope follows governance boundaries. Cross-meta-construct interactions define their range through potentiality confrontation and deterrence envelopes.
Phenomena represented: Order maintenance, boundary confirmation, violent backstop, sanction, warfare, regime foundation, systemic prevention of dissolution.

##### Rule-making Authority Sub-field

Attribute: Observational framework representing force of order validation. It models games over law, rights and status within a meta-construct, and collision, imitation and confrontation between rule systems of independent meta-constructs. Rule-making authority is borne by physical constructs carrying order potentiality, and inter-construct force manifests within this sub-field.
Origin logic: Society requires definition of status, law and rights to establish underlying order. Coexistence of multiple meta-constructs generates institutional competition. This sub-field models this class of order-validating force.
Evolution features: Rights-related forces evolve across eras. Competition between meta-constructs drives paradigm shifts.
Activation logic: Inside a meta-construct, force relies on order recognition and jurisdiction. Cross-meta-construct interactions lack unified compulsory constraints; influence is determined by institutional attractiveness and game outcomes.
Phenomena represented: Codification of rites and law, succession confirmation, institutional founding, power validation, legal revision, institutional contest, competition of civilizational paradigms.

##### Economic Domination Authority Sub-field

Attribute: Observational framework representing force for economic potentiality scheduling. It covers wealth distribution and resource allocation inside meta-constructs, and economic trade games and potentiality tug-of-war between independent meta-constructs. Production, distribution, transaction and commodity circulation are secondary expressions of potentiality scheduling by economic domination authority. Circulation carriers carry no potentiality and cannot initiate games. Only economic domination physical constructs enriched with sufficient potentiality can reshape internal order within a meta-construct or compress other meta-constructs externally.
Origin logic: Social organizations require resource allocation and wealth control. Coexistence of multiple meta-constructs generates cross-border trade games. Wealth carriers can circulate, but only potentiality locked within economic domination constructs acquires directed capacity to perform work.
Evolution features: Simple social organizations have single scheduling rules. Rising social complexity diversifies forms of inter-construct force. In multi-meta-construct systems, the pattern of economic potentiality flow grows correspondingly complex.
Activation logic: The effective range of force relies on control networks and resource channels. Cross-meta-construct interactions are constrained jointly by geography, violence and rules.
Phenomena represented: Wealth production, multi-tier distribution, resource scheduling, cross-regional trade games, economic regulation.

> **Remark**: The four fields follow a generative sequence, not a unidirectional operating sequence. During system operation, the four fields feed back and perturb one another bidirectionally. Enriched economic domination constructs within or across meta-constructs may reverse-perturb the patterns of violence and rule sub-fields.

### 3.3 Dual Functional Classification of Constructs (by core function)

#### 3.3.1 Social Construct

Social constructs are the core agents of potentiality generation, accumulation and consumption within the system. They possess the innate underlying attribute of spontaneous growth and profit-seeking expansion. They may spontaneously accumulate resources and potentiality without top-tier authorization and are the source of primitive kinetic energy across the whole domain.
Typical forms: Private partnerships, market industrial clusters, commercial organizations, stable informal collaborative groups, temporary coalitions of agents submitting collective demands.

#### 3.3.2 Public Construct

Public constructs are agents of rule governance and order maintenance for potentiality circulation within the system. They do not produce potentiality themselves, yet are also social-unit constructs with growth attributes. Their potentiality absorption and boundary expansion are constrained by governance functions and top-tier check-and-balance frameworks. Core functions: Backed by the prerogative of violence, they formulate top-tier rules, set locking constraints, demarcate interaction boundaries, regulate paths and thresholds of potentiality circulation, block systemic risks and sustain whole-domain steady state.
Typical forms: Courts, government administrative systems, regulatory agencies, national institutional systems.

### 3.4 Weathering and Oxidation of Atomic-constructs

#### Weathering

Weathering: A structural state of growth stagnation, vitality decay and continuous decline of native transport-capacity caused by long-term insufficient material nutrient input and restricted channels for spiritual nourishment for an atomic-construct. Weathering is gradual, progressing from mild growth retardation to severe exhaustion of driving force.
Grades of weathering: Mild weathering = growth retardation; severe weathering = near exhaustion of growth momentum. Widespread occurrence of this restriction generates social weathering. A rising proportion of weathered atomic-constructs reduces the vitality and external game capacity of the large construct.
Typical examples: Personal confinement under slavery; persistently draining harsh labour environments; suppression of civilian cognitive channels.

#### Oxidation

Oxidation: A structural state of motivational passivation, functional slackening and continuous decay of native transport-capacity for an atomic-construct, arising from excessive saturation of material nutrients together with dual absence of competitive pressure and spiritual stimulation. Surplus nutrients themselves constitute the oxidizing agent: no external oppression is required. The elimination of scarcity constraints and competitive challenges deprives the atomic-construct of intrinsic motivation for sustained scheduling. Oxidation is gradual, progressing from mild consensus dissipation to severe scheduling incapacity.
Widespread spread of this state among ruling groups generates social oxidation. A rising share of oxidized agents at the top tier leads to failure of master nodes of the large construct and collapse of distribution chains.

Boundary remark: Weathering and oxidation are judged relative to the position of the atomic-construct within the observation hierarchy. One atomic-construct may simultaneously carry both risks under different observational perspectives: viewed downward, it belongs to a dominating agent and faces oxidation risk; viewed upward, it is a constrained agent and faces weathering risk.

## Chapter 4 Axioms of Social Constructs

**Axiom 1: Openness Axiom**
All construct units within the social system, with atomic-constructs (individual human beings) as the minimal unit, and all tiers of composite constructs (families, organizations, polities, states), are living open systems.
Openness is the only intrinsic attribute for the persistence and growth of living systems. Complete closure with zero exchange extinguishes life. The “closure” referenced in this system means only selective blocking of partial potentiality, information and flow channels. No fully closed social living unit exists.

**Axiom 2: Agency Axiom**
Interactions among entities in the natural world occur passively and spontaneously.
Social units differ in possessing agency.
Agency manifests as follows: a social unit may initiate interactions relying on its own transport-capacity and exert force upon other units. Such force can damage and deconstruct other social constructs.

**Axiom 3: Self-growth Axiom**
All social-unit constructs, including atomic-constructs and composite constructs at all tiers, possess the inherent growth attribute of living systems. Constructs take potentiality persistence as their fundamental substrate. They spontaneously gravitate toward regions of high potentiality, continuously absorb external potentiality, expand in volume, extend boundaries and spawn differentiated sub-constructs. This growth tendency is fixed primitive kinetic energy of the system. External rule constraints can only modulate growth efficiency and boundary scope, and cannot eliminate the profit-seeking growth instinct of constructs.

**Axiom 4: Disorder Axiom**
No innate self-consistent global order exists in social systems. The spontaneous growth behaviours of separate social constructs are independent and lack natural coordination mechanisms. When multiple social-unit constructs coexist within one field, their mutually incompatible expansion drives produce continuous boundary conflict and potentiality contestation, generating steady endogenous stress. The fundamental contradiction of the system is the permanent confrontation between the inevitable growth tendency of constructs and the absence of naturally coordinated growth.

**Axiom 5: Public-Private Coupling Axiom**
Public constructs and social constructs cannot be fully separated by nature. All operational social fields are composite coupled systems of public and private constructs.
The intrinsic core fissure of the system: profit-driven potentiality accumulated by social constructs spontaneously infiltrates, co-opts and erodes the rule-governance authority of public constructs. This mechanism is the ontological origin of rent-seeking, corruption and systemic imbalance. It inevitably deteriorates absent strong top-tier constraints.

## Chapter 5 Minimal Closed Loop of the System

The system strictly follows a fixed evolutionary hierarchy: ontological primitives as foundation, social-unit mapping implementation, formation of meta-construct fields, ordering by social axioms, finally converging into a complete social dynamics closed loop.

Tier one: Underlying Flux-Construct ontology substrate. Flux and construct coexist as a unity. Constructs lock disordered flux and bear potentiality, and constructs are inherently multi-layer nested. The ontological layer uniformly defines four forms: potentiality, force, stress, potentiality transport, and two dissolution modes. It establishes general dynamical rules independent of social forms, the invariant underlying substrate of the whole framework.

Tier two: Social-unit construct mapping system. Ontological primitives are mapped onto human society. Atomic-constructs are defined as the sole primitive agentic units. The binary structure is distinguished: physical constructs bear transport-capacity while virtual constructs buffer stress. It clarifies that interface rigidity and roughness determine potentiality transport efficiency. It unifies the boundaries of potentiality, transport-capacity and force, and resolves the three-dimensional dislocation among potentiality lock-in, nominal ownership and scheduling authority, completing rule instantiation for all social interactive units.

Tier three: Meta-construct and social field system. Centered on high-order composite social-unit constructs, namely meta-constructs, the geopolitical field emerges from meta-construct territorial boundaries. Backed by the prerogative of violence as the ultimate foundation, meta-constructs sequentially form three power sub-fields: prerogative of violence, rule-making authority, and economic domination authority, jointly composing the master power field. The generative sequence of fields is unidirectional. In actual operation, all fields act upon and receive action from one another, paired as action and reaction, evolving synergistically. Material goods are manifestations of potentiality. Potentiality migration occurs through inter-construct force. If transfer constraints exist, those constraints belong to the interface.

Tier four: Social construct axioms. The five axioms of openness, agency, self-growth, disorder and public-private coupling define the innate attributes of social units. The expansive spontaneous growth and disorderly tendency of units confront the artificially constructed order constraints of meta-constructs, forming the fundamental contradiction of social systems that governs organizational evolution, institutional iteration and rise-fall processes.

From these tiers, a fixed causal chain of power and rise-fall logic converges. The steady-state path of power evolution: prerogative of violence backstops stability → rule-making authority establishes order → order solidifies into virtual-real coupled construct systems → finally economic domination authority implements potentiality scheduling. Virtual constructs continuously perform the function of stress buffering, and virtual-real coupling sustains the overall steady state of the meta-construct.

The rise and collapse of meta-constructs are fundamentally determined by the micro state of atomic-constructs and macro stress state of the construct. External geopolitical shocks and cross-meta-construct contests are superimposed perturbations. Yet two conditions are necessary for dissolution: stress exceeding the strength boundary of the construct, and decay of atomic-constructs. Absence of buffer mechanisms inside the meta-construct causes stress to exceed the bearing range of construct strength and triggers internal rupture. Widespread weathering of atomic-constructs depletes underlying potentiality and weakens the substrate, lowering the construct’s shock resistance. Collective oxidation and failure of top-tier master nodes break the order transmission chain directly, triggering layer-wise dissolution of the meta-construct propagating outward from the core.
