## I. Fundamental Concepts

### 1.1 Tracing the Origin

The political field defined herein corresponds to the field of rule-making power in the ontological classification of A40-00. The other two primitive fields in A40-00 are the field of violent dominion, corresponding to the military field, and the field of economic dominion right, corresponding to the A40-03 economic field.

### 1.2 Politics

Politics refers to a type of social activity in which political agents deploy their own transport capacity to carry out a sequence of actions including theme framing, dissemination, interpretation and cultivation, forming virtual constructs that shape human mentality, so as to strive for rule-making power or administrative power, and ultimately transform society and reshape social order.

**Boundary between Source Materials and Virtual Constructs**:
Written agendas, textual slogans are merely source blueprints. Only when source materials, after dissemination and cultivation, take root in group cognition and can stably guide human conduct and decision-making, do they solidify into virtual constructs. Source materials themselves are not virtual constructs.

**Functional Evolution of Virtual Constructs**:
Per A40-00, the original function of virtual constructs is to buffer and mitigate interactive stress between physical constructs. Alongside social evolution, virtual constructs acquire derived functions: within political activities, they serve to unify collective sentiment and facilitate contestation and bargaining.

**Basic Process of Political Activity**:
A typical pathway of political activity: social maladies → objectives → narratives → dissemination → laws and statutes → sustained reinforcement｜iterative revision. Sustained reinforcement relies on regular enforcement and repeated fulfilment of laws and statutes. Through long-term immersion, rules are internalised within group mentality and precipitate into stable virtual constructs. This pathway describes a common evolutionary thread rather than an inflexible, necessary procedure; stagnation, reversion and dissipation may occur at any stage. Iterative revision continuously incorporates feedback from new social stress, recalibrating narratives and amending statutes to adapt to shifting real-world conditions.

The principle *debts shall be repaid, murderers shall forfeit their lives* is an example of a virtual construct consolidated in public mentality through long-standing enforcement of law.

### 1.3 The Political Field

The political field is the interactive space where political agents contest one another, deploy transport capacity, frame themes, cultivate virtual constructs and compete for allegiance of collective sentiment.

**Behavioural Distinction of Public Physical Constructs**:
The fundamental function of public physical constructs is to implement established rules. Implementation by itself does not constitute political activity. Nevertheless, such agents may draw upon their transport capacity to launch agendas and join contests, thereby engaging in political activity.

**Distinction between Field and Interface**:
An interface specifically denotes a binding construct formed by covenant. The political field is not an interface; it is the space where contesting agents interact, potentiality circulates, and stress emerges and recedes through interaction.

## II. The Five Political Affairs

Governance is the way of yang, not the way of guile. It bears the suffering of sentient beings, governs the convergence and divergence of collective sentiment, determines the cardinal framework of order, and sustains the continuity of civilisation. It demands careful examination.

Hence, governance is assessed by five affairs, weighed by the allegiance of collective sentiment: first, discernment; second, transmission; third, potential; fourth, order; fifth, preservation.

**Discernment**: The surface of politics lies in human contention and shifts of authority and regulation. The essence of politics rests upon a single dual tension — the suffering of sentient beings awaiting alleviation, and social growth awaiting expansion. The interplay of these two forces accumulates and manifests outward as social maladies, from which politics arises. Maladies are causes; order is an outcome, not an end target. A malady emerges from the balance between subsistence and growth: without subsistence, growth loses its foundation; suppressing growth deprives subsistence of its origin. This difficulty of balancing is the enduring condition of politics.

**Transmission**: Once maladies are discerned, principles must be established to dispel obscurity. Principles are not empty words; they are methods to dispel obscurity, communicated to the multitude to stabilise collective orientation. Three criteria govern principle: authenticity of principle, conciseness of expression, consistency of momentum. Principles originate from discernment, converge toward ends, and are validated through practice. Only when these three links hold in sequence can principles take shape. Authentic principle inspires trust, trust unites people; conciseness facilitates comprehension, comprehension enables transmission; consistency fosters coherence, coherence enables accomplishment. Without transmission, obscurity cannot be dispelled, and collective sentiment scatters without anchorage.

**Potential**: Once principles are disseminated and collective orientations converge, potential naturally accumulates. Potential is the source of aggregated potency. When potential forms, action follows, in two modes: to occupy institutional stations by virtue of accumulated potential, whereby potential dissipates once the station is lost; to establish institutions by virtue of accumulated potential, whereby institutions eradicate obscurity. The most robust constructs rest upon the most formidable forces. Greater potential underpins broader institutions; weaker potential confines institutional reach. Potential is not fabricated arbitrarily, but accumulated gradually. Ample potential may break the constraints of old obscurities; thin potential renders action difficult. Seeking governance without potential is like pursuing a current without a source. Potential is not permanent; it waxes and wanes with the allegiance of collective sentiment.

**Order**: Order constitutes the fundamental framework of the world and the track of conduct. Impartiality is the core of the framework; without impartiality, there exists no benchmark. Impartiality relies on commensuration: all judgements of right and wrong, gain and loss are made against a shared reference standard. The track has two requisites: firmness and adaptability. Without firmness, the track collapses; without adaptability, the track stagnates. Firmness is the foundation of conduct, like the rigidity of rails; adaptability enables smooth movement, like slippery ice.

**Preservation**: Preservation has two branches: safeguarding the cardinal benchmark, and adjusting the track to align with temporal rhythms. The cardinal benchmark establishes the standard for the world; revisions to it demand prudence. The track governs practical operation and may shift with the times to fit present conditions. Laws evolve alongside the times; enduring maladies exist, yet no law is eternal. The longer a law persists, the more consolidated its construct becomes, and consolidated constructs resist adjustment. The true meaning of preservation lies in safeguarding the cardinal benchmark while revising operational tracks. Failure in preservation leads either to rigid stagnation, or casual erosion of benchmarks.

## III. Concluding Remarks

Discernment, transmission, potential, order and preservation form a cyclic continuum, jointly constituting the closed loop of political evolution. Discernment recognises maladies and apprehends the enduring tension between subsistence and growth; transmission establishes principles and disseminates reasoning to collective mentality; potential accumulates collective potency to enable transformative action; order sets frameworks and tracks, defining public benchmarks of commensuration; preservation balances transformation, holding the fundamental steady while adapting to temporal shifts.

Governance is inseparable from maladies, and no law is everlasting. Virtual constructs wax and wane in response to stress within the field. Without discernment, governance is blind; without transmission, estrangement arises; without potential, action is isolated; without order, chaos prevails; without preservation, deviation ensues. When the five affairs operate in concert, the balance between subsistence and growth may be harmonised, field steady-state maintained, and civilisational continuity sustained.

The core of political governance is to uphold the margin of variational capacity within the field, sustaining the interplay of contraction and expansion — this is the source of enduring vitality for the field.
