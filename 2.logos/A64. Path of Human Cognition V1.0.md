# A64. Path of Human Cognition V1.0

## Abstract

This paper constructs a complete formation path for individual cognition. Entities in reality exist within an interconnected relational network. Human observations are constrained by position, perspective and instruments, and can only capture partial appearances; absolutely complete "seeing" does not exist.

Human cognitive capacity is jointly determined by innate endowment and acquired cultivation. Cognitive growth relies on two pathways: guidance from predecessors and personal practice. External cognitive materials can only be internalized through practical immersion and **Practical Calibration**. Cognitive heritage from predecessors falls into three categories: General Methods at the general layer, Domain Knowledge at the domain layer, and specific empirical experience at the case layer. General Methods are cross-scenario underlying analytical tools, consisting of internal-factor investigation and external-factor Relational Positioning. **Practical Calibration** generates personal exclusive landmarks to prevent cognitive disorientation and reuse proven exploration paths. Domain Knowledge refers to nested architectures modelled by predecessors for specific domains. Learning Domain Knowledge requires establishing a global perspective and self-orientation first, followed by in-depth research via Upward Tracing and Downward Deep-Dive. Domain architectures are derived from inherited acquisition or autonomous construction. As man-made analytical models, they have temporal and contextual boundaries and must not be rigidly upheld.

The complete closed loop of individual cognition: taking General Methods as the underlying foundation, achieving System Elevation by inheriting mature domain paradigms, solidifying personal landmarks through Practical Calibration, verifying and iterating via continuous practice, and ultimately forming a self-renewable personal cognitive system.

Formula for the generation of a cognitive system:
**External inherited materials + processing by General Methods + individual Practical Calibration points = complete personal cognitive system**

**Keywords**：Path of Cognition; Practical Calibration; Relational Positioning; System Elevation; General Methods; Domain Knowledge; Cognitive Internalization
## I. What It Means to "See"

**Worldview Premise**: All things in the universe are universally interconnected. No fully independent or isolated entities exist; reality forms a complex relational web woven together.

**Epistemological Proposition**: Any observation outcome is constrained by the observer’s position, perspective, time point, and observation instruments. What a cognitive agent can perceive and capture is merely partial appearances revealed by this complex relational web, analogous to the tip of an iceberg, while the vast majority of information remains hidden beneath the surface.
Given these constraints, to attain more comprehensive observation, one must actively adjust observation positions, adopt different observation tools, and conduct observations across multiple time points. This offsets cognitive limitations and expands the pool of information samples.

Cognition only varies in depth; there is no absolutely complete, ultimate form of "seeing".

Cognitive advancement starts from surface appearances. By tracing underlying drivers along causal chains, identifying connections between things, and untangling hierarchical constraints, cognition advances layer by layer into deeper rational understanding, which further guides one’s judgements and decisions.

## II. Sources of Cognitive Capacity

The cognitive capacity to trace the drivers behind phenomena is shaped jointly by innate endowment and acquired cultivation. Mastery of effective cognitive methods constitutes the core variable in cognitive construction.

Such cognitive methods cannot emerge out of thin air, nor can they be directly implanted through others’ instruction. Their acquisition relies on two pathways: guidance from predecessors and personal practice.

Guidance from predecessors reduces the cost of trial and error. It is divided into two types. The first is verbal and behavioral demonstration, illustrating perspectives and reasoning paths for analyzing problems in real-world scenarios. The second is reading and study, extracting predecessors’ thinking paradigms from texts. Both fall within the scope of the Inherited Corpus.

Guidance from predecessors nonetheless has limitations: it only enables the input of method-related information and cannot trigger automatic internalization. For instance, a coach may demonstrate how to shoot a football, and a teacher may teach the essentials of calligraphy brushwork. Without hands-on practice, such knowledge cannot be converted into real capability.

Practice is the mandatory pathway for internalization. Only through immersion, verification and Practical Calibration within personal practice can externally introduced analytical frameworks truly transform into intrinsic cognitive modes belonging to the individual.

The fundamental stance for cognitive growth: **humbly embrace predecessors’ systems, and prudently verify against real-world facts**. Predecessors’ approaches must never be treated as absolute standard answers; they must be examined and revised within real contexts. Draw mature reasoning paths from predecessors’ guidance, and achieve genuine cognitive internalization through personal practice.

## III. Three Categories of Cognitive Heritage

Cognitive heritage handed down by predecessors can be classified into three major categories based on its characteristics. This chapter defines each category, clarifies its positioning and corresponding principles, without elaborating specific operational techniques.

All three types of external inherited materials must be verified and filtered through the General Methods introduced in Chapter IV before internalization by the cognitive agent.

**Category 1: General Methods (General Layer)**
A set of underlying universal cognitive heritage refined and shared by predecessors.

Positioning: It belongs to the method layer and acts as the underlying pillar for the full set of cognitive methods. It is a cross-industry and cross-scenario analytical framework, not bound to any specific object. General Methods cannot be acquired ready-made; they require polishing and eventual internalization into personal thinking methods.

Guiding principle: It requires intensive learning and repeated training, serving as the most fundamental raw material for refining personal cognitive methods.

**Category 2: Domain Knowledge (Domain Layer)**
A complete body of heritage formed over thousands of years of human accumulation. Predecessors used proven methods to build models for specific subjects.

Positioning: It holds scientific value, representing nested architectures, causal logic and **predecessors’ domain-exploration points** for a given domain. General Methods alone are insufficient. Abandoning long-accumulated Domain Knowledge and exploring everything from scratch will drastically slow cognitive progress.

Guiding principle: Learn and inherit with priority while maintaining critical scrutiny. Leverage predecessors’ achievements to boost cognitive efficiency.

**Category 3: Case-Specific Empirical Experience (Case Layer)**
Empirical real-world materials.

Positioning: Fragmented minor experiences and real causal pathways generated under specific conditions.

Guiding principle: No systematic learning required; kept only for reference and must not be generalized or applied directly.

## IV. General Methods (General Layer)

### 4.1 Definition

General Methods are broadly applicable underlying analytical tools unrestricted by specific domains, serving as practical pathways for driver investigation.

Core proposition: Where there is a phenomenon, there exists a driving cause, and the origin can be traced backwards along causal chains.

### 4.2 Learning and Application of General Methods

The application of General Methods follows a three-stage progressive workflow: select observation dimensions, adopt corresponding investigation tools, and finally conduct **Practical Calibration**.

**Stage 1: Demarcate observation dimensions (define directions of observation)**
Driver investigation is split into two major directions: internal factors and external factors.

- **Internal-factor direction**: Focus on the object itself. Examine its inherent structure, intrinsic properties, internal operating states, interactions among internal elements, and inherent conditions and constraints of the object.
- **External-factor direction**: Focus on the external nested system. Every entity is embedded within a higher-order construct; its behaviours and states are constrained and shaped by the overarching whole.

**Stage 2: Select investigation tools (define concrete approaches)**
Matching investigation tools are deployed under the internal and external factor dimensions.

Internal-factor investigation includes two approaches: Direct Decomposition and Reference Mapping.

- **Direct Decomposition**: Dismantle the research object and observe its internal composition to understand the whole. Example: Cut open a melon and directly inspect the state of pulp, seeds and rind.
- **Reference Mapping**: When the target object cannot be dismantled or directly observed internally due to environmental, technical, ethical or other objective constraints, select similar, dissectable counterparts for analysis, then map the derived cognitive conclusions back onto the original inaccessible object. Example: Ancient people dissected animals to infer the internal structure of the human body.

External-factor investigation:

- **Relational Positioning**: First clarify the subordinate constitutive relationship between the object and its containing whole, then analyse opposing, contradictory or synergistic parallel relationships between peer entities within the system, so as to define the object’s position, role and constraint boundaries.

**Stage 3: Practical Calibration (pin down exclusive individual landmarks)**
Practical Calibration is carried out after dimension demarcation and field investigation.

The exploration directions and investigation tools in the first two stages are public methods learnable from external sources. In contrast, landmarks generated through calibration cannot be copied directly from others; they must be generated endogenously through personal practice.

Complex reality resembles a vast ocean or primeval forest. Practical Calibration delivers two core values:

1. Prevent disorientation. When exploring unknown scenarios, investigation without calibration leaves no stable anchor points. One cannot distinguish explored from unexplored zones and tends to circle repeatedly in the same area.
2. Enable rapid advancement. Landmarks preserved through calibration allow reuse of validated pathways when similar problems arise later. There is no need for full exploration from scratch; existing landmarks support fast progress and reduce redundant trial and error.

The specific techniques for building valid landmarks vary across domains and require continuous refinement through repeated practice within each domain. Practical Calibration is an indispensable practical link in the closed loop of General Methods.

### 4.3 Boundaries of Applicability for General Methods

General Methods do not deliver ready-made conclusions. They only provide tracing logic and underlying analytical instruments, compatible with **Practical Calibration points** for tackling unfamiliar scenarios. Independent of domain-specific knowledge, they contain no factual conclusions within any domain and must be combined with corresponding Domain Knowledge to complete full judgement and analysis.

## V. Domain Knowledge (Domain Layer)

### 5.1 Definition and Nature of Domain Knowledge

Domain Knowledge is a knowledge system accumulated through a complete closed-loop research process of observation, dissection, definition and verification conducted by predecessors for research subjects within a specific domain.

Generally speaking, Domain Knowledge carries hierarchical relationships. Fundamentally, all Domain Knowledge is built upon modelling. Models establish structural divisions from whole to parts and clarify relational connections among components within the system.

Knowledge models constructed vary greatly across different domains, or within the same domain at different developmental stages, with differing hierarchical structures and system scales. Mature domains possess extremely large, highly branched knowledge systems. For example, modern medicine has evolved countless specialties and subfields; a single cognitive agent cannot exhaust all details. Emerging domains, by contrast, remain in early stages with simple knowledge architectures and limited system scale. Most mature domains have undergone systematic dissection and organisation by predecessors to form complete system architectures featuring multi-layer nesting and subdomain differentiation.

### 5.2 Learning and Application of Domain Knowledge

The learning, research and application of Domain Knowledge follows a two-stage standard progressive workflow, which cannot be reordered.

**Stage 1: Establish global perspective and self-orientation (fundamental prerequisite)**
For learning and application in any domain, two foundational tasks must first be completed:

1. **Establish a global perspective**: Understand the complete architecture of the domain through learning and clarify relationships among its components.
2. **Set self-orientation**: On this basis, recognise one’s own position and define the direction of advancement — **know where you reside, know where you head**.

This stage focuses on positioning and anchoring the direction of inquiry, without diving into fine details. **This step is vital yet often overlooked.**

**Stage 2: Dual-path in-depth exploration and cognitive breakthrough (advanced extension)**
After establishing a global perspective and self-orientation, one enters a phase of alternating learning and practice focused on the object-of-concern. Extended research unfolds along two paths:

- **Upward Tracing**: Ascend to higher hierarchical levels of the system containing the object-of-concern, and study the rule constraints, logical transmission and boundary limits imposed by the upper architecture upon the object-of-concern. Existing upper architectures are not fully complete. Upward Tracing constitutes the core pathway for discovering system loopholes and architectural defects to achieve top-level cognitive breakthroughs.
- **Downward Deep-Dive**: Descend to lower sub-levels of the object-of-concern, dismantle its internal constituent elements, and explore the structural relationships, operating mechanisms and interaction logic of sub-components.

> 
> Upward Tracing and Downward Deep-Dive are essentially the concrete implementation of General Methods within professional domain contexts. Both the practical application of Domain Knowledge and cognitive expansion rely on this pathway.

In summary, Stage One builds the global domain perspective and completes self-orientation to identify one’s position and direction of advancement. Stage Two involves in-depth learning and practical application targeting the object-of-concern. Upward Tracing and Downward Deep-Dive support expanded research exploration. With sufficient practical accumulation, one may supplement, revise or even partially reconstruct the original architecture. Based on the macro framework, continue investigation using Direct Decomposition, Reference Mapping and Relational Positioning, carry out gradual calibration, and develop domain-specific causal paradigms and standardised exploration landmarks.

### 5.2 Two Sources of Nested Domain Architectures

Nested hierarchical domain architectures have only two modes of generation:
First, Inherited Acquisition: directly adopt and learn mature, validated domain architectures systematically organised by predecessors.
Second, Autonomous Construction: leverage General Methods to conduct closed-loop research of observation, dissection, definition and verification for entirely new real-world objects, inducing and building custom nested domain architectures from scratch.

### 5.4 Boundaries of Applicability for Domain Knowledge

Nested domain architectures are man-made analytical models, only valid under defined conditions of their corresponding domain and era; they are not absolute objective reality. Once the upper-level environment and constraint conditions of the research object shift, rigid adherence to the original domain architecture will directly cause cognitive bias and flawed judgement.

## VI. Conclusion

To perceive the world and build a personal cognitive system, innate endowment is not the core. What matters is mastering effective cognitive methods and completing internalisation and refinement through continuous practice.

The core learning materials for human cognition derive from two foundations: General Methods at the general layer and domain causal paradigms at the domain layer. Various case experiences serve only for reference. General Methods build universal underlying analytical capacity, the foundation for all cognitive investigation. Domain causal paradigms carry long-term accumulated achievements of predecessors and greatly improve the efficiency of cognition and judgement within specific domains.

The two are inseparable and must work in tandem. Separated from General Methods, domain paradigms become rigid stored knowledge incapable of flexible deduction. With only General Methods but no inheritance of mature domain paradigms, all research must start from zero, resulting in extremely high cognitive cost and low efficiency. Meanwhile, case experiences emerge under specific scenarios and constraints, lacking universality and cannot be copied and applied directly.

The complete closed-loop cognitive path: take General Methods as the underlying foundation, achieve System Elevation by inheriting mature domain paradigms, solidify personal landmarks via **Practical Calibration**, repeatedly validate and revise through continuous practice and empirical results, retain case materials for reference, and gradually internalise, iterate and refine to form a mature, self-renewable personal cognitive system.

In summary, the formula for generating a complete personal cognitive system is expressed as:
**External inherited materials + processing by General Methods + individual Practical Calibration points = complete personal cognitive system**


>The model of **inheritance‑practice‑development, iterating in cycles** presented in this paper is an independently formed epistemological perspective. Many epistemological discourses focus on the macro cognitive movement of humanity as a whole. This model shifts to real‑world processes of individuals and generations: cognitive agents do not start practice from a blank state. They always carry pre‑existing cognitive outcomes into reality. After verification and expansion through practice, renewed cognition is generated and passed on as the foundation for subsequent iterations.
