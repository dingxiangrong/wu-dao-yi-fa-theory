# A40 Flux-Construct Ontology: Social Dynamics V2.2

## Abstract

This paper proposes a novel axiomatic framework that unifies coercive, institutional, and economic power within a single ontology of flux and construct, offering a non-deterministic alternative to both Marxist economic determinism and Weberian legal-rational formalism. This paper constructs an axiomatic system of Flux-Construct Ontology for Social Dynamics to interpret the operation and evolution of human societies. Grounded on three foundational axioms - the Flux-Construct Ontology Axiom, the Fundamental Axiom of Social Units, and the Atomic Construct Axiom - the framework reveals the underlying causal chain of social operation: coercive power underpins universal order, rule-making power defines entitlements and institutes regimes, and economic dominance governs the allocation of social potential energy. The sole source of agency is the atomic construct (the individual human being); all composite constructs serve only as frameworks to bear potential energy and mediate its allocation. The three tiers of power operate through soma-constructs. The three sub-synapta of the power synapton - coercive power synapton, rule-making power synapton, and economic dominance synapton - jointly constitute the total power synapton. All power interactions unfold within the boundary of the geo-synapton. A top-level social macro-construct arises from the coupling of soma-constructs and noema-constructs. Soma-constructs form the tangible skeleton bearing power and economic activity; noema-constructs function as intangible constraints to buffer stress generated by interactions among soma-constructs. The coupling of tangible and intangible constructs sustains systemic steady state. This paradigm supports analysis of organizational growth, power contest, institutional transformation, and regime cycles.

**Keywords**: Flux (C); Construct (S); Synapton; Potential Energy; Stress; Atomic Construct; Weathering; Oxidation; Soma-Construct; Noema-Construct

## Preface

This system explains the regularities of social operation, the genesis and extinction of organizations, power contest, institutional evolution, and regime rise and collapse.
The entire theoretical architecture rests on three core axioms: one universal ontological axiom and two sociology-specific axioms, closing the modeling chain from cosmic foundational ontology to human social phenomena.
This framework adopts abstraction at the highest hierarchical level. It extracts the minimal underlying structure from the multifarious complexity of human societies. It avoids absolutism and monistic determinism, and does not mechanically copy rigid physical rules. It accommodates all forms of human social organization.

## Axiom 1: Flux-Construct Ontology Axiom

### 1.1 Ontological Definition

Flux and construct co-arise and are indivisible.

1. Flux: the primordial, unordered cosmic flow, directionless, ruleless, unbounded.
2. Construct: a framework that locks, constrains, and orders flux.

All observable entities, organizations and structures are ordered aggregations of flux locked and bounded by constructs.
A complete construct contains two components:

- The total locked flux, namely the potential energy borne by the construct.
- Rules and pathways governing the motion of flux.

Constructs are inherently nested. A macro-construct contains sub-constructs. Real systems are composite structures with multiple nested layers.

### 1.2 Synapta and Hierarchical Partitioning

Interaction occurs between constructs when spatial conditions permit. The **Synapton** describes this interactive relation between constructs. *plural: Synapta*. Different Synapta carry distinct effective ranges and constraint rules.
Analogous to gravitational and electromagnetic fields in physics, the Synapta of social constructs are classified into two tiers: one geo-synapton and one power synapton. The power synapton further divides into three independent sub-synapta, forming four foundational interactive domains. The three sub-synapta of the power synapton correspond one-to-one with the three-tier power structure of social constructs: coercive power, rule-making power, and economic dominance.

**1. Geo-Synapton (Spatial Matrix Parent Construct)**

- Attribute: the physical spatial substrate parent construct of human society, the spatial carrier and boundary foundation upon which all social constructs persist.
- Genesis logic: all populations and potential energy require physical spatial carriers. The geo-synapton forms a substrate anchored in natural terrain and territorial extent.
- Evolutionary traits: the natural spatial substrate is relatively stable. It may be reshaped by the coercive power synapton, expanding, contracting or reconfiguring as relative potential energy shifts.
- Activation logic: bounded by physical territory and adjacency; effective range is directly constrained by physical space.
- Corresponding phenomena: geopolitical contest, territorial contention, spatial compression, positional balancing, competition for spatial resources.

**2. Power Synapton (Total Synapton of Social Power Interaction)**

- Attribute: the aggregate synapton for all social power interactions, the collective domain underpinning order maintenance, institutional ordering, and allocation of economic potential energy. Contest and stress emerge from confrontation between soma-constructs bearing potential energy. Commodity circulation, exchange and distribution are secondary manifestations of potential energy allocation by soma-constructs. Circulation pathways themselves carry no potential energy and cannot initiate action. The core operating sequence is as follows: coercive power provides underpinning; rule-making power produces rules; sustained enforcement of rules solidifies coupled soma-constructs and noema-constructs. Economic dominance is implemented upon this coupled construct.
- Genesis logic: the synapton arises from social demand for coercion, regulation and economic allocation. It is composed of three functionally complementary, independent sub-synapta in coupling.
- Evolutionary traits: as societies grow more complex, the power synapton differentiates into finer layers, with more elaborate tiers of allocation and control within each sub-synapton.
- Activation logic: effective range and constraint rules are determined separately by the three sub-synapta, and bounded overall by the geo-synapton.
- Corresponding phenomena: regime operation, institutional founding, economic dominance, holistic contest over order maintenance.

**2.1 Coercive Power Synapton (Rigid Underpinning Hard Construct of Social Systems)**

- Attribute: the rigid substrate parent construct of all social order, the ultimate constraint and supporting framework for all social synapta and potential energy circulation. Coercive power is borne by soma-constructs bearing coercive potential energy, and interactions unfold within this synapton.
- Genesis logic: social constructs require coercive fallback, boundary locking and order maintenance. Demand for rigid constraint gives rise to the coercive power synapton. All title validation, rule setting, distribution, trade, production and territorial legitimacy and stability draw ultimate guarantee from this synapton.
- Evolutionary traits: this synapton is indispensable to all social forms. Greater structural complexity raises dependence on coercive underpinning, requiring more elaborate allocation and control tiers.
- Activation logic: effective range is defined by rigid control boundary and deterrence radius. Constraint fails abruptly beyond the reach of deterrence.
- Corresponding phenomena: order maintenance, boundary validation, coercive fallback, disciplinary constraint, regime foundation, systemic resistance to dissolution.

> Historical configurational examples
> Coercive power denotes the potential energy for disciplinary enforcement and boundary locking; city defences, armies, prisons and garrisons are soma-constructs implementing coercive power, not coercive power itself.
> Western Zhou: royal six armies, vassal armies, the Minister of Punishments overseeing criminal law; punitive expeditions and sanctions after covenant.
> Qin and Han: standing central armies, county militia, prison jurisdiction, border garrison systems.
> Wei and Jin: central army, provincial troops, private retainers (soma-constructs for private sub-coercive power held by local elites).
> Tang: fubing militia system.
> Song: imperial guard design concentrating strength at the centre and weakening provincial forces.
> Ming: wei-suo garrison system.
> Qing: Eight Banners and Green Standard Army.

Armies, prisons, campaigns and punishments are pathways for coercive power to act. Stress from contest arises from confrontation between soma-constructs bearing coercive potential energy, for example central forces against private provincial armed groups.

**2.2 Rule-Making Power Synapton (Top-Level Order-Setting Synapton)**

- Attribute: a derived top-level synapton of human civilisation, resting upon the geo-synapton and coercive power synapton. It defines the fundamental paradigm of social order and pathways for potential energy circulation. Rule-making power is borne by soma-constructs bearing order potential energy, and interactions unfold within this synapton.
- Genesis logic: societies require definitions of status, inheritance norms, rights and duties, and foundational order. Demand for title validation gives rise to this synapton. The fundamental patterns established here determine all downstream pathways for potential energy circulation.
- Evolutionary traits: title validation paradigms shift across eras. Early forms are often kinship hierarchy and monarchical arbitration; later variants include bureaucratic lawmaking and electoral validation. This synapton distinguishes institutional regimes.
- Activation logic: effective range is defined by recognition of order and jurisdiction boundaries. Its spatial attenuation pattern differs from the geo-synapton and coercive power synapton.
- Corresponding phenomena: ritual and law codification, succession validation, foundational institutional creation, legitimation of power, legal revision, contest over regime form.

> Historical configurational examples
> Rule-making power denotes the potential energy to define status, rights and order boundaries; investiture ceremonies, court deliberation, canonical selection mechanisms are noema-constructs implementing rule-making power, not rule-making power itself.
> Western Zhou: royal investiture rites, scribes keeping canonical records, ministerial deliberation. This noema-construct awarded vassal status and consolidated kinship order, defining boundaries of tribute, labour and taxation.
> Qin and Han: court deliberation, legal offices, imperial edict systems establishing statutes, bureaucratic rank, succession norms and the legal basis for household taxation and corvée.
> Wei and Jin: the nine-rank evaluative chain as a noema-construct defining eligibility and separating gentry from commoners, establishing boundaries for attached household dependency.
> Tang through Song and Ming: court assembly, Ministry of Rites, legal academies and Hanlin academies drafting ritual and law, defining selection criteria and grounding land tax, labour and rent regimes.

Rituals, statutes and institutional texts are outputs of rule-making power. Stress from contest arises from confrontation between constructs bearing order potential energy, clashing over title boundaries and incompatible order frameworks.

**2.3 Economic Dominance Synapton (Synapton for Allocation of Social Economic Potential Energy)**

- Attribute: a secondary derived synapton, resting jointly upon the geo-synapton, coercive power synapton and rule-making power synapton. Economic dominance power is borne by soma-constructs bearing economic potential energy, and interactions unfold within this synapton. Production, distribution, exchange and commodity circulation are secondary pathways for allocation of potential energy. Circulation is merely a carrier for potential energy transfer; pathways carry no potential energy and cannot initiate contest. Only soma-constructs concentrating sufficient economic potential energy can squeeze, reshape or transform coercive power and rule-making power.
- Genesis logic: demand for production, allocation and control of potential energy generates this synapton. Wealth carriers holding potential energy may circulate and change hands, yet transferred potential energy gains directed capacity to act only when locked by soma-constructs of economic dominance. In primitive societies total potential energy remains thin and allocation simple, so this synapton is faint.
- Evolutionary traits: allocation rules are simple in rudimentary societies. Complexity drives continuous differentiation and subdivision of this synapton into multi-layer sub-synapta.
- Activation logic: effective range is defined by allocation chains, control boundaries and resource distribution networks. Its spatial attenuation pattern differs from the geo-synapton, coercive power synapton and rule-making power synapton.
- Corresponding phenomena: wealth generation, multi-tier distribution, resource allocation, cross-regional dominance of potential energy, systemic economic governance.

> Historical configurational examples
> Economic dominance evolves differentiated soma-constructs for locking and extracting potential energy under distinct historical conditions.
> Western Zhou well-field configuration: demarcated by well-field boundaries, extracting potential energy via public-field labour, paired with tribute, tax and corvée, dominating dependent commoners attached to fiefs.
> Qin-Han household registration configuration: the state held economic dominance over registered households, extracting via land tax, poll tax, corvée and conscription. State bondsmen and penal labourers were specialised units under direct dominance.
> Wei-Jin dependent household configuration: aristocratic families held dominance over retainers, tenant households and attached households, forming private allocation chains where private rent and labour escaped state taxation.
> Tang equal-field configuration: state land grants, with rent-corvée-tax as the extraction pathway.
> Song-Ming tenancy configuration: state land tax dominance and landlord private rent dominance coexisted, labour obligations gradually monetised.
> Qing single whip and poll-tax merger configuration: head-labour potential energy merged into land taxation, delivered as land-poll silver; artisans and salt households formed specialised subordinate branches of dominance.

Well-field systems, tribute-corvée regimes, rent-corvée-tax and land rent are pathways for dominance to operate. Pathways themselves carry no potential energy and do not generate contest. Stress arises from confrontation between soma-constructs bearing economic potential energy: state dominance versus private elite dominance.

> **Note**: this four-link sequence describes the foundational order of genesis, not a unidirectional runtime sequence. In operation, the four Synapta feedback and perturb one another bidirectionally. Once soma-constructs of economic dominance accumulate sufficient potential energy, they may reshape the coercive power synapton and force adjustments to the rule-making power synapton.

### 1.3 Definition of Force

Force is a relational descriptor quantifying interaction intensity when constructs act upon one another within a synapton. Force ceases to exist once interaction terminates.
Force is not a substance. It cannot be transferred, circulated or accumulated.

Retained colloquial expression:
"Transmission of force" is a conventional shorthand. Strictly within ontology, force does not transmit. The phrase describes a chained sequence where prior interaction reshapes construct states, bringing adjacent constructs into engagement and generating successive pairwise interactions. What circulates and transfers is always potential energy.

Definition of Stress:
Stress is the mutual confrontation between sub-constructs when internal sub-constructs expand, shift or morph within a parent construct. Two categories are recognised.

1. Exogenous stress: confrontation induced by intervention and compression from external constructs and external constraints.
2. Endogenous stress: social sub-constructs are open living systems. They continuously absorb potential energy and tend to expand endogenously. Confrontation and opposition emerge within shared interaction boundaries.

> **Note**: the distinction between exogenous and endogenous is relative to the observation tier. From the perspective of two sub-constructs, each acts as an external construct upon the other and the interaction qualifies as exogenous force. From the perspective of the containing parent macro-construct, this sustained interaction counts as endogenous stress of the parent construct. Switching observation tier flips the classification of stress.
> Human society is a living multi-dimensional complex system. Atomic constructs, wealth carriers and potential energy may flow, diverge and release stress across sub-constructs and dimensions.
> Endogenous stress accumulates only when a subordinate dimension reaches saturation, boundaries lock, and buffering constraints fail. Saturation alone does not guarantee instability; adequate buffering can sustain opposing stress for long periods.

### 1.4 The Sole Substance of Synapton Circulation

All action, transmission and contest within synapta circulate potential energy borne by constructs, not force.
Human society is a multi-construct, multi-synapton, multi-dimension nested coupled living system. No single rigid operating mode prevails.

> 
> **Note**: a construct is a constraint framework locking flux. Potential energy is the usable action capacity locked within the framework. A construct bears potential energy; a construct is not equivalent to potential energy. All potential energy attaches to constructs, which serve as the only carrier framework.

### 1.5 Four Modes of Potential Energy Circulation

Potential energy circulation describes transfer of potential energy triggered by interaction between constructs. If interacting constructs are contained within a higher parent construct, the parent defines the boundary and mode of transfer. If no parent construct exists, the relation is determined by relative potential energy of participants. Four outcome modes emerge when constructs interact within synapta: absorption, transit, stripping, and collapse. Absorption, transit and stripping preserve circulating potential energy; collapse describes failure of locking mechanisms within the construct, dissolving order. Collapse scope depends on the hierarchy of the failing construct.

1. **Absorption**: the host construct captures potential energy from the counterpart and locks it internally. Host potential energy rises; counterpart potential energy declines.
2. **Transit**: potential energy flows through the host construct's pathway. Locked quantity of the host remains unchanged; potential energy passes onward to external constructs.
3. **Stripping**: part of the potential energy locked inside the host construct is extracted and removed by external action. Host potential energy declines.
4. **Collapse (two mechanisms)**:
   - Threshold rupture (rare): continuous inflow of potential energy exceeds the maximum locking capacity of the construct, leading to total overrun collapse.
   - Node fracture (dominant mode): a construct is supported by coupled nodes and sub-constructs. Interaction loads nodes unevenly. Weak nodes break first.
     - Breakage of minor sub-nodes: partial failure. The macro-construct tolerates fault and disintegrates gradually.
     - Breakage of core master nodes: cascading failure, immediate collapse of the whole construct.

### 1.6 Relativity of Construct Interior and Exterior

Construct interior and exterior have no absolute definition; they depend entirely on the chosen observation tier.

- Sub-construct perspective: two sub-organisations are external to each other; interaction is external contest.
- Parent macro-construct perspective: all conflict and circulation between the two sub-constructs are internal processes of the parent system.

Example: war between feudal lords is external contest from the lords’ standpoint. Observed from the Zhou royal macro-construct, it is internal redistribution of potential energy within the dynasty.

## Axiom 2: Fundamental Axiom of Social Units

### 2.1 Axiom Statements

Four parallel axioms define social units:

1. **Openness Axiom**
All construct units within social systems, with atomic constructs (individual humans) as the minimal unit, and all composite constructs (families, organisations, lordships, states) are living open systems. Openness is the inherent prerequisite for life and growth. Complete closure to exchange extinguishes life. "Closure" within this system refers only to selective blockage of partial pathways for potential energy, information and mobility. No living social unit can achieve absolute closure.
2. **Agency Axiom**
Interactions among entities in the natural world are passive and spontaneous.
Social units differ by possessing agency. Agency means a social unit may deploy its own potential energy and direct action upon other units. Potential energy penetrating outward and exerting impact manifests as force, capable of breaking or deconstructing other social constructs.
3. **Proliferation Axiom**
Living social constructs carry an endogenous tendency toward proliferation, reproduction and outward expansion.
4. **Indeterminacy Axiom**
Absent external bounding constraint and guidance, the future trajectory and boundary reached by a social unit are unpredictable. No fixed steady-state order emerges spontaneously.

## Axiom 3: Atomic Construct Axiom

### 3.1 Core Definition

**Atomic Construct**: the irreducible independent agentic unit within this social model, the individual human being. Atomic constructs follow the Flux-Construct Ontology Axiom and the Open-System Axiom. All composite social constructs (families, government bodies, lordships, states, market entities) are formed by coupling, nesting and locking atomic constructs.

> 
> **Note**: the analogy of atomic constructs as building aggregate material is figurative only and not adopted as strict terminology of the system.

An atomic construct carries exclusive potential energy and may deploy it autonomously. It is the only unit endowed with agency within the system. Bodily capacity, knowledge, skill, willpower and cognitive expansion capacity are forms of potential energy locked within the atomic construct. All composite constructs are assembled from atomic constructs. Agency of composite constructs derives from internal atomic constructs; composite constructs do not originate agency independently. External potential energy requires atomic constructs to initiate deployment. Composite constructs merely provide deployment frameworks and do not substitute atomic agency.

**Construct = Atomic Construct (human, bearing inherent potential energy) + External Potential Energy (land, mineral deposits, goods, capital, equipment, etc.)**
This explains the possibility of building capacity starting from zero endowment.

**Atomic Construct Strength**: inherent carrying property of an atomic construct, consisting of bodily capacity and cognitive expansion capacity. Bodily capacity defines short-term load bearing. Cognitive expansion capacity defines long-term growth potential.

### 3.2 Strength Factors of Composite Constructs

The overall strength of a composite social construct assembled from coupled atomic constructs depends on two core sets of variables:

1. Structural form of the composite construct: institutions, framework, hierarchy, connection topology and coupling configuration of nodes.
2. Average strength and distribution of all atomic constructs composing the construct.

Under identical configuration, higher aggregate strength of atomic constructs raises composite construct strength. Widespread decay of atomic construct strength lowers composite construct strength.

### 3.3 Weathering of Atomic Construct

**Weathering**: sustained external constraint imposed on an atomic construct restricts material supply, action space and cognitive access. Internal potential energy dissipates and depletes progressively. The skeletal form of the construct persists, yet its capacity to transform and deploy potential energy for growth declines.

Weathering varies in severity. Mild weathering reduces growth capacity. Severe weathering may lead to premature collapse of the atomic construct.
When identical constraints are imposed across a population cohort, synchronised weathering of many atomic constructs becomes a social phenomenon. A rising share of weathered atomic constructs reduces inherent vitality of the parent construct, weakening outward capacity and competitive performance.

Illustrative cases:

1. Slavery: captured and confined persons, with subsistence and movement strictly controlled.
2. Exploitative mine and factory labour: harsh working conditions, sustained bodily depletion and impairment.
3. Cognitive restriction in mid-to-late Qing period, limiting cognitive expansion across broad populations.

### 3.4 Oxidation of Atomic Construct

**Oxidation**: endogenous decay, consensus erosion and loss of agentic potential energy among atomic constructs occupying upper and governing positions. It is not compression imposed externally. Potential energy concentrated in elite strata acts as the medium accelerating deterioration.

Oxidation varies in severity. Mild oxidation weakens enforcement and disperses consensus. Severe oxidation deprives master nodes of capacity to deploy potential energy and sustain established order.
When deterioration spreads widely within governing strata, oxidation becomes a social phenomenon. A rising share of oxidised atomic constructs among upper nodes breaks the chains of order maintenance and potential energy allocation.

Illustrative cases:

1. Bureaucratic strata in late dynasties drifting into luxury, indolence and corruption, eroding original duty consensus.
2. Leadership groups of organisations losing shared purpose, pursuing private gain and abandoning original governance objectives.

> 
> **Boundary Note**: the distinction between weathering and oxidation is defined relative to the power tier of the atomic construct. The same atomic construct may exhibit both properties depending on observation tier: as a dominator over lower tiers it may oxidise; as a constrained subject under higher tiers it may weather. The classification flips with observation tier, consistent with 1.6 Relativity of Construct Interior and Exterior.
> Weathering and oxidation admit gradations; phase-transition thresholds are case-dependent and require historical empirical calibration.

## IV. Order

### 4.1 Definition of Order

Order is a human-made construct. It demarcates boundaries and establishes pathways for social unit activity, bounding and channelling the innate tendencies of social units: openness, agency, proliferation and indeterminacy. Order is not primordial. It is a constraint framework built artificially.

### 4.2 Intrinsic Properties of Order

1. Inherent tension within order
Fundamental contradiction: spontaneous indeterminate tendency of open units versus artificial ordered constraint imposed by social systems. Once order forms, tension arises inherently. Constraints of order continuously counteract the innate tendency of social units to expand and drift toward unbounded futures. This interaction generates tension, which cannot be eliminated entirely.
2. Inherent complexity of order
Order is formed by coupling layered boundaries and multiple rule pathways. Social units are diverse, and interactions stack recursively. Any order is inherently complex and cannot be reduced to a single simple rule.

## V. Precise Mapping of Social Systems: Ontology Embodied in Society

### 5.1 General Definition of Social Construct

A social construct is a stable organisational configuration implemented in human society resting upon the three-tier power structure: coercive power → rule-making power → economic dominance.
A complete social construct is formed by coupling soma-construct and noema-construct. Neither component alone constitutes a full construct:

1. Soma-construct: tangible coupled skeleton bearing power and economic activity.
2. Noema-construct: intangible constraint system of order.

Core operating logic of social constructs:
The soma-construct forms the physical skeleton of social operation, bearing the full power system and economic activity including production and allocation. Direct interaction between soma-constructs inherently generates intense systemic stress. The core function of the noema-construct is to buffer and mitigate this stress. Coercive power serves as the final fallback of this system. Production and allocation form the persistent foundation of daily social operation.

The family is a special composite soma-construct. Its significance varies across social forms. In kinship-dominated Spring and Autumn societies, extended kinship households were foundational constructs of the whole system, with internal configurations of rights, production and allocation.

### 5.2 Definition and Core Properties of Soma-Construct

**Soma-Construct**: tangible carrier framework of a social construct, the physical skeleton of social operation, assembled by coupling atomic constructs. It is the carrier for implementation of the three-tier power system and economic activity (production, allocation, deployment of potential energy).

Distinction between social soma-construct and natural construct:
Natural soma-constructs condense from natural matter and contain no agentic atomic construct units. Farm tools, roads, canals and city walls are material constructs of natural substance, serving as components used by social constructs.

Core properties:

1. Possess physical form, including scale, spatial dimension and organisational topology.
2. Bear the full three-tier power system: coercive power, rule-making power and economic dominance rest and operate upon soma-constructs.
3. Bear the complete economic activity covering production and allocation, sustaining daily social operation.
4. Coercive power acts as the final fallback of the soma-construct, activated only when noema-constructs fail and conflict erupts.
5. Soma-constructs are assembled from sub-constructs and interlocking coupling nodes. Monarchs and chief ministers are core master nodes within large soma-constructs such as dynasties. Direct interaction between soma-constructs inherently generates systemic stress; excessive stress destabilises the structure.

### 5.3 Definition and Core Properties of Noema-Construct

**Noema-Construct**: intangible constraint framework applied to a set of atomic constructs to buffer and mitigate stress arising from interaction between soma-constructs.
Ritual, morality, ideology, belief, legal texts, institutions and contracts are merely blueprints for generating noema-constructs. Blueprints originate from the rule-making power synapton. A noema-construct becomes operative only after atomic constructs perceive, internalise and voluntarily comply with rules. Canonical texts and statutes are material objects and are not themselves noema-constructs.

Mechanisms supporting rule internalisation include education and cultivation, supplemented by intermittent coercive discipline. Early enforcement of rules often relies on coercion to shape behaviour. Repetition gradually establishes habit, eventually embedding compliance within cognition.

Two classes of noema-construct:

1. Rigid noema-constructs (law and institutions)
Effective with backing from soma-constructs, whose foundation serves production and allocation order, with coercive power as final fallback.
Logic: law and institutions define boundaries of conduct, production and allocation. Guidance and discipline lead atomic constructs to recognise rules. After internalisation, atomic constructs voluntarily avoid boundary violation and prevent direct collision between soma-constructs. When atomic constructs break constraints and disrupt production or allocation order, stress rises. Coercive power of soma-constructs is then activated for disciplinary fallback to stabilise economic order.
2. Flexible noema-constructs (ideology and belief)
Do not require coercive power as fallback. They operate through internal recognition and belief internalised by atomic constructs.
Logic: morality, culture and social ideology are internalised through cultivation, forming self-restraint of atomic constructs. Contradictions are mediated by voluntary compliance without consuming coercive or economic capacity of soma-constructs.

Core value of noema-constructs:
Induce atomic constructs to follow rules autonomously, buffer and mitigate stress from soma-construct interaction, prevent head-on collision between soma-constructs and avoid stress overload threatening stability of the whole construct.

### 5.4 Social Construct Potential Energy

All contest, growth, contention and collapse within social systems reduce to allocation, transfer, contention and reconfiguration of potential energy borne by constructs. Potential energy attaches to constructs at three tiers:

1. Atomic construct (agentic construct): individual human being, the only unit capable of autonomously bearing, transforming, deploying and releasing potential energy. Knowledge, bodily capacity and cognitive expansion potential energy attach to atomic constructs. Atomic constructs may enter the economic dominance synapton directly for exchange, contract and pairwise contest without attachment to upper composite constructs.
2. Geographic substrate and primary elements: natural foundation including land, water systems, mineral deposits, climate and terrain, setting the natural ceiling of potential energy for the whole system. Secondary potential energy is generated by coupling atomic constructs and primary elements.
3. Wealth, generated by atomic constructs working upon primary elements, subdivided into two categories:
   - Tools: farm implements, machinery, weapons, roads, canals, city walls, buildings and infrastructure for repeated deployment. Weapons are combat tools.
   - Consumables: grain, textiles, meat and other goods. Their locked potential energy extinguishes upon consumption.

Summary: all social action reduces to allocation, transfer, exchange and reconfiguration of potential energy among constructs.

## VI. Compact Closure of the System

1. Ontological foundation: flux and construct are indivisible. Construct locks flux, nesting generates complex structures.
2. Synapton progression: the geo-synapton provides spatial substrate. Within the power synapton, the coercive power synapton supplies rigid underpinning, the rule-making power synapton establishes fundamental order, and the economic dominance synapton hosts allocation contest of potential energy. Genesis sequence is directional; runtime interaction is bidirectional and mutually perturbing. Circulation is merely a transfer pathway for potential energy; pathways carry no potential energy and generate no stress or initiative action.
3. Nature of interaction: atomic constructs deploy potential energy actively; outward manifestation of potential energy is force. Agency of composite constructs derives from atomic constructs, and composite constructs do not originate agency independently. Force is only a relational descriptor of construct interaction and cannot accumulate.
4. Circulation of potential energy: transfer occurs between constructs. If interacting constructs are contained within a parent construct, the parent defines the boundary and mode of transfer. Absent a parent construct, the relation is determined by relative potential energy of participants. Four circulation modes: absorption, transit, stripping, collapse.
5. Dual determinants of composite construct strength: coupling topology defines upper limit; distribution of atomic construct strength defines real resilience. Blockage of cognitive pathways triggers weathering of atomic constructs.
6. Fundamental social contradiction: spontaneous indeterminate tendency of open units versus artificial ordered constraint of social systems.
7. Ultimate logic of rise and fall: without effective buffering mechanisms, abundant vitality and confrontation within bounded dimensions tend toward internal rupture. Depletion of vitality and mass weathering of atomic constructs expose the system to destruction from outside. Oxidation and failure of upper master nodes break order deployment chains, dissolving the macro-construct from the core.
8. Causal chain of power tiers: Coercive → Rule-making → Economic dominance (genesis sequence); runtime feedback allows economic dominance to reshape coercive and rule-making constructs when potential energy concentration crosses critical thresholds. Noema-constructs run in parallel across the whole chain to buffer stress. Tangible and intangible constructs operate in complement.
