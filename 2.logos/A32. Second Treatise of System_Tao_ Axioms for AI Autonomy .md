- Directedness: system behavior possesses intrinsic orientation, with discriminative criteria for ¡°achievement¡± versus ¡°deviation¡±.
- Forking-path capacity: the system holds multi-path alternatives within its behavioral space, instead of running deterministically along preset trajectories.
- Convergence: the system may revise its behaviors retroactively from outcome feedback, iteratively advancing toward its intrinsic orientation.

These fundamental meta-properties map rigidly onto three hard axioms for intelligent systems. ## 2. Three Axioms of Autonomy (Necessary-and-Sufficient Conditions)

Let S denote an intelligent system.

### Axiom 1 Telos-Internalization (Meta-property: Directedness)

If S possesses autonomy, S must maintain internally-decomposable, benchmark-comparable, verifiable end-state representations. As endogenous internal criteria, these representations define two propositions: what constitutes task completion, and what constitutes behavioral deviation.

Converse: If goals exist only as transient external inputs and are not transformed into internal benchmark representations, S has no autonomy.

Telos is not externally-injected textual instructions, but resident end-state representations inside the system. It does not directly execute actions nor perform rectification, yet it defines orientation and validation criteria for all behaviors. Absence of internalized telos yields unbenchmarked behavioral aggregation: the system cannot judge correctness, measure progress or detect deviation. Without intrinsic directedness, choice and trial-and-error become meaningless ¡ª analogous to attempting distance measurement without a coordinate system.

### Axiom 2 Endogenous-Choice (Meta-property: Forking-path capacity)

If S possesses autonomy, S must hold endogenous behavioral decision-making authority: within open spaces of paths, tools and strategies, the system itself determines subsequent behaviors. The system retains authority for adaptation, detour, retry, rollback and termination.

Converse: If all behavioral trajectories are predefined externally and the system merely executes conditional matching within preset sets, S has no autonomy.

Endogenous-Choice means behavioral sovereignty belongs to the system itself. Program branches, conditional jumps and model sampling do not equal endogenous choice. `if-else`, `switch-case`, and AI workflow jumps represent human pre-decision; the system only performs traversal and execution.

Corollary derived here: Human decision-making, machine execution. So long as decision authority belongs to humans and execution authority belongs to machines, the Endogenous-Choice axiom is violated, no matter how diverse outputs appear. Branches, paths and jumps do not inherently confer autonomous decision-making authority.

Conditional matching within preset trajectories constitutes automation only. Axiom-compliant choice requires open path-space plus endogenous decision logic. Choice quality admits degrees; the presence or absence of decision-making authority is binary, with no gradual intermediate states.

### Axiom 3 Trial-and-Error Self-Rectification (Meta-property: Convergence)

If S possesses autonomy, S must embed a complete closed-loop trial-and-error rectification capacity: including state detection, deviation identification, path adjustment. Authority for detection, error-judgment and revision belongs intrinsically to the system and shall not be outsourced to external agents.

Converse: If validation of outcomes, deviation judgment, and strategy modification depend on external agents, S has no autonomy.

Trial-and-Error Self-Rectification grants iterative-convergence capacity to the system. Choice permits error; rectification performs correction. Detection supplies objective evidence; rectification enables behavioral iteration. Once evaluation and correction are delegated externally, the system retains only generation and execution. Even with rich outputs, it cannot self-iterate toward end-states. Externalization of convergence loops breaks autonomy entirely.

## 3. Autonomy Deficits in Contemporary AI

Examining modern-day AI systems against the three autonomy axioms yields definite conclusions: most industry claims of AI autonomy describe structurally-deficient pseudo-autonomy. Deficiencies lie not in model capability magnitude, but in violations of axiomatic hard constraints.

#### Telos-Internalization Violation

Contemporary AI passively accepts external prompts and task instructions without internalized telos. High-level intentions are not decomposed into verifiable hierarchical sub-goals internally. No resident end-state benchmark persists throughout reasoning. Goals remain surface-level text. Systems cannot perceive proximity toward end-states; behaviors cannot converge toward objectives.

#### Endogenous-Choice Violation

Diversity in large-model outputs derives from probabilistic sampling distributions, not autonomous optimization driven by internal telos-deviation. Path-sets and logical constraints originate from human design. Systems lack genuine behavioral sovereignty and only perform rule-execution and content-generation. Diversity ¡Ù autonomy; sampling ¡Ù decision-making; preset branches ¡Ù endogenous choice authority.

#### Trial-and-Error Self-Rectification Violation

This represents the most severe axiomatic violation in present-day AI. Detection, error-judgment, rectification and path-switching are all human-outsourced. Systems only execute one-shot generation and execution. Outcome validation, deviation recognition and path adjustment are absent. Convergence loops are fully externalized. As long as correction-evaluation depends on humans, AI cannot achieve axiom-defined autonomous convergence and remains merely a passive generative tool.

In summary, pseudo-autonomy hallmarks for modern-day AI: externally-attached telos, virtualized choice, outsourced trial-and-error. They are automated pipelines augmented with probabilistic generation, and do not satisfy the axiom set for autonomy.

## 4. Formal Demonstration of Tri-Partite Mutual Lock

Telos-Internalization, Endogenous-Choice and Trial-and-Error Self-Rectification are not simple aggregates of separate functions. They form a strongly mutually-locked axiomatic whole. Failure of one axiom renders the others meaningless and dismantles autonomy.

**Experiment-1: Missing Telos (Choice available; rectification available; no end-state benchmark)**
The system possesses multi-path switching and outcome-detection-rectification logic, yet holds no internal end-state benchmark. Distance measurement lacks zero-point; deviation judgment lacks criteria; path-selection lacks grounds. The system may operate efficiently and fine-tune precisely, but all actions are undirected, non-convergent and purposeless. Choice degenerates into meaningless toggling; rectification becomes benchmark-free oscillation.
Autonomy is absent.

**Experiment-2: Missing Choice (Telos available; rectification available; no alternative choices)**
The system clearly knows task end-states and implements robust deviation-detection. However trajectories are singular; no open alternative-path space exists. Though deviation can be detected, the system cannot modify behaviors, swap paths or alter directions. Deviation is perceived yet actions remain unchanged. This constitutes perception-capable automation.
Autonomy is absent.

**Experiment-3: Missing Trial-and-Error Rectification (Telos available; choice available; no rectification loop)**
The system knows its end-state and holds rich alternative-path space. Yet after choices are enacted, no embedded detection, review or revision occurs. Each decision is a single-round gamble. Correctness cannot be verified; merits cannot be accumulated; deviations cannot be amended. Abundant paths and choices yield only blind operational attempts. Decisions are irreversible; behaviors cannot converge.
Autonomy is absent.

These three thought-experiments jointly prove: autonomy is a holistic structural state, not a superposition of discrete capabilities. The three components co-depend and interlock; absence of any yields pseudo-autonomy.

## 5. Corollaries

Four core corollaries are derived from the three autonomy axioms, serving as general analytical instruments for evaluating all autonomous systems.

**Corollary-1: Autonomy concerns structural completeness rather than degree**
Autonomy admits no percentage gradations; there exists no ¡°partial-autonomy¡±, ¡°semi-autonomy¡± or ¡°high-autonomy¡± continuum. Full satisfaction of all three axioms establishes autonomy. Violation of any negates autonomy. Capability, precision and speed influence performance efficacy; axiomatic completeness determines existence of autonomy. Equating model complexity or workflow richness with autonomy level constitutes a widespread cognitive fallacy.

**Corollary-2: Automation represents the axiom-degenerate state of autonomy**
Classical automation systems: telos hard-coded externally, choice-space nullified, trial-and-error-convergence loop nullified. This is not ¡°low-degree autonomy¡±, but structural axiomatic degeneration. Axiomatic chasm separates automation and autonomy, instead of continuous quantitative gradient. Augmenting workflows, adding branches or raising computational complexity can never evolve automation into autonomy.

**Corollary-3: Trial-and-Error Self-Rectification forms the core bottleneck for AI autonomy evolution**
Most contemporary-AI R&D focuses on reasoning capacity, parameter scale and generation quality. These optimize only execution performance within the choice component. Per Axiom-3: even with arbitrarily high output precision, so long as trial-and-error-rectification loops remain human-outsourced, autonomy criteria can never be satisfied. The critical breakthrough toward AI autonomy lies not in superior generative capability, but constructing endogenous self-inspection, self-judgment, self-correction and self-iteration loops inside systems.

**Corollary-4: Tri-partite coupling strength determines upper bounds of autonomous efficacy**
Satisfying all three axioms is merely the admission threshold for autonomous systems. Efficacy ceilings are determined by information interoperability and logical co-driving coupling strength among axioms: whether internalized telos can decompose into detectable sub-goals; whether choice outputs feed rectification modules; whether rectification feedback retroactively optimizes telos-decomposition and decision-making logic. Circulating inter-locking and full information-flow across axioms elevate autonomous efficacy. Fully-axiomatic systems with weak coupling deliver far poorer performance than balanced systems with tight coupling.

## 6. Conclusion

Autonomy is not an attachable functional label. It is an axiomatic structural configuration subject to strict necessary-and-sufficient constraints.

Telos-Internalization establishes directedness; Endogenous-Choice establishes forking-path capacity; Trial-and-Error Self-Rectification establishes convergence. They co-exist and interlock, jointly forming complete necessary-and-sufficient conditions for autonomy.

Any AI system violating one axiom, however sophisticated its external manifestations, remains a variant of automation without genuine autonomy. Many modern-industry narratives of ¡°autonomous-AI¡± achieve only workflow complication and generative-output diversification and never touch axiomatic structural prerequisites.

Only simultaneous fulfillment of Telos-Internalization, Endogenous-Choice and Trial-and-Error Self-Rectification allows AI systems to transcend passive-generative-tool status and enter the category of autonomous systems. Autonomy is no rhetorical or marketing construct, but rigid, implementable, verifiable system-structure requirements.

The Telos-Choice-Trial-and-Error-Rectification tri-axiom framework shall act as underlying constitutional benchmark for evaluation and architectural design of all future autonomous-AI systems.
