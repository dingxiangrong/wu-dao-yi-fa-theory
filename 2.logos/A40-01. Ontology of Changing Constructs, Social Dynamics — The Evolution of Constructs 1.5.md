# Part One: Fundamental Definitions and Axiom System

## I. Three-Tier Right-and-Responsibility Clusters (Independent Observation Dimensions)

Central, middle, and bottom tiers are not classifications of constructs. They are observational perspectives independent of constructs and interfaces.
They are vertically stratified potential-energy clusters naturally formed in the operation of dominant meta-constructs, sorted by right-and-responsibility hierarchy, potential-energy position, and executive attributes. They serve as carriers for the operation of constructs rather than the ontology of constructs itself.

### 1.1 Central Cluster: Rule-Setting Tier

Holds the authority to formulate overarching top-level rules, locking paradigms, and potential-energy distribution systems.
Responsible for building the overall operational framework, boundary constraints, and underlying logic of the meta-construct; does not participate in micro potential-energy generation and on-site implementation.

### 1.2 Middle Cluster: Economic Executive Tier (Core Increment Tier)

A shared implementation carrier for both public and private constructs. It accepts top-level rules from the central cluster and fulfills cross-domain potential-energy circulation, resource allocation, and industrial deployment.
Public carriers: administrative executive departments, regulatory implementation agencies;
Social carriers: leading enterprises, industrial capital clusters.
The middle tier is the core hub for system potential-energy transit, amplification, and transmission.

### 1.3 Bottom Cluster: Source Tier of Potential Energy

Predominantly composed of pure social constructs, with low coupling to public constructs.
Outputs primordial cross-domain potential energy, productive nourishment, and foundational driving force. It holds no rule-setting power or top-level dominion, and forms the survival foundation of the entire system.

## II. Underlying Causal Axioms

**Derived Axiom 1: Three-Right Hierarchy Axiom**

- First Order: Prerogative of Violence (primordial, fallback, root power; determines the typing and evolution of meta-constructs)
- Second Order: Rule-Making Right (secondary, dependent on Prerogative of Violence)
- Third Order: Economic Dominion Right (secondary, dependent on rules and Prerogative of Violence)

Corollary: As the primordial fallback power, Prerogative of Violence directly determines the degree of order aggregation of a meta-construct and the upper bound of interface rigidity. It serves as the sole first-order criterion for meta-construct classification.

**Derived Axiom 2: Source of Power and Power Summation Axiom**
Human beings are the source of social agentic power. All transport capacity, potential energy, game power, and transformative force within a system originate from collective human activities. Power follows the causal rule of additive superposition: dispersed individual power is feeble; homogeneous individuals forming unified collective action produce superimposed and amplified power, which may develop into combined force capable of impacting the order of a meta-construct.
Corollary: Steady-state meta-constructs inherently seek to suppress large-scale aggregation at the bottom tier. Meta-constructs differ only in suppression methods and intensity, which may include association control, organizational regulation, and constraint on group behavior to decompose combined bottom-tier force and stabilize the existing order framework. High dominant meta-constructs tend toward association control and group behavioral constraint (backstopped by force locking); low dominant meta-constructs tend toward organizational regulation and rule restriction (constrained by institutional interfaces).

**Derived Axiom 3: Interface Transmission Causal Axiom**
All interactions complete potential-energy transfer via interfaces. Interfaces fall into two categories: flexible and rigid. Rigid interfaces are backstopped by upper-level rules. Interface performance is defined by two metrics: transmission efficiency and interface service life. Rigidity strength and gradient difference govern efficiency; buffer cushions govern steady-state service life.

**Derived Axiom 4: Nourishment Supply Causal Axiom**
The sustained potential-energy existence and steady operation of all sub-constructs rely entirely on nourishment supplied from the bottom tier. Imbalanced nourishment supply and long-term one-sided predation trigger weathering at the bottom tier and oxidation at the upper tier, eventually leading to system-wide structural decline.

**Derived Axiom 5: Potential-Energy Transfer Causal Axiom**
Within the rule framework of a meta-construct, transport capacity gaps between constructs drive continuous circulation and migration of potential energy. When top-level constraints of the meta-construct relax and latches weaken, the speed and scope of potential-energy transfer expand, rewriting the hierarchical status and discourse power of various constructs.

**Derived Axiom 6: Interdependence Causal Axiom (Universal Axiom)**
The prerequisite for multiple independent sub-constructs to converge into a higher-order meta-construct is mutual dependence and symbiotic gain. Once symbiotic benefits vanish and interdependence breaks, the composite meta-construct inevitably disintegrates. Ad-hoc demand groups, marital constructs, cooperative constructs, and national meta-constructs all obey this axiom.

> 
> **Coupling Force**: The binding force generated by symbiotic interdependence  (cf. ontological bonding force) between multiple sub-constructs, which maintains the higher-order meta-construct against dissociation. The magnitude of coupling force corresponds to the strength of interdependent benefits between sub-constructs; coupling force dynamically counterbalances the inherent repulsion force for growth carried by sub-constructs. When symbiotic interdependence disappears, coupling force diminishes continuously until it vanishes, and the meta-construct drifts toward disintegration.

**Derived Axiom 7: Threshold Dissolution Causal Axiom**
Stress generated by tug-of-war and game between interacting constructs with collaborative relationships carries a bearing threshold.
When stress stays below the threshold: parties game, concede, and collaborate steadily within existing rule interfaces;
When stress crosses the threshold: constructs completely break away from the original contractual rule system, terminate prior collaboration, and dissolution occurs.
Outcomes of dissolution are indeterminate and have no fixed correspondence with stress magnitude or construct scale. Minor disputes may trigger extreme dissolution, while large-scale games may only end with mild termination of cooperation.

**Derived Axiom 8: Virtual-Real Steadying Causal Axiom**
Direct rigid interaction between physical constructs generates high-intensity wear stress, which easily crosses thresholds and triggers dissolution;
Virtual constructs such as rituals, consensus, contracts, and public narratives buffer physical confrontation, internalize game stress, smooth interactive friction, and extend the steady-state cycle of meta-constructs.

# Part Two: Deduction on the Evolution of Meta-Constructs

## III. General Outline of Dual Evolution Dimensions for Dominant Meta-Constructs

The evolution of all dominant meta-constructs is governed jointly by two independent yet coupled underlying dimensions. The intensity dimension determines the **cyclical rise and fall** of the system; the identity dimension determines the **evolutionary stage** of the system. All institutional iterations and order replacements stem from linked changes across the two dimensions.

### 3.1 Dimension One: Cyclical Alternation of Dominant Intensity (Ontology of the Historical Cycle Law)

Core Law: Dominant intensity has no unidirectional progression and follows the cyclical law of **alternation between strength and weakness, and turnover of aggregating subjects**.
Alternation Mainline: High Dominance <--> Low Dominance

The underlying causal logic of this law is fixed: long-term operation of top-level public constructs inevitably leads to decay and dissipation of transport capacity. Middle-tier and local sub-constructs continuously accumulate transport capacity relying on potential-energy growth. As one fades and the other waxes, mature unified high-dominance patterns inevitably relax and fragment, transitioning into low-dominance multi-subject game states.

A low-dominance state is an unstable transient state. Without locking from external forces, it has three evolutionary trajectories: the top tier regains transport capacity to rein in sub-constructs and revert to high dominance; the original top tier loses transport capacity, and a new dominant subject emerges amid games to defeat other parties, complete aggregation of Prerogative of Violence, and iterate into a new high-dominance meta-construct; the original top tier loses transport capacity, yet no party can complete aggregation, all sides disavow the nominal identity of the original meta-construct, the framework disintegrates, and prolonged division and confrontation ensue. External intervention may lock this transitional state for a long time.

Alternation between strength and weakness, and turnover of aggregating subjects constitute the underlying cyclical law of dominant meta-constructs.

### 3.2 Dimension Two: Evolution of Identity Carriers

**Core Law**: The carrier to which Prerogative of Violence attaches exhibits a long-term unidirectional evolutionary trend, with partial local backsliding permitted. Evolution Mainline: Strong Identity → Weak Identity → No Identity

This evolution belongs to passive stress-response iteration of the system. Personalized and fixed-organizational private carriers carry an innate instinct for privatization and continuously accumulate structural stress. When stress crosses the threshold, the system must reconstruct institutions to strip Prerogative of Violence from private carriers and migrate it to more public carriers to relieve internal stress and sustain system steady state.

If public rule latches fail and clique privatization proliferates, higher-order identity forms may backslide to lower-order forms.
Formal Definition: A No-Identity Dominance features rule-centrism as its core trait. It is the highest-order dominance form where Prerogative of Violence is completely decoupled from personalities and organizational private ownership and anchored to public rules.

### 3.3 Coupling Mechanism of the Dual Dimensions

Intensity cycles and identity carrier forms are mutually independent yet mutually constraining:
Identity carriers determine the **decay rate and steady-state upper bound** of the system; dominant intensity determines the **current order intensity and game state** of the system.

Typical coupling effects: At equal high-dominance intensity, rule-centric no-identity systems enjoy far longer steady-state periods than strong-identity consanguineous systems; under identical identity carrier forms, high-dominance patterns possess stronger capacity to suppress privatization stress than low-dominance patterns.

Cross-positioning across the dual dimensions enables precise definition of the system health status, evolutionary stage, and residual steady-state cycle of any meta-construct.

## IV. Evolution of Strong-Identity Dominant Meta-Constructs

A strong-identity dominant meta-construct is a meta-construct with personality identity as the anchor of power. Prerogative of Violence, rule-making right, and economic dominion right are bound and inherited attached to specific personality identity carriers. The central cluster is borne by this identity system, forming a nested three-tier structure extending outward: central, middle, and bottom. Hereditary bloodline succession is one of the most typical forms of such carriers.

### 4.1 Formation of Strong-Identity Dominant Meta-Constructs

Numerous dominant sub-constructs under the same low-dominance meta-constructs sustain power (violence) games and potential-energy compression, converging rules, and finally yield two steady-state outcomes:

1. **Convergence into Unity**: Sub-constructs compete, annex, and integrate. One party prevails and restrains the rest, merging into a unified system to form a single central-led, three-tier nested strong-identity dominant meta-construct.
2. **Coexistence in Confrontation**: Games do not cease; only violent conflict temporarily halts. Parties enter a stalemate and confrontation. Games persist throughout, with conflict intensity fluctuating. Once conditions shift, fierce conflict may reignite at any time. Under coexistent confrontation, each party independently matures into separate dominant meta-constructs and disavows the original suzerain; this differs fundamentally from a low-dominance meta-construct: within a low-dominance meta-construct, all parties nominally recognize a sole central authority, even if the central authority lacks capacity for full control.

The foundation for meta-construct formation is power. The central cluster takes shape first relying on dominant power.
Prerogative of Violence, rule-making right, and economic dominion right are established sequentially centered on the central cluster; rules are generated from these rights, and public entities derive from the rules. Public entities function to implement these rules. Public constructs themselves carry growth attributes, and their potential-energy extraction and boundary expansion are constrained by regulatory functions and top-level check-and-balance frameworks.

In the course of continuous social operation, the middle and bottom tiers gradually differentiate. The division into central, middle, and bottom tiers serves as a macro perspective for observing internal stress within the meta-construct. The central cluster undertakes rule formulation, supervision of the middle tier, and holds the aspiration to underpin the bottom tier. Floating space is reserved between tiers; class solidification emerges through subsequent evolution, and whether solidification occurs depends on the transport capacity of the central cluster.

### 4.2 Four Groups of Tiered Stress

From this macro perspective, four sets of irreversible endogenous stress inherently exist within the system:

① **Central — Central Stress (Top-Level Rule Game Stress)**
Rule units inside the central cluster are interdependent, with transport capacity disparity and discourse weight as core variables. Long-standing gaps in potential energy and transport capacity continuously accumulate. Upon reaching critical points, primary and secondary potential energy reverse, and top-level fulcrums shift. This stress persists across the whole lifecycle; usurpation by imperial relatives, dynastic replacement by imperial clansmen, and ministers overawing the throne all represent its iterative forms.

② **Central — Middle Stress (Rule Constraint versus Expansion Stress of Middle-Tier Growth)**
This is the core tension among all stress groups. At the founding stage of a meta-construct, the central cluster has limited transport capacity. To drive sustained social growth, it must mobilize capable subjects with practical transport capacity and release their initiative. Driven by the central cluster, these subjects gradually grow into the middle tier.
The central and middle tiers maintain dynamic equilibrium: the central cluster relies on the middle tier to drive social growth, yet continuously constrains and suppresses the middle tier’s impulse for expansive growth. When the central cluster holds sufficient transport capacity, it can effectively govern this balance. Once the central cluster’s transport capacity decays and oversight weakens, the upward growth potential of the middle tier is released. Leveraging economic executive power, it spontaneously expands outward and accumulates potential energy, gradually forming independent potential-energy blocks beyond central control. The rise of powerful clans in the Eastern Han Dynasty and disorderly expansion of modern capital both follow this logic.

③ **Middle — Bottom Stress (One-Way Compression Stress)**
A one-way compression relationship with stark power disparity. In conjunction with the Source of Power and Power Summation Axiom, individual bottom-tier agents are feeble, yet once aggregated produce massive combined force. Therefore meta-constructs inherently tend to suppress aggregation of bottom-tier power, making unified combined force hard to form at the bottom. Middle-tier power is concentrated and readily produces overwhelming pressure. Without suppression from above, compression continues uninterrupted, and core productive factors of the bottom tier (land and various production resources) keep converging toward the middle tier. Productive factors are the foundation of potential energy; nourishment is only a secondary superficial component. Sustained compression gradually depletes the bottom tier and initiates weathering. This process is compression driven by power gaps, with potential energy spontaneously converging toward the middle tier.

④ **Central — Bottom Stress (Foundation Interdependence and Underpinning Stress)**
The bottom tier is the source foundation of the central cluster’s transport capacity, and the two share the most fundamental interdependence. When the central cluster has ample transport capacity, it carries an inherent willingness to underpin the bottom tier, hoping bottom-tier power remains abundant to consolidate its own foundation.
The central cluster constantly mediates contradictions between the middle and bottom tiers to sustain their balance — an extremely delicate relationship: growth requires activating middle-tier vitality, yet rapid growth of the middle tier inherently compresses the bottom tier. The transport capacity of the central cluster is essentially the capacity to allocate resources. Once the central cluster’s scheduling capacity weakens and suppression over the middle tier fails, the middle tier grows out of control and the balance breaks.
This stress group contains two chains:

1. Source Decay Chain: An abundant bottom tier stabilizes the central cluster’s transport capacity; sustained depletion and weathering of the bottom tier erode the central cluster’s transport capacity accordingly.
2. Resistance Breakthrough Chain: When balance collapses and the bottom tier suffers persistent compression, once the survival threshold is breached, dispersed bottom-tier power gains an aggregation tendency and forms resistance, striking back and weakening the central cluster.

### 4.3 Vicious Closed-Loop Cascade Reaction

Multiple triggers may initiate the vicious closed-loop cascade reaction; imbalance in any core stress group can launch the whole deteriorating chain:
It may start from depletion and weathering of the bottom tier, or from imbalance of Central — Middle stress and uncontrolled overgrowth of middle-tier forces. The Anshi Rebellion first ignited from the middle tier and then drove deterioration across the whole chain.
Closed-Loop Chain: Stress imbalance trigger → failure of original constraint latches → disorderly potential-energy transfer → amplification of stress in other tiers → deepening systemic imbalance, accompanied by oxidation in upper tiers.

### 4.4 Configuration Efficiency and Verification of Axiom Implementation

1. **Interface Transmission Causal Axiom**
Public constructs serve as the core interfaces for transmission of tiered potential energy, rules, and economic directives. Interface transmission is a universal axiom. Interface performance is determined by two dimensions: institutional coverage and inherent rigidity of institutions. Combinations of the two vary across societies and directly decide transmission efficiency. Broad coverage and moderate rigidity enable smooth transmission between nodes; solidified interface barriers, incomplete coverage, or inappropriate rigidity cause rule blockage and potential-energy siltation. Stress accumulates, and the system rapidly rigidifies and fails.
2. **Potential-Energy Transfer Causal Axiom**
Potential-energy transfer takes place between constructs. Constructs are distributed across tiers and carry floating and shifting attributes. Potential-energy transfer drives constructs to jump between tiers and reshape their own positions.
When the central cluster has ample transport capacity, it voluntarily builds transmission channels and can guide middle-tier potential energy downward to the bottom tier. At different development stages, the central cluster chooses different potential-energy flow directions. In early development, the central cluster tilts potential energy toward the middle tier to activate middle-tier initiative and drive overall social growth. When the bottom tier is continuously compressed and its foundation begins to weather, the central cluster attempts to adjust channels and guide potential energy downward to underpin the bottom tier and repair the foundation. Once the central cluster’s scheduling capacity fails and transmission channels break down, potential energy continuously flows one-way toward the middle tier and amplifies compression trends.
This axiom focuses on describing causal chains of potential-energy circulation. It belongs to a different observational dimension from the stress balance discussed previously: the stress dimension observes contradictory balance and tug-of-war between parties, while the potential-energy dimension observes delivery and conversion of potential energy between constructs.
3. **Nourishment Supply Causal Axiom**
The bottom tier continuously outputs nourishment and potential energy to sustain the middle and central tiers. Long-term one-sided predation of bottom-tier productive factors by public and private middle-tier clusters triggers bottom-tier weathering and upper-tier oxidation, ultimately leading to system-wide structural decline.
4. **Interdependence Causal Axiom**
Central, middle, and bottom tiers are mutually dependent foundations. Excessive growth of the middle tier and sustained decline of the bottom tier will break the interdependence equilibrium, forcing the central cluster to iterate rules and rebuild constraints, or move toward systemic reconstruction.
5. **Threshold Dissolution Causal Axiom**
Stress from the four tiered groups continuously couples, resonates, and accumulates layer by layer. If stress remains controllable, the system adjusts gradually; once stress crosses the bearing threshold of the meta-construct, three-tier steady state fails and the whole system deconstructs and reconstructs.
6. **Virtual-Real Steadying Causal Axiom**
Pure institutional and pure force-based rigid control of public constructs generates extremely high systemic wear stress, easily crossing thresholds and triggering dissolution. Virtual constructs such as rituals, consensus, and value narratives internalize conflicts, smooth friction, reduce confrontation intensity between public and private subjects, and extend the steady-state cycle of meta-constructs.
7. **Three-Right Hierarchy Axiom**
Prerogative of Violence is the primordial fallback power. Rule-making Right and Economic Dominion Right depend on Prerogative of Violence for existence. Implementation of all rules is ultimately backstopped by Prerogative of Violence. The state of Prerogative of Violence directly determines the degree of order aggregation of a meta-construct and the upper bound of interface rigidity.

### 4.5 Three Collapse Paradigms of Strong-Identity Dominant Meta-Constructs

#### 4.5.1 Single-Point Central Collapse

Stress is fully confined to **Central — Central top-level games**, while middle-tier growth and bottom-tier compression stay within controllable thresholds. Potential energy reverses inside the central cluster, top-level fulcrums shift, rule-making right falls into disorder, top-level latches slip, and the central cluster loses transport capacity. Only the top single point disintegrates; the middle and bottom tiers do not spiral out of control on a large scale. Typical case: Fall of the Western Han Dynasty.

#### 4.5.2 Cascade Transmission System-Wide Collapse

Stress imbalance triggers the vicious closed-loop cascade reaction, which propagates and amplifies along tiered chains, resonating bidirectionally top-down and bottom-up. The three-tier steady state collapses entirely. Typical case: System-wide collapse at the end of the Eastern Han Dynasty.

#### 4.5.3 Defeat by External Meta-Construct after Systemic Decay

A long-term closed-locked strong-identity dominant meta-construct suffers continuous oxidation and decay of the middle tier and weathering and depletion of the bottom tier. The potential-energy foundation of the whole meta-construct declines persistently and its bearing capacity falls. Before internal stress triggers system-wide dissolution, it is breached and disintegrated by force from an external heterogeneous meta-construct. Typical case: Late Qing Dynasty.

### 4.6 Special Stress under Low-Dominance Mode

When a strong-identity meta-construct transitions to low-dominance mode, the central cluster retains nominal identity legitimacy yet lacks force control capacity. Secondary sub-constructs hold independent transport capacity and Prerogative of Violence. Two unique stress groups emerge within the system:
① **Sub-Construct — Master Meta-Construct Game Stress**: Sub-constructs accumulate potential energy relying on self-owned resources and continuous growth, repeatedly testing the bottom line of the central cluster, forming the tension of separatist resistance and overshadowing of central authority. The Zhou royal house and vassal states in the Eastern Zhou Dynasty, and military governors in the late Tang Dynasty fall into this category.
② **Sub-Construct — Sub-Construct Annexation and Confrontation Stress**: Peer sub-constructs have uneven potential energy. Without strong central checks and balances, they annex and confront one another, continuously dissipating systemic potential energy. This is the core driver of chaotic separatist eras.

These two stress groups under low dominance may also trigger vicious closed loops and accelerate systemic collapse. **Low-dominance modes have poorer overall stability. Continuously expanding sub-constructs readily collide with one another and tear apart the whole meta-construct order.**

## V. Evolution of Weak-Identity Dominant Meta-Constructs

### 5.1 System Subjects

A weak-identity dominant meta-construct system comprises three core subjects: political power subjects, economic power subjects, and the bottom tier. Interactions and coupling between the three subjects generate the full set of endogenous forces of the system and constitute the underlying operational foundation of weak-identity dominant meta-constructs.

> 
> **Note**: For weak-identity dominant meta-constructs, identity construction and governance constitute fundamental work, used to counter its endogenous splitting stress and delay or regulate evolutionary trajectories.

### 5.2 Four Layers of Endogenous Stress

Based on interactive relationships between the three subjects, four fixed stress layers inherently sustain the stability of the system.

Layer One: Internal Stress of Political Power Subjects
Political power subjects are collective personality assemblages of multiple factions and interest blocks. Games and tug-of-war over discourse power, benefit distribution, and faction positions persist internally, forming inherent top-level forces.

Layer Two: Political Power Subject — Economic Power Subject Game Stress
Political power subjects govern systemic rules and public order; economic power subjects govern social resources and market dominion. The two constrain and game against each other, forming the core tension of the middle tier. Both types of subjects carry growth attributes and extend potential energy along their respective paths.

Layer Three: Economic Power Subject — Bottom Tier Compression Stress
Economic power subjects continuously aggregate social production resources and value and exert unidirectional potential-energy compression on the bottom tier. The bottom tier continuously outputs production value and bears foundational transport capacity of the system, staying in a long-term passively pressurized state. Cumulative compression induces weathering of the bottom tier.

Layer Four: Political Power Subject — Bottom Tier Interdependence Stress
The legitimacy of political power subjects and the survival foundation of the system rely on the bottom-tier foundation, which requires maintenance of basic steady state at the bottom tier. Long-term pressurization of the bottom tier conversely constrains the governance boundary of political subjects, forming a bidirectional interdependent force.

### 5.3 Commonalities with Strong-Identity Dominant Meta-Constructs

First, system formation follows homologous logic. Both form through power games and potential-energy convergence for order aggregation, with two steady-state forms: convergence into unity and coexistence in confrontation. Power generation logic obeys a unified underlying paradigm.

Second, cross-subject stress structures are largely corresponding and highly similar.
The game relationship between political and economic power subjects benchmarks the game relationship between the central and local tiers in strong-identity systems;
The compression relationship exerted by economic power subjects on the bottom tier benchmarks compression exerted by local tiers on the bottom tier in strong-identity systems;
The interdependent constraint relationship between political power subjects and the bottom tier benchmarks the interdependent relationship between the central cluster and the bottom tier in strong-identity systems.

### 5.4 Core Differences from Strong-Identity Dominant Meta-Constructs

First, the nature of top-level games differs fundamentally. A strong-identity central cluster anchors on a single personality; internal games only involve faction rotation, and the ontology of power remains stable with no risk of disintegration. A weak-identity system features collective personalities with no fixed anchor point. Unordered games across multiple blocks normalize, carrying an innate tendency toward internal splitting.

Second, boundary attributes of middle-tier subjects differ. Boundaries between the central cluster and local tiers in strong-identity systems are rigid and hierarchically fixed; in weak-identity systems, political and economic power interpenetrate and mutually alienate. Politics interferes with resource allocation while capital reversely influences rule formulation, with continuously flowing and dissolving boundaries.

Third, ultimate evolutionary trajectories are entirely different. Strong-identity systems feature strong closed-loop steady state and high solidification. Driven by the dual forces of internal splitting tendency and dissolving political-economic boundaries, weak-identity systems carry an innate evolutionary tendency to transition toward no-identity meta-construct modes. Dual stress resonance from internal splitting tendency and amplified middle-tier privatization stress may cross thresholds, forcing the system to reconstruct institutions and strip Prerogative of Violence from fixed organizations into public rules to complete transition to the no-identity form.

## VI. Evolution of No-Identity Dominant Meta-Constructs

### 6.1 Origin of System Formation and Primary-Secondary Disintegration Causality

No-identity dominant meta-constructs have no path for independent primordial formation. They represent the ultimate dominance form generated through passive stress-response iteration and transition from weak-identity dominant meta-constructs, and are not outcomes of active systemic advancement.

> 
> **Note**: Beyond this main transition line, there exists a special origin scenario: multiple independent dominant subjects jointly build a meta-construct through equal-status coalition and jointly obtain Prerogative of Violence. At founding, there is no single dominant subject or primary-subordinate affiliation, presenting no-identity traits from inception. Such scenarios also obey the complete subsequent stress and evolution rules for no-identity dominant meta-constructs. It should be distinguished that violent deconstruction such as the French bourgeois revolution belongs to a transitional sequence of repeated oscillation of identity carriers, rather than steady no-identity status from the outset.

The unified political power subject in the weak-identity stage is a **collectivized personality construct system**, which constitutes the primary internal cause and first core driver of system disintegration. Single-personality construct systems hold unified will and unified paths and possess strong latch stability; collective personalities inherently accommodate diverse wills, diverse perceptions, and diverse governance paths, carrying an innate tendency for disintegration where “incompatible doctrines preclude shared governance”.
Continuous growth penetration, interest entanglement, and resource binding of economic subjects constitute the secondary external cause and catalytic driver, which only accelerate and amplify the innate splitting tendency of collective personalities, rather than being the root of disintegration. Under the dual effect of the innate internal splitting tendency and catalytic external penetration, the unified, complete political power subject of the weak-identity stage, which can bear overall accountability for the meta-construct, fully disintegrates, and the iterative transition to the no-identity dominant meta-construct stage is finalized.

### 6.2 General Outline of the System

The first core iron law for the formation of a no-identity dominant meta-construct: **Within the whole domain, no subject or group bears responsibility for the overall survival, long-term steady state, and global development of the meta-construct**.
Strong-identity meta-constructs bear global accountability through single-personality subjects; weak-identity meta-constructs bear global accountability through unified collective personality subjects. By contrast, no-identity meta-constructs completely dissolve the global accountability subject. All social subjects serve only their own short-term local interests or those of local alliances. No force targets the long-term holistic interests of the meta-construct. This is the most fundamental institutional trait of meta-constructs at this stage.

### 6.3 Definition of System Subjects

No-identity meta-constructs inherit the three-layer underlying subject framework. Subject attributes, correlation relationships, and behavioral logics undergo essential reconstruction. All definitions strictly follow the preceding system without newly invented private concepts:

#### 6.3.1 Political Elite Blocs

Generated by the disintegration of the unified political power subject in the weak-identity stage. Multiple political blocs coexist independently without mutual subordination. This group holds no inherent exclusive statutory political power; power is acquired passively only through social games and electoral competition. Each faction inherently diverges in governance philosophy, development path, and value proposition, and engages in long-term contest.
Political elite blocs need resource support from corresponding economic subjects to participate in power competition, forming interest alliances with them. **Friction and divergence persist inside alliances and belong to secondary contradictions. Alliances may also disintegrate when contradictions intensify; yet such contradictions do not dominate the overall pattern trajectory of the meta-construct.**

#### 6.3.2 Economic Subjects

Under no-identity dominant meta-constructs, economic subjects form the largest group of constructs within the system and rise to become the top governing force of this order: the triumph of capital. Individual economic subjects operate independently and are not integrated as a single body, yet share two underlying consensuses:

1. The core objective of operation and growth is profit maximization;
2. Core demands are perpetuation of property rights and inviolability of rights and interests.

This consensus serves as the foundation for their alliance and shaping of underlying rules.

#### 6.3.3 Bottom-Tier Groups

The bottom tier is inherently dispersed and lacks unified aggregated force, which is fixed background of this system as specified by Axiom Two and will not be repeated in this chapter.

### 6.4 Endogenous Stress of the System

After capital ascends to dominance, the hierarchy of stress reshapes. Ordered from the inner upper circle to the foundational bottom tier, there are four layers:

#### 6.4.1 Internal Game Stress of Economic Subjects

Capital blocs compete, annex, and diverge over development paths. Although they share the underlying consensus of inviolable property rights and growth for profit, they continuously squeeze one another over markets, resources, and growth trajectories. The intensity of this stress relies heavily on external expansion space: sufficient outward release of contradictions in periods of abundant increment suppresses internal pressure; when external expansion hits ceilings and overlain by competitive pressure from other economies, stress rebounds inward and internal games intensify markedly. Multiple dimensions of divergence may emerge. The divide between virtual and real economy, emerging and traditional economy, is one pair of noteworthy observational directions. Whether they will evolve toward thorough disintegration remains an unvalidated evolutionary proposition.

#### 6.4.2 Game Stress between Different Political-Economic Alliances

This belongs to interest redistribution games within the upper elite circle. Different political-economic alliances contest for dominance over rule-making. Such games circulate mainly within the closed upper circle, with relatively limited direct transmission of impacts to the bottom tier.

#### 6.4.3 One-Way Compression Stress of Economic Subjects on the Bottom Tier

Deep, sustained, cumulative stress. Leveraging rule advantages gained via alliances, economic subjects continuously extract value from the bottom tier. Without a global accountability subject to impose checks and balances, compression carries the trait of continuous cumulative amplification. Long-term compression brings weathering of the bottom tier.

#### 6.4.4 Interdependence Stress of the Whole Political-Economic Alliance on the Bottom Tier

Foundation constraint of the whole system. The gain acquisition, property right persistence, and order stability of all discrete political-economic alliances rely on production value output and basic steady-state environment from the bottom tier. Bottom-tier supply is the prerequisite for realization of all upper-tier benefits.

### 6.5 Core Traits of No-Identity Dominant Meta-Constructs

1. Dissolution of accountability subjects: No subject bears global accountability; neither single personalities nor collective personalities hold overall responsibility.
2. Reshaped stress hierarchy: Games between economic subjects form the primary contradiction; contest between political-economic alliances belongs to upper redistribution games; the bottom tier bears long-term compression. Stress can be diverted outward or rebound inward. Differentiation between virtual/real and emerging/traditional economic directions is a potential observation point.
3. Fragmented and dependent political forces; economic forces sit at the top and serve as the anchor of order.

Summary: Upper-layer politics fragments, the bottom tier stays dispersed, and only the economic sphere carries unified growth will. This is the triumph of capital.

## VII. Non-Dominance Construct: Evolution of Interfaces with Coexisting Multiple Dominances

### 7.1 Origin of System Formation

A non-dominance construct is jointly built in parallel by multiple dominant constructs with independent potential energy. Each dominant construct retains its own Prerogative of Violence and potential energy. There exists no top-level subject overriding all members and aggregating Prerogative of Violence.

It belongs to an interface-type functional construct rather than a meta-construct. It gains social identity from consensus and is therefore classified as a construct; yet it carries no self-evolving order framework. Its essence is merely a game field between dominances. Permanent secretariats and secretaries-general are only agents of this interface. Agents themselves do not generate game contradictions, and all conflicts and tensions stem from participating dominant constructs. Such interfaces are built because direct confrontation between multiple dominances carries excessive costs. Parties jointly establish contractual negotiation interfaces to manage conflicts, exchange potential energy, and delineate bottom lines for coexistence.

### 7.2 Essential Character Traits of the System

1. **Extremely low rigidity of the construct as a whole: a soft interactive interface**
Lacking aggregated Prerogative of Violence from above as mandatory fallback support, all agreements are merely interfaces formed by multilateral contracts. Participating dominances may veto, suspend, or renounce agreements, and the interface itself has no means to impose sanctions.
2. **Rules emerge from games and shift dynamically with the potential-energy balance of dominances**
Rules lack independent stable attributes. They are jointly shaped through games between participating parties. When the power structure between dominances changes, original rules are renegotiated, revised, or even discarded. Rules attach to real-time potential energy rather than constant law.
3. **No independent inherent potential energy, no independent subject will**
It is not an entity unit with self-owned power. It is merely a platform for games and negotiation. There exists no will, objective, or accountability belonging to this functional construct itself. All actions and tradeoffs are attributable solely to participating dominant constructs.
4. **The sole source of binding force is multilateral consensus**
Consensus persists, and the interface retains interactive validity; consensus dissipates, and the binding effect of the interface vanishes simultaneously, with no underlying Prerogative of Violence sustaining order.

### 7.3 Definition of System Subjects

#### 7.3.1 Participating Dominant Constructs

Member dominances within the system, the sole subjects of this interface. Each participant is an internally consistent and self-sufficient dominant construct, independently holding Prerogative of Violence, inherent potential energy, interest objectives, and boundaries. They access this interface to reduce confrontation costs and will not cede their foundational potential energy. Potential-energy gaps exist between dominances, and each participating dominance retains growth attributes.

#### 7.3.2 Negotiation and Coexistence Interface (the Non-Dominance Construct Itself)

The non-dominance construct itself, the game field of dominances. It is a soft interactive interface with no self-owned potential energy, bearing only functions of communication, voting, mediation, and conclusion of multilateral agreements. All validity of the interface derives from voluntary acceptance of consensus by all parties. Positions such as Secretary-General are merely agents without independent decision-making power and do not independently generate contradictions.

### 7.4 Endogenous Stress of the System

The source of endogenous stress inside the system is game and growth tug-of-war between participating dominant constructs. This underlying stress differentiates into two action directions:

#### 7.4.1 Destructive Stress against Old Rules

After continuous growth and potential-energy shifts of parties, old rules no longer fit the new power structure. Dominances actively break, renounce, and negate old rules, generating tearing force against existing consensus.

#### 7.4.2 Generative Stress for New Rules

Through sustained tug-of-war and negotiation in games, parties keep pushing for new agreements favorable to themselves and continuously impact the original order framework.
The two forces continuously counterbalance and tug. Once stress crosses the rigidity upper bound of the construct, the interface cracks and the construct disintegrates. If generative stress successfully condenses into new consensus and new rules land, destructive stress is partially absorbed, the intensity of counterbalance recedes, and the interface persists. If new rules fail to match the power structure, destructive stress accumulates continuously until the interface disintegrates.
