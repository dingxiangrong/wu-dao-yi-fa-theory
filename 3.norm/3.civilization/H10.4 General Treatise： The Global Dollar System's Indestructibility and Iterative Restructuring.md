# H10.4 General Treatise: The Global Dollar System's Indestructibility and Iterative Restructuring

## I. Two Core Characteristics of the Present Era: Capital Globalization + Wealth Virtualization

Compared with the eras of World War I and World War II, the present world exhibits two fundamental, decisive underlying characteristics that serve as the starting point for understanding the modern global monetary order.

**First, capital globalization.** During World War I and II, so-called globalization was confined to trade in goods, resource extraction and the geographic spread of production. Capital itself retained a strong national character: ownership, funding bases and settlement centers were firmly bound to the colonial home country, and overseas investment was essentially domestic capital projected outward. The contemporary world, by contrast, has achieved genuine capital globalization. Capital is no longer confined within the borders of a single state. Asset holders are dispersed across sovereign nations worldwide, flowing across regions and sovereign boundaries. Capital no longer belongs to any single government; it is jointly held by diverse institutions, sovereign funds and family capital spread across the globe.

**Second, wealth virtualization.** In the World War era, the principal form of social wealth was physical assets: land, mines, factories, railways, gold and other tangible goods. Wealth was attached to geographic space, with physical entities serving as the primary carrier. Today, the total scale of global virtual financial assets far exceeds physical assets. U.S. Treasuries, equities, debt claims, derivatives and other book-entry entitlements constitute the dominant share of global wealth. Such virtual assets have no physical embodiment; their value exists only through credit and bookkeeping consensus.

Together, these two characteristics fundamentally reshape the underlying logic of how wealth persists, transfers and is destroyed. They constitute the fundamental precondition why the modern monetary system cannot replicate the wholesale reset that followed World War II.

## II. The Essential Distinction of Modern Wealth: Physical Assets Transfer Hands; Virtual Assets Annihilate

This is the first axiom for understanding the destiny of the modern monetary order.

**1. Physical assets: only change hands; never disappear.** Land, mines, factories and infrastructure are wealth with physical existence. Regardless of war, regime change or geopolitical restructuring, physical assets only change ownership. The physical object does not vanish, and its value foundation is not extinguished. Physical wealth endures forever; only its ownership shifts.

**2. Dollar-denominated virtual assets: when the anchor fails, wholesale annihilation.** The principal component of global wealth today is already ultra-scale virtual financial assets: U.S. Treasuries, U.S. equities, dollar-denominated debt claims, cross-border derivatives, and global book-entry entitlements.

The value of such assets rests on a value consensus collectively formed by global holders. Physical assets derive value from their physical existence; virtual assets have no physical carrier, and their value comes from a collective consensus reached by countless asset holders: a unified choice of the dollar as the unit of account, and unified recognition of U.S. Treasuries as the anchor of this value system. One may picture the entire dollar-denominated virtual wealth system as a giant ship. The dollar and U.S. Treasuries form the hull and keel. Every passenger aboard, regardless of nationality — all who hold dollar-denominated assets, whether central banks, institutional capital, family funds or multinational enterprises — rides this ship.

As long as the hull remains intact, the wealth on everyone's books remains valid: it can be priced, traded, pledged and reserved. But once the keel fractures and the ship sinks — that is, when the dollar's core anchor fails — the collective consensus collapses instantly. This is not a loss suffered by a single country. Every holder aboard, regardless of nationality, sees the virtual wealth measured on their ledgers annihilate directly. The digital records in the servers remain, but the value consensus vanishes: assets lose their unified unit of account, cannot be traded, cannot be redeemed, cannot be stored — equivalent to wealth reverting to zero. This is the true meaning of "anchor loss means annihilation." For precisely this reason, all holders of dollar-denominated wealth naturally share a common interest in preventing this great ship from sinking.

## III. The Core Guardians of the System: A Global Cross-Sovereign Capital Coalition

The force sustaining the current dollar system is not the U.S. government, but a comprehensive cross-sovereign capital group spanning the globe.

This group possesses three world-class top-tier characteristics:

- **Global distribution:** Encompassing U.S. asset managers, the European financial system, London markets, sovereign funds, central bank reserves, global family capital and multinational enterprises, it no longer belongs to any single nation.
- **Strongest global resource-allocation capacity:** It commands the largest liquidity pool and asset base in human history.
- **Highest level of intellectual capability:** The world's leading economists, model teams and macro-strategy circles serve as its long-term advisory apparatus, continuously deducing systemic risks and iteration pathways.

In ordinary times, this group competes, extracts from one another and diverges in interests. Yet at the system's existential red line it holds an absolutely unified underlying demand: the dollar's core anchor must never collapse, and the wholesale annihilation of global virtual assets must never occur. This constitutes a spontaneous, treaty-less, headquarter-less, cross-national super interest community.

## IV. Fatal Defect of the Old System: Supporting the Virtual with the Virtual; a Single Anchor Cannot Endure

The underlying architecture of the traditional dollar system is a classic virtual-upon-virtual structure.

The system's core master anchor is U.S. Treasuries — a promise of future U.S. government taxation, a purely virtual debt claim. Using "government virtual debt" to support "global currency credit" amounts to supporting the virtual with the virtual, the empty with the empty. Under this framework's deduction, such a single virtual-anchor model can operate in the short term but structurally tends toward overdraft over the long run and cannot endure indefinitely. The system possesses an internal impetus for self-rescue and iteration, seeking a new path.

From this derive the system's two major evolutionary directions:

1. **Introducing global physical resources as a new entity anchor.** To escape the fragility of a purely debt-based virtual anchor, the system tends toward continuously anchoring oil, minerals, grain and core strategic physical resources. Real global physical resource bases are used to hedge against the hollowing-out of virtual Treasury claims, constructing a dual-anchor structure of "virtual master anchor + physical resource auxiliary anchor." One potential evolutionary possibility also exists: U.S. Treasury gold reserves may in the future be transferred to the Federal Reserve as a supplement to the physical base, adding a systemic safety cushion.
2. **Global financial entities spontaneously gravitating toward the new system.** London and European financial institutions, sovereign capital and Asian cross-border capital possess strong incentives to spontaneously converge, connect and integrate into this multi-anchor new system. This is not political alliance, but the result driven by wealth preservation, asset protection and interest maximization.

## V. China's Necessary Pathway: Preserving an Offshore Connection Window

Global financial systems are deeply interconnected; no economy can be fully sealed off from this system. China's choice is to maintain a stable offshore connection window, retaining a channel to the global financial system and avoiding the risk of complete economic isolation. Hong Kong serves this offshore interface function.

## VI. Cross-Coalition Trading Platforms: The Continuing Contest Between Sovereign Capital and Global Financial Capital

In the process of systemic iteration, cross-coalition trading platforms may become the most important future infrastructure. From the perspective of global private financial capital, there is a natural aspiration to build a global trading platform independent of sovereign control, enabling free capital flow. Yet national sovereign capital and sovereign governance systems will not permit a financial platform completely free of sovereign constraints to take shape.

The two will enter a long-term contest, exhibiting an evolutionary character of progressive compromise. The cross-coalition platform that ultimately takes shape will not be dominated by any single force, but a coalition platform formed through repeated bargaining and mutual compromise between multiple sovereign actors and global financial capital. Each party continuously concedes and reaches stage-by-stage equilibrium on rules, access, risk control and capital-flow permissions, adapting to the cross-entity trading needs of global assets under a multi-anchor system.

## VII. The Greatest Systemic Difficulty and Core Contradiction: Largely Locked Inside the United States (Core Conclusion)

The future destiny of the entire global monetary system, its iteration pace, and whether it can achieve a smooth soft landing — all pressures, all risks, all knife-edge balances — are, to a large extent, concentrated within the United States itself.

This is the most central, most critical ultimate conclusion of the entire treatise:

1. The system's current master anchor remains U.S. Treasuries — not global resources, not gold, not other assets.
2. At the present stage, the absolute first priority is to keep Treasuries from collapsing and preserve U.S. government credit from default.
3. Should Treasuries suddenly crash while the new multi-anchor system is not yet fully built, and global capital has no substitute base and no time to switch, it would most likely trigger the collective annihilation of vast quantities of global virtual assets.
4. Therefore, the sole core task of the Federal Reserve and the global capital coalition at present is to maintain knife-edge balance within the United States: stabilizing debt, stabilizing fiscal conditions, stabilizing market expectations, avoiding a hard landing, and winning a long transition window.

The future evolutionary logic is clear:

- First, stabilize the old anchor inside the United States — no collapse, no rupture;
- Then gradually add physical resource anchors and the potential gold-reserve transfer as a physical base;
- Finally, slowly reduce the weight of the single Treasury anchor, achieving the dollar system's gradual migration from being bound to U.S. national fiscal policy toward serving as a neutral monetary carrier for cross-sovereign global capital.

All global shifts are, to a large extent, gated on conditions within the United States. When the U.S. is stable, the global system is stable; when the U.S. is disordered, trillions of human virtual assets face immediate annihilation risk.
