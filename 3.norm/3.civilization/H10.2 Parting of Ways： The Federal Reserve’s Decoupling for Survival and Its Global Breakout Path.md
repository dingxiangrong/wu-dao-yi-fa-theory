# Chapter 2｜Parting of Ways: The Federal Reserve’s Decoupling for Survival and Its Global Breakout Path V1.0

**Core Thesis**: The global circulation of U.S. Treasury securities has fractured, driving the two entities toward separation. The Federal Reserve’s survival logic undergoes a fundamental shift: its objective is to break free from the fate of being hostage to the U.S. federal government, avoid being dragged down by risks stemming from U.S. fiscal conditions, and evolve into a global monetary institution with an independent foundation for survival.

A critical clarification is required upfront: this is not “de-Americanization.” The U.S. dollar will not, nor does it intend to, exit the U.S. sovereign framework, and the Federal Reserve will not overthrow the American system. More precisely, it seeks to avoid being held hostage by the U.S. government. It diversifies its credit foundation from a single bet on U.S. Treasury securities toward multiple anchors, thereby reclaiming an independent basis for its continued existence.

## 1. Complete Divergence of the Two Entities’ Incentives

After separation, the underlying objectives of the U.S. government and the Federal Reserve become fully opposed and incompatible.

The core priority of the U.S. government: continued credit overdraw and expanded debt issuance. It expects the Federal Reserve to provide ongoing backstops and sustain fiscal operations via monetary instruments.

The core priority of the Federal Reserve: ending unlimited fiscal accommodation for the U.S. government, escaping fiscal capture, preserving the foundation of global dollar credit it upholds, and securing its own independent right to survive.

The prior mutually symbiotic relationship has terminated. The two entities now pursue distinct survival goals and map out separate paths forward.

## 2. The Federal Reserve’s Fundamental Survival Motive: Rejection of Bondage to a Single Actor

The Federal Reserve stewards a massive global dollar credit system and stands as a major monetary policy actor. Yet the old system carries a fatal flaw: dollar credit is anchored to U.S. Treasury bonds, tying the Fed’s destiny closely to U.S. sovereignty. The U.S. government may perpetuate a Ponzi-style debt expansion and overdraw long-term credit. The resulting risks of credit dilution and asset devaluation ultimately fall entirely on the Federal Reserve and the dollar system. For the Fed, dependence on this single dominant power means placing its very survival in the hands of another party. Should the U.S. fiscal system collapse, the Federal Reserve and the global dollar credit system will collapse alongside it. This constitutes the fundamental motive driving the Fed’s pursuit of a breakout strategy.

It must be emphasized that diversification is its pathway toward independence, not merely a short-term self-preservation tactic. Diversification is not intended simply to delay collapse. Its purpose is to gradually build an independent survival foundation — ultimately ceasing to function merely as a component within the U.S. government apparatus, and transforming into a self-sustaining, self-generating entity.

## 3. The Federal Reserve’s Strategic Objectives and Breakout Pathway

The Fed’s top-level strategic objective is clear: break free from single-sovereign bondage and advance toward globalization and operational autonomy.

The core breakout pathway is to identify new third-party anchors and build an offshore dollar circulation decoupled from U.S. domestic fiscal conditions. No longer anchoring dollar credit exclusively to U.S. Treasury securities, it expands the vehicles for dollar issuance, circulation and anchoring across the globe, delivering monetary services for global capital. It leverages diversified global assets as credit backing to hedge risks originating from U.S. domestic fiscal pressures, freeing itself from unilateral capture by the U.S. government, and developing capacity for autonomous decision-making and capital flows.

This transition cannot be achieved in one step. The Federal Reserve remains deeply coupled with the U.S. government: U.S. Treasuries dominate its balance sheet; dollar credit is locked to U.S. sovereign debt; the U.S. Congress retains authority to amend the Federal Reserve Act and adjust the Fed’s institutional framework, with power to alter its operating rules. Therefore it can only follow an evolutionary route: incremental progress. It first builds offshore liquidity channels, then gradually identifies new anchors, and finally differentiates the two sets of operating rules. Every step tests institutional boundaries, and each advance risks being rolled back.

## 4. Implemented Practical Measures

This breakout framework is not purely conceptual; several instruments have already been deployed. The **FIMA Repo Facility**, activated temporarily during the 2020 pandemic and converted to a permanent standing facility in 2021, represents a pivotal measure. This tool allows foreign central banks to pledge their holdings of U.S. Treasury securities to the Federal Reserve in exchange for immediate dollar liquidity.

The significance of this tool extends far beyond simple crisis response. It converts U.S. Treasuries held by overseas central banks into collateral readily convertible to dollars, establishing an independent liquidity channel within offshore markets. Foreign central banks no longer need to sell Treasuries on open markets to obtain dollars, which would risk triggering a Treasury market crash. This step establishes a liquidity fulcrum within the offshore dollar system separate from the U.S. domestic fiscal market, serving as a practical experiment for constructing third-party anchors.

Beyond this facility, the Federal Reserve maintains an extensive network of central bank swap lines across major global economies. This swap system channels dollar liquidity directly to foreign central banks, bypassing the U.S. domestic fiscal market, sustaining the foundational circulation of global dollars and continuously reinforcing the independent offshore dollar cycle.

## 5. Likely Future Trends and Directions

Extrapolating along this decoupling and breakout trajectory, a series of subsequent developments will emerge.

First, asset diversification. The Federal Reserve will gradually restructure its asset holdings, reducing exclusive reliance on U.S. Treasury securities and adding high-quality diversified global assets as credit underpinnings, expanding the range of assets anchoring the dollar.

Second, expansion of offshore dollar mechanisms. Continue developing liquidity tools for global central banks and scale overseas dollar circulation, so that offshore dollar operations depend less on U.S. domestic fiscal conditions.

Third, rule divergence. Gradually distinguish “U.S. domestic fiscal dollars” from “global offshore dollars.” Liquidity rules for these two domains will slowly diverge; offshore dollar operations will serve global market priorities rather than domestic U.S. fiscal demands.

Fourth, expanded cross-border monetary collaboration. Sustain monetary coordination with central banks worldwide, dispersing the foundation of dollar credit away from exclusive U.S. sovereign backing toward global assets and multilateral cooperation.

## Chapter Conclusion (Hook)

The Federal Reserve’s strategic direction of decoupling for survival is clear, with selected tools already operational. Yet deep entanglement with the U.S. government renders this breakout inherently fraught: it seeks independence while the umbilical cord remains uncut; it pursues diversification while its primary holdings remain Treasuries; it aspires to act as a global central bank, yet the U.S. Congress imposes constraints. Every step forward meets partial pushback.

Thus the critical question is not “whether it will succeed,” but the evolutionary process itself — how new structures grow incrementally within the old system, and how each exploratory advance avoids being reversed.
