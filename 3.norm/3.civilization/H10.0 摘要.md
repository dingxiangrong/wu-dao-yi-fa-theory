**中文摘要**

本系列包含五篇 Working Paper，构成完整研究框架，探讨现行全球货币体系的内生演化路径。核心前置公理：全球经过百年沉淀形成体量庞大、跨主权分布的美元计价金融资本存量，具备不可湮灭的底层属性；无论主权国家实力格局如何更迭，全球存量金融资本不会接受整体财富清零。因此，美元体系的一切变革，均是为这套天量资本寻找可永续存续的承载载体，而当前唯一现实可行的载体仍是美元全球化体系。

第一篇《协议婚姻：美国政府与美联储的分家》以 "协议婚姻" 隐喻，刻画美国政府与美联储两套相互独立主体的契约合作关系。1913 年美联储成立即为独立主体；1951 年《财政部 — 美联储协议》解除战时强制捆绑。1951 至 2008 年，美债出海、美元回流构成双向共赢的蜜月期；2008 年金融危机后，海外承接机制断链，美债由全球分担转为国内单方兜底，演变为政府向美联储单向索取，债务体系呈现庞氏化特征，双方诉求彻底对冲，博弈陷入死局。其背后是现代世界最深层的结构性矛盾 —— 主权资本与全球金融资本的长期对抗。

第二篇《分道扬镳：美联储脱绑求生与全球突围路径》指出，环流断裂后双主体走向分家。美联储的突围并非 "去美国化"，而是不被美国政府绑架，把信用底座从单一押注美债分散为多元锚。多元化是其走向独立的路径而非自保手段。文章梳理 FIMA 海外回购便利、全球央行货币互换网络等已落地工具，推演资产多元化、离岸美元扩容、规则分化与跨境货币协作等未来方向。

第三篇《美联储体系的核心矛盾与全球化演化双路径》剖析美联储 "想脱绑却不敢破局、想减负却只能慢行" 的底层两难，提出两条并行演化路径：一是资源锚构建，通过欠发达经济体美元化，形成 "美债锚 + 资源锚" 的多元信用底座，美债降权而非替代；二是搭建多边跨域金融流通平台，作为美元成为全球货币的必要条件与身份标识。两路径成型后，美元将完成从 "美国的货币" 到 "全球货币" 的形态跃迁。整套体系强调演化而非革命，在旧体系之上生长新结构，旧锚降权、新锚抬升，渐进完成转型。

第四篇《全球美元体系不可颠覆与迭代重构总论》提出当代世界两大底层特征：资本全球化与财富虚拟化。对比两次世界大战时期，现代资本跨主权弥散分布，虚拟金融资产规模远超实体资产，成为全球财富主要载体。文章区分两类财富本质：实体资产只会变更权属，不会灭失；美元计价虚拟资产一旦信用锚崩塌，价值将整体湮灭。维系现有体系的力量是跨主权全球资本联盟，其共同底线是阻止美元锚崩溃。当前依托美债构建的单锚架构存在内生脆弱性，体系将演化形成 “美债主锚 + 实物资源辅锚” 双锚结构，全球各金融主体将逐步向新范式靠拢。中国将依托香港保留离岸对接窗口。主权资本与全球私人金融资本长期博弈，催生跨联盟交易平台。整套转型的核心瓶颈在美国本土，稳住美债是首要任务，以此换取漫长转型窗口期，推动美元体系从绑定美国财政信用，渐进转变为服务跨主权全球资本的中性载体。

第五篇《美联储的 "离婚"：一场全球金融体系的变局》以夫妻关系隐喻推演全球货币体系的迭代路径。文章把美国政府与美联储的关系刻画为一场 "离婚"：2008 年金融危机前是和睦夫妻、国债顺利出海；危机后政府欠债累累，转向单向索取，双方从嫌隙走向分家，最终 "离婚不离家"，美联储逐步购置独立空间。文章给出美联储 "租房 — 看房 — 交定金 — 装修 — 搬入" 五步渐进行动策略，以及美国政府的复婚、阻挠、施压三步应对，与各主要经济体 "欢迎合作、有利有节、差异化强硬" 的参与策略。最终演化为 "全球多元共治" 稳态：美联储不再隶属于美国政府，转型为面向全球的金融公共服务平台，同时保留美国清算优先、重大事项缓冲、议事席位倾斜等特殊权益，并附三道硬性约束 —— 不再能无限透支、不能单方面破坏体系、不再是唯一庄家。文中拟人化的美联储，代表百万亿级全球存量金融资产背后的庞大利益群体。本文属结构性思辨模型推演，非史实纪实。

**English Abstract**


This five-part working-paper series develops a complete analytical framework for the endogenous evolution of the existing global monetary system. Its foundational axiom is that a century of accumulation has produced an immense, cross-sovereign stock of dollar-denominated financial capital—an asset base that is, in essence, un-erasable. Regardless of how the hierarchy of sovereign powers shifts, global financial capital will never accept the wholesale annihilation of its accumulated wealth. Consequently, every transformation of the dollar system serves one purpose: finding a durable, self-sustaining carrier for this vast capital. Within the present institutional framework, the only realistic such carrier remains the globalization of the dollar itself.

**Part I, "The Marital Contract: The Separation of the U.S. Government and the Federal Reserve,"** frames the relationship between the two independent actors as a "marital contract." The Federal Reserve has been an autonomous body since its founding in 1913, and the 1951 Treasury–Federal Reserve Accord ended the wartime coercion. From 1951 to 2008, the outward issuance of U.S. Treasuries and the return flow of dollars formed a mutually beneficial honeymoon period. After the 2008 financial crisis, however, the overseas absorption mechanism broke down; U.S. debt shifted from global distribution to domestic bailout, devolving into one-way extraction by the government from the Fed. As the debt system displayed Ponzi-like characteristics, the two parties' demands became diametrically opposed and their game reached an impasse. Beneath this lies the deepest structural contradiction of the modern world: the long-term struggle between sovereign capital and global financial capital.

**Part II, "The Divergence: The Federal Reserve's Decoupling and Global Breakout Paths,"** argues that after the breakdown of the monetary cycle, the two parties move toward separation. The Fed's breakout is not "de-Americanization" but rather liberation from capture by the U.S. government—diversifying its credit foundation from a single bet on Treasuries to multiple anchors. Diversification is a path to independence, not mere self-preservation. The paper reviews already-implemented tools such as the FIMA Repo Facility and the network of global central-bank swap lines, then projects future directions: asset diversification, expansion of offshore dollar mechanisms, divergence of rules, and cross-border monetary collaboration.

**Part III, "The Core Contradiction of the Fed System and Two Paths of Globalizing Evolution,"** dissects the Fed's underlying dilemma—"wanting to decouple but daring not to break the grid, wanting to deleverage but forced to move slowly." It proposes two parallel evolutionary paths. The first is the construction of a resource anchor: through dollarization in less-developed economies, a diversified credit foundation of "Treasury anchor + resource anchor" emerges, with the Treasury anchor down-weighted rather than replaced. The second is the establishment of a multilateral, cross-border financial circulation platform—the necessary condition and identity marker for the dollar to become a truly global currency. Once both paths materialize, the dollar completes its metamorphosis from "America's currency" into a "global currency." Throughout, the series stresses evolution rather than revolution: new structures growing on the old system, old anchors gradually down-weighted and new anchors gradually elevated, in a gradual transformation.

**Part IV, "The Indestructible Global Dollar System and Its Iterative Reconstruction: A General Thesis"**, identifies two foundational features of the contemporary era: capital globalization and wealth virtualization. In contrast to the World War era, modern capital flows across sovereign borders, while virtual financial assets have surpassed physical assets and become the main carrier of global wealth. It draws a critical distinction: physical assets merely change ownership, whereas dollar-denominated virtual assets will be annihilated once their credit anchor fails. The system is sustained by a cross-sovereign global capital coalition, united by the shared imperative to avoid the collapse of the dollar anchor. Built on U.S. Treasury bonds, the existing single-anchor framework suffers from inherent fragility. The system will evolve toward a dual-anchor structure by incorporating physical commodity resources as auxiliary anchors, and major global financial participants will converge into this new paradigm. China will maintain offshore connectivity through Hong Kong. A cross-alliance trading platform will take shape through repeated bargaining between sovereign capital and global private financial capital. The decisive bottleneck of the whole transition lies inside the United States. Stabilizing U.S. Treasury bonds is the top priority to secure a lengthy transition window, enabling the dollar system to gradually shift from reliance on U.S. fiscal credit into a neutral carrier serving cross-sovereign global capital.

**Part V, "The Divorce of the Federal Reserve: A Transformation of the Global Financial System"**, uses the metaphor of a marriage to model the iterative path of the global monetary system. It frames the relationship between the U.S. government and the Federal Reserve as a divorce: before the 2008 financial crisis they were a harmonious couple with Treasuries smoothly issued abroad; after the crisis, the government, burdened with debt, turned to one-way extraction from the Fed, and the two moved from estrangement to separation, eventually entering a "divorce without leaving home" as the Fed gradually secures its own independent space. The paper outlines the Fed's five-step gradual strategy of "renting, viewing houses, paying deposits, renovating, and moving in," alongside the U.S. government's responses of seeking reconciliation, obstructing, and pressuring, and the participation strategy of other major economies of welcoming cooperation, handling obstruction with restraint, and responding to pressure with differentiated firmness. The evolution culminates in a stable pattern of "global pluralistic co-governance": the Fed no longer belongs to the U.S. government and transforms into a global public financial service platform, while retaining special privileges for the United States—clearing priority, veto-buffer on major matters, and tilted representation—subject to three hard constraints: no more unlimited overdraft, no unilateral destruction of the system, and no longer the sole dealer. The personified Fed represents the vast interest group behind trillions of global financial assets. This paper is a structural speculative-model exercise rather than a historical account.
