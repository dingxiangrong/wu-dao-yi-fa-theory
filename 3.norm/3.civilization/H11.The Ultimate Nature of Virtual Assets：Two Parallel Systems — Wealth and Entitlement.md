# The Ultimate Nature of Virtual Assets: Two Parallel Systems — Wealth and Entitlement


##  Abstract

Conventional economic theories of wealth distribution focus merely on adjusting the stock of objective wealth and struggle to explain the underlying operating mechanism of the modern virtual financial system and the logic of global top-tier games. This paper puts forward a core proposition: virtual assets are not equivalent to wealth; they are standardized bookkeeping vouchers for social entitlement. It establishes an analytical framework of **Wealth–Entitlement–Authority**. Wealth denotes objectively existing resources; entitlement means the authority to allocate resources under established rule systems; authority describes the resource boundary, valid scope and rule domain attached to entitlement.

Human society holds two tiers of value pursuits. Ordinary individuals tend to redeem authority within rule boundaries in exchange for tangible wealth. In contrast, top-tier entities such as sovereign entities and international capital pursue magnitude expansion and domain expansion of authority. From this emerges the fundamental law of the virtualization era: **Wealth persists; entitlement can perish**. Wealth survives through physical substance and cannot vanish instantly merely due to rule failure, while entitlement relies on consensus and rules and may be revoked once boundaries are breached.

The paper further argues that the fundamental contradiction behind global geopolitical conflicts and financial contests today is not confrontation between nation-states, but the game between two supreme entitlement systems: sovereign entities and international financial capital entities. The future evolutionary path of the Federal Reserve arises from the contest between these two systems. This analytical paradigm connects the micro logic of virtual assets with the macro global financial order, offering a fundamental analytical tool for understanding civilizational patterns in the age of asset virtualization.





The primary, secondary and tertiary distributions defined by conventional economics serve only one dimension from start to finish: **wealth**. This framework addresses the ownership, circulation and rebalancing of objective resources, representing superficial adjustments to existing value within human society.

The advent of the modern virtual era has brought about an entirely independent, higher-dimensional new operating system: **the system of entitlement**.

Most people struggle to understand finance, wealth disparity and global games not because they confuse the terms wealth and entitlement, but due to **a widespread misconception about the nature of virtual assets**. People readily equate the book value of virtual assets directly with wealth.

**[Original Core Insight] Virtual assets do not equal wealth; they are standardized bookkeeping vouchers for social entitlement.**

Virtual assets themselves have no objective physical existence. Paper, code and digits are merely carriers. They record not how much consumable, usable objective resource an entity holds, but the **authority to allocate resources** held by the entity within a system of social rules.

Every category of virtual asset is bound to a dedicated set of rules that define its valid scope. Any resource-allocation authority embedded in virtual instruments only operates within the bounds of its corresponding rules. Once outside those boundaries, the authority lapses immediately.

Wealth and entitlement constitute two fully independent logics of civilizational operation. Wealth is a fixed outcome; entitlement is the authority to allocate resources. Wealth exists objectively; entitlement is conferred by a system. Wealth as such cannot vanish instantly merely because rules collapse, while entitlement may become invalid and reset to zero in an instant.

Under the logic of wealth: whoever owns real estate, materials or valuable resources possesses usable, consumable value. No matter how society fluctuates, objective wealth does not vanish out of thin air; it only changes ownership or depreciates, possessing strong stability.

Under the logic of entitlement: holding book balances of virtual assets does not mean possessing wealth. It only means the system has granted the holder **authority to allocate resources**.

Human society has two tiers with distinct value pursuits. Ordinary people holding modest virtual authority stay anchored in the dimension of wealth. They tend to redeem their authority within existing rule boundaries and convert it into tangible wealth.

By contrast, games played by top-tier entities, leading institutions and state-level actors transcend the wealth dimension. Their core pursuit **is not to redeem wealth, but to expand authority**. Authority expansion falls into two core dimensions: First, **magnitude expansion**: continuously scaling the volume of one’s resource-allocation authority within the existing rule framework. Second, **domain expansion**: broadening the scope of rules underpinning the authority, moving from internal corporate rules and regional market rules to national and global rules.

The underlying logic of corporate development fully follows this law of authority expansion. The allocation authority of a private company is confined to internal corporate rule boundaries. After listing, its authority connects to public capital market rules, vastly expanding its effective scope. When a company introduces foreign capital or pursues overseas listing, in essence it embeds its resource-allocation authority into the global financial rule system. All financing, equity expansion and capitalization operations are not primarily for cashing out wealth, but for continuously expanding top-tier resource-allocation authority.

This explains why the US dollar system has become the world’s most powerful financial system. The full suite of virtual assets denominated in the US dollar rests on globally universal rules with the broadest coverage and deepest penetration today. The larger the rule domain, the stronger and more stable the resource-allocation authority sustained by the system.

This is the core underlying reason why the US dollar system cannot collapse abruptly: **Wealth may be consumed, transferred or destroyed, but a globally spanning rule system of entitlement carries strong structural resilience.** It may only undergo gradual erosion of consensus and contraction of authority; instantaneous total collapse is not possible.

This yields the defining feature unique to the virtualization era: **Wealth persists; entitlement can perish.**

Objectively existing wealth resources cannot be instantly erased solely by rule failure. Even amid destruction, confiscation or devaluation, the underlying resources remain in existence.

Yet authority to allocate resources exists entirely on the basis of systemic consensus and rules, with no objective substance or physical carrier. **Any entity, regardless of the scale of its authority, will suffer impairment, downgrade or total revocation of such authority once it violates systemic rules or allocates resources beyond its bounds.**

This law applies to entities at all tiers. Individuals crossing boundaries illegally lose their financial and social resource-allocation authority. Enterprises in breach see their operational, valuation and financing authority granted by capital markets collapse rapidly. States that violate rules face freezing, sanctions and invalidation of top-tier authority for cross-border trade, capital flows and global settlement.

This exclusive trait is absent from the wealth system, and constitutes the harshest underlying rule governing modern high-level world order.

Traditional tertiary distribution can only adjust the stock of already produced, objective wealth. It achieves superficial fine-tuning and cannot shake society’s top-tier order.

What truly defines social strata, patterns and the balance of contests is the **entitlement bookkeeping system** built by virtual assets. It does not distribute wealth; it vests authority. It does not adjust stock; it defines rule boundaries.

Grasping this logic allows one to pierce the superficial chaos of modern finance, class solidification and global rivalry.

Stock market swings, valuation volatility, currency fluctuation and interest rate adjustments never merely represent rises or falls in wealth. **Their essence lies in social rules dynamically rewriting every entity’s resource-allocation authority and its valid scope.**

A rise in book value means the system expands allocation authority and broadens the rule domain. A drop in book value means the system contracts allocation authority and narrows the rule domain. Boundary violation triggers direct revocation of allocation authority by the system.

Wealth determines an individual’s present quality of subsistence. Entitlement determines an entity’s ultimate capacity to mobilize resources, cross tiers and engage in top-tier contests in the future.

Civilization has undergone a fundamental paradigm shift across history: In the pre-virtual era, humanity fought for **ownership of objective wealth**. In the virtualization era, humanity contends for **bookkeeping power over social rules and the power to vest top-tier authority**.

Wealth is the mortal body of civilization; entitlement is its central nervous core. The mortal body may decay; the core governs all outcomes.

The **fundamental contradiction at the core of today's world** behind global geopolitical conflicts, financial games and institutional confrontations is essentially a contest between two top entitlement systems: sovereign entities and international financial capital entities.

## Appendix: Definitions of Concepts and Relationships Used in This Paper

### 1. Three Core Concepts

**Wealth**: All categories of objectively existing resources within society. It includes material wealth and intangible valuable wealth (such as intellectual property). Its core attribute is direct usability and consumption. Wealth itself cannot vanish instantly due to rule failure; it may only gradually depreciate, be damaged or transferred.

**Entitlement**: Under established rules, **the authority to allocate resources**. It has no objective substance and exists entirely based on systemic consensus and rules.

**Authority**: The quantitative and qualitative description of the resource boundary, valid scope and rule domain attached to a given entitlement.

### 2. Logical Relationships Between the Three

1. Wealth is objective stock resources, the object to be allocated.
2. Entitlement is the authority to allocate objective resources, the active capacity to exercise disposition.
3. Authority defines and constrains the valid boundary of an entitlement.
4. Entitlement is never equivalent to wealth: ordinary people redeem entitlement for wealth; top-tier entities expand the domain of entitlement rather than accumulating wealth.
5. Wealth carries physical stability; entitlement is vulnerable to rule changes: wealth persists, entitlement can be instantly nullified.
