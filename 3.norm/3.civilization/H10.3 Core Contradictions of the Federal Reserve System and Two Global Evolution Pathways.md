# Chapter 3｜Core Contradictions of the Federal Reserve System and Two Global Evolution Pathways V1.1

## 1. The Federal Reserve’s Core Underlying Contradiction (Systemic Condition)

The most binding constraint on the current dollar and Federal Reserve system is the single-anchor structure: assets are heavily bound to U.S. Treasury securities. Within the current system, the core underlying anchor of the Fed's balance sheet rests overwhelmingly on U.S. Treasuries, forming the central foundation of dollar credit. This single-anchor structure creates an insurmountable innate contradiction and constitutes the fundamental reason the Federal Reserve has long "danced on the blade's edge."

Viewed as a systemic condition, the core problem is severe asset-structure imbalance and highly concentrated risk: dollar credit is deeply dependent on the Treasury market. This yields an extreme dilemma:

**The necessity of reducing exposure:** For the Federal Reserve to complete systemic evolution, escape capture by U.S. domestic fiscal policy, and achieve genuine dollar globalization, its core pathway is to lower the share of Treasuries within its asset structure and cast off the shackles of a single Treasury anchor.

**Operational constraint:** The Treasury system functions like the blood-pressure lifeline of the dollar system and absolutely cannot be adjusted rapidly or abruptly. Like a hypertension patient who cannot force a drastic pressure reduction at once, adjustment can only be extremely gradual and finely calibrated. Any aggressive reduction or balance-sheet shrinking would directly impact the Treasury market and trigger a collapse in Treasury prices. A Treasury default would be tantamount to a default on the dollar's underlying assets, precipitating the complete collapse of the global dollar credit system.

Beyond this, the Federal Reserve faces the risk of passive external dilution: even if the proportion of Treasuries on its own balance sheet remains stable, continued rapid expansion of the total U.S. public-debt stock will persistently dilute the market value and credit content of existing Treasury holdings, continuously eroding the dollar's underlying credit. Therefore, the Fed must not only fine-tune its own asset structure, but also urgently acquire means to control the disorderly expansion of U.S. debt and curb the unlimited swelling of domestic liabilities.

This contradiction — "seeking decoupling yet daring not to break the game, seeking to reduce exposure yet only able to move slowly" — constitutes the most central and fundamental survival predicament of the Federal Reserve and the dollar system today.

## 2. Two Potential Future Evolution Pathways for the Dollar System

To fully escape the fatal dependence on a single Treasury anchor and break through the shackles of domestic fiscal policy, two parallel global restructuring pathways exist in logic. These represent extrapolation of future possibilities and are not yet judged as already underway.

### Pathway One: Resource-Anchor Construction — Treasury Anchor + Resource Anchor, Diversifying the Credit Base; Treasury Downgraded Rather Than Replaced

The Treasury anchor plus resource anchor is essentially a diversification of the original Treasury anchor, not its replacement. U.S. Treasuries remain within the dollar credit asset portfolio but cease to be the sole foundation. Just as a wealthy investor would not concentrate all capital in the equity of a single company — not because that company is judged destined for bankruptcy, but because betting everything on one target carries extreme concentration risk — the Federal Reserve's strategic objective is to diversify its credit asset portfolio: the weight of Treasuries gradually declines, the weight of physical resource anchors rises, and a single anchor transitions into multiple anchors.

This pathway follows a clear causal chain: driving dollar regional expansion is the means, anchoring physical resources is the derivative result, and stabilizing global currency status is the ultimate goal.

**Starting point:** Many third-world underdeveloped economies suffer weak domestic currency stability, frequent inflation, and fragile native credit foundations.

**Objective condition:** These nations' local currencies have inherent stability shortcomings, creating autonomous incentives to accept the dollar as a settlement currency. A typical reference case is Argentina, which has witnessed large-scale grassroots dollarization, with much commerce shifting to dollar settlement, while the government has not abolished the peso or surrendered legal tender issuance rights. Such economies hold potential space for official or grassroots dollarization. This model is replicable: if similar economies continue to accept the dollar, they will progressively expand the geographic coverage of dollar circulation.

**Intermediate result:** As more resource-based underdeveloped economies move toward dollarization, the dollar becomes the common currency within these nations. Transactions involving local minerals, agricultural products and other basic physical resources will naturally be denominated and settled in dollars, thereby anchoring the dollar to the world's underlying physical resources.

**Ultimate goal:** Once the dollar is bound to global basic physical resources, large economies — whether the EU or China — cannot bypass the dollar whenever they need to procure these underlying resources. In this way, the dollar's global currency status is consolidated.

Although individual resource nations have limited scale and cannot replace Treasury volume in the short term, the core value of this pathway lies in its replicable, standardized institutional model. If similar economies continue to choose the dollar as their settlement vehicle, they can persistently expand the dollar's circulation territory, indirectly forging a binding between the dollar and global foundational resources.

### Pathway Two: Building a Multilateral, Independent Cross-Domain Financial Circulation Platform — a Necessary Condition and Identity Marker for the Dollar Becoming a Global Currency

The resource anchor addresses diversification of the dollar credit base. Yet credit-base diversification alone is insufficient; it must be paired with an independent circulation framework.

If a currency's circulation skeleton remains controlled by the U.S. government, it remains an American currency, not a global one. Only when the financial platform bearing monetary circulation achieves cross-sovereign independence, no longer subordinate to a single state, can the dollar complete its metamorphosis from an American currency to a global currency. Platform independence is the formal marker of this evolution. Absent this vehicle, even a fully diversified credit base leaves the system dependent on U.S. sovereignty.

The traditional dollar financial system is attached to the U.S. federal government and Wall Street, tightly constrained by U.S. sovereignty, and struggles to achieve genuine globalization with independent operation. Accordingly, this evolutionary direction envisions building a multilateral, coalition-style, cross-sovereign global financial asset circulation mechanism with greater operational autonomy.

The key points of this pathway's conception:

- **Weakening single-sovereign binding:** Encourage Wall Street and core global trading venues to secure greater operational autonomy within their home jurisdictions' regulatory frameworks, reducing the impact of single-sovereign policy intervention.
- **Multilateral collaborative construction:** Not limited to U.S. soil; coordinate with global core financial trading venues such as the London Stock Exchange and Hong Kong Stock Exchange, establishing cross-national, cross-regional channels for asset circulation, pricing and settlement.
- **Cross-domain asset circulation:** Establish a unified cross-domain circulation mechanism supporting the cross-border flow, trading and pricing of various global financial virtual assets.

Major global exchanges themselves harbor aspirations for greater operational autonomy and reduced sovereign-government intervention, and these aspirations align with this evolutionary direction.

## 3. Deduced Outcomes Once the Two Pathways Take Shape

If both the resource anchor (the Treasury-plus-resource diversified base) and the multilateral independent cross-domain financial circulation platform (the independent circulation skeleton) come to fruition, the dollar will complete its systemic iteration.

**Complete logical chain:** The single Treasury anchor carries fatal concentration risk → Introduce a resource anchor, constructing a Treasury-plus-resource dual-anchor system, with Treasury weight reduced but not eliminated, achieving credit-base diversification → Adjusting the credit base alone is insufficient for globalization; the circulation skeleton must be released from single-sovereign control, establishing a cross-sovereign independent financial circulation mechanism → With both conditions satisfied, the dollar completes its metamorphosis from an American currency to a globalized currency.

This evolution does not overthrow the old system and rebuild from zero as a revolution. Rather, it grows new structures upon the existing system: the weight of old components gradually declines, the weight of new components steadily rises, and a gradual morphological transition is completed.

At that point, the dollar will fully discard the historical limitation of a single Treasury anchor, simultaneously supported by both a physical-commodity credit base and a global financial circulation vehicle. The dollar will no longer be simply "America's currency," but will evolve into a globalized currency bound to the world's physical resources and interlinked across global financial markets.
