# H10.1 The Federal Reserve’s Marital Contract: The Survival and Evolution of Global Financial Capital

This series does not discuss systemic overthrow or revolution. It focuses on the endogenous evolution of the existing global monetary system: new structures grow upon the original institutional foundation, old anchors gradually lose primacy while new anchors gain weight, ultimately completing the morphological transition of monetary attributes.

## Foundational Axioms｜Immutable Bedrock of the System

After a century of accumulation, the global system has built an enormous, cross-sovereign stock of dollar-denominated financial capital spread across institutions and family offices worldwide. This forms a giant wealth base solidified within the modern human economic system, bearing intrinsic properties: it cannot be annihilated, wholly written off, or completely overturned.

Regardless of future shifts in the relative power of sovereign states or whether the United States declines in its global standing, holders of global stock financial capital will never accept the total annihilation of their wealth. This is the fundamental constraint governing the evolution of all monetary regimes and the logical origin of every structural shift.

Within humanity’s existing institutional framework, the realistic vehicle capable of hosting, sustaining and operating this massive pool of global capital remains the dollar-based global system. Accordingly, all transformations within the dollar system and all breakthroughs and evolutionary moves by the Federal Reserve are not intended to rescue U.S. sovereign power. Instead, they seek a long-term safe, independent and sustainable survival base for the indestructible giant pool of global financial capital.

**Core Premise**: 

One of the fundamental structural contradictions of the modern world lies in the game between sovereign capital and financial capital. This conflict is not inherent; it has gradually taken shape alongside the globalization of the dollar. The Federal Reserve represents global financial capital rather than acting as a fiscal agent for the U.S. government. The U.S. government is a temporary rotating sovereign-capital actor, while the global financial capital carried by the Fed constitutes a long-lived core force. Their objectives and time horizons are inherently conflicting.

> **Note**: The terms “sovereign capital” and “financial capital” deployed in this text are analytical constructs defined within this framework, not standard economic terminology. They characterize two distinct actors operating under separate monetary standards.

# Chapter 1｜The Marital Contract: Separation between the U.S. Government and the Federal Reserve

Core Thesis: The U.S. government and the Federal Reserve have been two independent entities since the Fed’s founding. Whether they maintain cooperative relations hinges on the equilibrium shaped by both interests and contradictions.

## 1. Underlying Logic: The Marital Contract

In most sovereign states, the central bank is subordinate to the national government and functions as a lower administrative agency. The entity that holds coercive power also controls fiscal authority; power and finance are unified.

The U.S. architecture is fundamentally different. Established in 1913, the Federal Reserve was created as an independent entity from its inception.

The U.S. government is the sovereign power holder, governing force: it controls territory, coercion, legislation and taxation. Sustaining its internal order and competing for global resources and influence requires continuous massive funding.

The Federal Reserve originally represented domestic U.S. capital and governs credit: it stewards the dollar credit system, with monetary credit as its core asset. This group relies on the U.S. government’s coercive power to safeguard asset security while pursuing free capital flows and asset preservation.

There is no hierarchical subordination between them. The U.S. government holds no statutory authority to directly command the Federal Reserve. As an analogy: two fully separate parties enter a cooperative contract. They may sustain collaboration or drift toward separation; cooperation persists only when both sides capture benefits.

During World War II, wartime financing forced tight alignment. The Federal Reserve implemented a yield peg on Treasury securities.

The **1951 Treasury‑Federal Reserve Accord** lifted mandatory wartime binding provisions. The two entities were no longer forced together by wartime contracts and reverted to a loose cooperative relationship, each retaining its own foundational interests and core priorities. Cooperation persists at a dynamic equilibrium of overlapping interests and contradictions.

As the dollar de facto became the global settlement currency, the Federal Reserve’s stance and interest boundaries also globalized. Its constituency is no longer merely wealthy groups inside the United States, but capital worldwide that holds and depends on dollar credit. Therefore, its core positioning must be clarified: in its modern form, the Federal Reserve represents global financial capital, not merely domestic U.S. capital. This is the source of its full institutional strength.

## 2. Honeymoon Phase: Treasury Outflows and Dollar Recirculation, Mutual Gain (1951–2008)

Around the end of WWII, the Bretton Woods system cemented the dollar’s status as the global settlement currency. Alongside U.S. economic recovery, dollars flowed outward continuously, forming a complete cycle: Treasury securities exported globally and dollars recirculating back.

Mechanism: The U.S. government exports dollars via trade deficits. Overseas economies accumulate dollar reserves and continuously purchase U.S. Treasury bonds. Treasury securities flow globally, and dollars return to U.S. soil as overseas parties buy Treasuries.

Overseas sovereign funds became stable long-term primary buyers in the Treasury market. Large volumes of newly issued Treasury debt were absorbed by foreign central banks, keeping bond yields persistently low. This created mutual gains: the U.S. government’s borrowing burden was shared externally, enabling low-cost sustained financing to maintain its global power. The global financial capital represented by the Federal Reserve preserved dollar credit via this cycle, with assets continuously preserved and expanded.

During this period, cooperative gains outweighed frictional costs, and the two parties operated in a mutually beneficial honeymoon phase.

## 3. Emerging Contradictions: The Breakdown of Treasury Export and the Shift Toward Unilateral Fiscal Claims on the Fed

The 2008 global financial crisis marks the turning point. When the crisis erupted, U.S. financial risks spilled outward, and nations worldwide absorbed the shock passively. This episode made clear to global actors that U.S. Treasuries and dollar assets were not risk-free safe assets, carrying profound underlying vulnerabilities.

Shifting risk perceptions, paired with structural global economic change and accelerating U.S. Treasury issuance, structurally transformed the cross-border circulation mechanism. Treasury securities no longer exported smoothly. Global markets became unwilling to absorb incremental debt on a sustained basis, breaking the cycle. The burden of Treasuries shifted from global sharing to domestic absorption.

Empirical evidence: The overseas share of U.S. public debt declined thereafter. Foreign holdings accounted for roughly 50% of U.S. public debt in 2012 and fell to 31% by the end of 2025. The absolute stock of foreign-held Treasuries did not collapse. Instead, the total stock of U.S. public debt expanded far faster than foreign accumulation. New Treasury issuance can no longer be exported on a large scale. The debt burden once shared globally can no longer be shifted outward and must be digested domestically.

The U.S. government transitioned from a mutually beneficial partnership with the Fed to a pattern of unilateral fiscal claims. The old two-way win-win cycle collapsed, planting the root of structural conflict.

## 4. Escalating Contradictions: Sovereign Debt Enters Ponzi-like Structure; The Fed Safeguards Trillion-Dollar Global Assets

The equilibrium sustaining their coexistence collapsed, and contradictions intensified.

U.S. public debt expanded exponentially. In cash-flow terms, the debt system exhibits ponzi-like characteristics: new debt must be continuously issued to service interest payments on outstanding obligations, and tax revenue is insufficient to repay the massive debt stock. Interest expenses keep swelling and crowd out fiscal space.

Behind the Federal Reserve lies a multi-trillion-dollar pool of global financial capital anchored in dollar credit. Its own balance sheet holds only several trillion dollars, yet it guards the credit foundation of the entire dollar system. Unconditional accommodation of unlimited fiscal easing would dilute dollar credit continuously, triggering sharp devaluation of the massive assets held by global financial capital and exposing the foundation of wealth to annihilation.

Their priorities become fully opposed. The U.S. government requires liquidity, debt backstops and rate cuts to reduce interest burdens. The Federal Reserve must defend dollar credit and preserve asset value and cannot permit its erosion.

The government keeps applying pressure on the Fed. The Fed is willing to provide short-term emergency liquidity backstops but refuses permanent unlimited fiscal monetization. This is not an isolated dispute, but a long-running structural conflict, with continuous bargaining and tug-of-war.

## 5. Manifestation of the Contradiction in Practice

The two entities retain independent status, and the U.S. government lacks statutory authority to command the Federal Reserve. Meanwhile their core interests keep diverging. This combination of institutional independence and conflicting interests has repeatedly erupted into public confrontation.

The most prominent case is the standoff between Trump and Fed Chair Powell. Powell was nominated by Trump during his first presidential term. Trump repeatedly called publicly, demanding Powell cut interest rates to suppress Treasury yields and ease federal fiscal pressure. Powell resisted White House pressure, slowed rate cuts and did not follow Trump’s preferred policy path. Trump repeatedly criticized Powell publicly, even drafted dismissal letters and threatened to remove Powell and demand his resignation. Under the **Federal Reserve Act**, the president may only remove Fed governors for statutory misconduct. Mere disagreement over monetary policy is not grounds for removal; the president cannot dismiss the chair arbitrarily over policy differences.

When Powell’s chairmanship term expired, Trump nominated Kevin Warsh to succeed him, expecting a nominee sympathetic to rate cuts. Yet after taking office, Warsh also declined to blindly follow White House fiscal demands. Facing inflationary pressure, he instead raised interest rates, prioritizing inflation suppression. This confirms that even Fed chairs personally nominated by the president will exercise independent decision-making under the Fed’s institutional mandate and will not take orders from the White House.

All Fed liquidity operations and Treasury market interventions are reactive emergency responses. It can deliver short-term backstops, but rejects permanent unlimited fiscal monetization. This is the clearest practical manifestation of contradictions within this cooperative framework.

## Chapter Conclusion (Hook)

This cooperative relationship has always been collaboration between two independent actors. The honeymoon was built on the global Treasury-dollar circulation for mutual benefit. After 2008, the absorption mechanism fractured, relations shifted toward unilateral extraction, contradictions intensified, and bargaining reached an impasse.

**Global financial capital cannot permit the annihilation of dollar credit. It must find a path forward**.
