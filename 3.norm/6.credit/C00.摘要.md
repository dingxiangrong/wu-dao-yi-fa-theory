This deposit contains seven working papers of the C0 Credit Series. This series constructs a theoretical system for credit analysis built around the core concept of default cost. It adopts a dual perspective: default cost forms, viewed from the borrower’s ontology, and enforcement instruments, viewed from the lending institution.

C00 serves as the overview abstract for the whole series.
C01 establishes the foundational qualitative framework. It defines credit as the temporary transfer of capital usage rights. Traditional risk evaluation based on repayment capacity and moral willingness has inherent defects. The core of credit risk control is to build enforceable default penalties. Five categories of default cost are identified: loss of existing material wealth, restriction of future economic capacity, erosion of social identity, deprivation of access to social infrastructure, and criminal liberty deprivation. It analyzes composite penalty combinations for mainstream credit products, exposes paradoxes in the credit reporting system, and reconstructs the underlying logic of industry competition and financial regulation.
C02 Version 2.0 formalizes the qualitative theory into a four-dimensional mathematical quantitative model. It distinguishes MaximumCost, the upper bound of deterrence, and ExpectedCost, the probability-weighted expected loss. The four loss dimensions include static asset value loss C1, time-integrated economic flux loss C2, one-off social reputation depreciation C3, and social access right impairment C4. Two calculation paths are provided: analytic white-box modeling and sample-fitted grey-box modeling. It further deduces product design rules, dimension trade-offs, asymmetric information weighting and endogenous deterrence mechanisms for practical credit engineering.

C03 analyzes typical credit business patterns, risk levers, advantages and weaknesses.
C04 outlines the overall architecture of commercial banking business.
C05 explores fundamental structural differences between Chinese and American credit systems, grounded in the Circle Model theory.
C06 discusses personal lending in a short standalone paper.

The central axiom running through the whole series: risk prediction models can estimate risks, but only enforceable default costs secure contractual performance. This system closes the loop from qualitative theory, mathematical quantification, to industrial business architecture and cross-system comparison. These are speculative working papers and do not constitute financial or regulatory advice.
